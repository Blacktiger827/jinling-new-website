# 2205 Duplex Stainless Steel Datasheet

2205 is the chloride step-up route when 316L no longer feels safe enough and higher yield can help the design.

Jinling check: use this when chloride margin, strength, or wall-thickness reduction may justify duplex. Verify heat chemistry, heat treatment, weld control, and document language before release.

## Use This When

Use 2205 when chloride comes with stress, pressure, or weight reduction, and the fabrication route is controlled enough to respect duplex welding discipline.

Brine, desalination, and coastal splash-zone work are good review candidates. Warm seawater, severe brine, and high-temperature service need a harder stop: check whether 2205 is already too light before the PO moves.

## Core Data

- Standard IDs: UNS S32205 preferred; EN 1.4462.
- Chemistry band: Cr 22.0-23.0%, Ni 4.5-6.5%, Mo 3.0-3.5%, N 0.14-0.20%.
- Mechanical baseline: tensile minimum 620 MPa; yield minimum 450 MPa.
- Corrosion cue: PREN is usually around 35, confirmed from heat chemistry when margin matters.

## Stop Before Release

- 2205 gives strength and chloride margin at the same time.
- The weld window is narrower than austenitic grades.
- If the service is hot rather than chloride-driven, duplex is usually the wrong answer.

## Finish and Form Note

2B, No.4, Hairline, and pickled/passivated routes are common. Coil, sheet, plate, bar, welded pipe, and seamless pipe are standard forms.

## Verify Before PO

1. Name `2205` and the preferred UNS designation.
2. Confirm chloride, pressure, or sour-service intent.
3. Confirm ferrite, hardness, and inspection needs.
4. Move beyond 2205 if the service is hot seawater or severe brine.
