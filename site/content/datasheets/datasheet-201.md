# 201 Stainless Steel Datasheet

201 is an indoor decorative cost grade. It makes sense only when the part stays dry and the buyer accepts the narrower corrosion margin.

Jinling check: use this when the project is dry, indoor, decorative, and cost-sensitive. Verify exposure and receiving checks before PO; if wet cleaning appears, move the conversation to 304.

## Use This When

Use 201 for dry indoor trim, panels, and decorative tube when cost matters and corrosion duty stays light.

Do not use it as a cheaper 304 when repeated wet cleaning, salt contact, food contact, coastal air, or outdoor service is part of the real job. Move to 304 or 316L before price is discussed.

## Core Data

- Standard IDs: UNS S20100; EN 1.4372.
- Chemistry band: Cr 16.0-18.0%, Ni 3.5-5.5%, Mn 5.5-7.5%, N up to 0.25%.
- Mechanical baseline: tensile minimum 655 MPa; yield minimum 260 MPa.
- Corrosion cue: PREN is typically around 16, so exposure review matters more than a small price gap.

## Stop Before Release

- 201 is not a cheaper 304 for wet service.
- It works best when appearance matters more than corrosion reserve.
- If the receiving team expects 304 behavior, the spec is already drifting.

## Finish and Form Note

2B, BA, No.4, Hairline, and AFP are common finish routes. Coil, sheet, slit strip, and decorative tube are the normal supply forms.

## Verify Before PO

1. Name `201 / S20100` if real 201 is required.
2. State that the service is dry and indoor.
3. Confirm the finish and whether the part is decorative or structural.
4. If the project sounds like 304, move up instead of trying to force 201.
