# 304 / 304L Stainless Steel Datasheet

304 / 304L is the baseline pair for mild stainless service. Use 304L when welding matters; move up when chloride becomes part of the real job.

Jinling check: use this when the team needs an everyday stainless baseline. Verify welding, chloride absence, product form, finish, film, and MTC wording before release.

## Use This When

Use 304 / 304L as the mild-service baseline for indoor fabrication, general equipment, and everyday stainless parts.

Prefer 304L or dual-certified stock when welded parts stay in service. If coastal air, chloride cleaners, or brine appear in the brief, move to 316L before the PO is locked.

## Core Data

- Standard IDs: 304 is UNS S30400 / EN 1.4301; 304L is UNS S30403 / EN 1.4307.
- Carbon line: 304 allows up to 0.08% C; 304L tightens that to 0.030% for welded service.
- Chemistry band: Cr 18.0-20.0%; Ni 8.0-11.0% for 304 and 8.0-12.0% for 304L.
- Mechanical baseline: 304 minimums are 515 MPa tensile and 205 MPa yield; 304L minimums are 485 MPa tensile and 170 MPa yield.
- Corrosion cue: PREN is typically around 19, so chloride exposure is the usual upgrade trigger.

## Stop Before Release

- 304 is the everyday route, not the chloride route.
- 304L is the safer choice for welded assemblies left in service.
- If the job sounds like coastal or saline service, 316L is the right next conversation.

## Finish and Form Note

2B, BA, No.4, Hairline, mirror, and AFP are common finish routes. Coil, sheet, strip, bar, and tube are standard supply forms.

## Verify Before PO

1. Name `304` or `304L` explicitly.
2. Confirm whether the stock is dual-certified.
3. Confirm finish, edge condition, and film if the part will be handled visibly.
4. If chloride is already part of the brief, do not leave the grade at 304.
