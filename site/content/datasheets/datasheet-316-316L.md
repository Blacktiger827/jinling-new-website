# 316 / 316L Stainless Steel Datasheet

316 / 316L is the first chloride-upgrade pair. Use 316L when the part is welded and the service still needs molybdenum-bearing margin.

Jinling check: use this when 304 looks too light for chloride. Verify welded-service wording, heat chemistry, cleaning media, and whether the job is already asking for duplex.

## Use This When

Use 316 / 316L when 304 looks too light for coastal, hygienic, or mild chemical service, but temperature and crevice risk are still controlled.

Prefer 316L for welded process parts. If the brief says warm seawater, severe crevice, or chloride plus stress, start the 2205 review early instead of stretching 316L.

## Core Data

- Standard IDs: 316 is UNS S31600 / EN 1.4401; 316L is UNS S31603 / EN 1.4404.
- Carbon line: 316 allows up to 0.08% C; 316L tightens that to 0.030% for welded service.
- Chemistry band: Cr 16.0-18.0%, Ni 10.0-14.0%, Mo 2.00-3.00%.
- Mechanical baseline: 316 minimums are 515 MPa tensile and 205 MPa yield; 316L minimums are 485 MPa tensile and 170 MPa yield.
- Corrosion cue: PREN is typically around 25, a real step above 304 but not a seawater guarantee.

## Stop Before Release

- 316 / 316L adds real chloride margin over 304 / 304L.
- 316L is the better welded-service default.
- When chloride, temperature, and stress all rise together, move to duplex instead of stretching 316L.

## Finish and Form Note

2B, BA, No.4, Hairline, mirror, and AFP are common finish routes. Coil, sheet, bar, welded tube, and seamless pipe are standard forms.

## Verify Before PO

1. Name `316` or `316L` explicitly.
2. Confirm whether heat chemistry or PREN review is required.
3. Confirm whether the job is welded, sanitary, or coastal.
4. If the service sounds like seawater or severe crevice, start the duplex conversation early.
