## Choose the Right Inquiry Path

### Quote and lead time

Use this when the spec is mostly ready. Include product form, grade, finish, size, quantity, destination port, and any tolerance, packing, or document requirement.

### Grade and finish review

Use this when the application is known but the material decision is not. Share the service environment, cleaning method, chemical exposure, standard requirement, and what you are trying to avoid.

### Product-form route

Use this when the order could still move between coil, sheet, strip, tube, pipe, or bar, or when downstream cutting, bending, welding, machining, edge, burr, or flatness will affect the quote.

### Documents and samples

Use this when the buying decision depends on MTC format, PMI, COO, sample finish, third-party inspection, or a qualification deadline.

### Shipment and release

Use this when timing, container loading, packing, film, pallet design, port choice, or export documents could decide whether the order arrives cleanly.

### Claim or technical review

Use this for scratches, stains, wrong finish expectation, tolerance dispute, document mismatch, or post-arrival inspection questions. Photos under normal and raking light help much more than a long description.

## Factory Visit

Factory visits are useful when the scope is clear. Tell us whether the visit is for finish capability, slit-width program, packing discipline, QC workflow, document review, or a live RFQ. If the visit is tied to an audit, share the checklist before travel dates are fixed.

## Ports and Shipment Context

Most South China orders move through Guangzhou or Shenzhen routes when that is the cleaner logistics choice. Other Chinese ports may be used when product origin, mill route, destination, or freight conditions make them more practical.

Container loading and packing review should be discussed before the order is released, not after the shipment is ready.
