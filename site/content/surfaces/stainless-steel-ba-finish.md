# BA Finish Stainless Steel Sheet & Coil

**BA is the bright mill finish that sits between 2B and mirror. It makes sense when the project wants a cleaner, brighter face or a better polishing base, but not a full mirror promise.**

This is a foundation finish page, so the useful question is not "what is BA?" but "should BA stay the delivered face, or is it only the base for AFP or later mirror polishing?"

## Use BA for Brightness, Not Reflection

BA is useful when the project needs a bright mill face, a cleaner appliance route, or a better polishing base than 2B. It is not the same promise as 8K mirror.

**Bright but not full mirror** is the natural BA lane. Substrate, handling, and lot consistency decide whether the face stays clean enough.

**High-touch final faces** should bring AFP into the conversation before the film and coating route are fixed.

**True reflection work** should move into 8K mirror release language instead of asking BA to carry a job it was not built to carry.

## Decide Whether BA Is the Final Face or the Process Base

BA can play two honest roles:

- a bright final face for indoor panels, appliance skins, and selected decorative work
- a smoother base for later [AFP](/surfaces/stainless-steel-afp-finish) or [8K mirror](/surfaces/stainless-steel-8k-mirror-finish) processing

Problems start when those two roles get mixed. A PO that expects true mirror from ordinary BA invites disagreement later.

## Keep BA as BA When the Job Wants a Bright Mill Finish

BA is strongest when the brief is:

- appliance-front brightness without a full mirror promise
- indoor reflective trim and decorative sheet
- a cleaner, brighter face than [2B](/surfaces/stainless-steel-2b-finish)
- a lower-process-cost bright finish where scratches and handling are still manageable

BA is usually weaker when the finish must hide abuse, show a clear directional grain, or behave like a polished mirror.

## When BA Should Move to AFP or Mirror

- Move from `BA` to `AFP` when the face stays bright but becomes a high-touch panel.
- Move from `BA` to `8K mirror` when reflected image clarity, higher gloss, or a stricter visual release standard matter.
- Step back from `BA` to 2B when the surface is mainly process feedstock and brightness is not part of the value.

## Compare BA With 2B, No.4, and 8K

BA is easiest to place when the neighbouring finishes are named plainly.

- **2B** is the smooth working surface for fabrication, process equipment, and feedstock. It is not a bright decorative face.
- **BA** is bright mill reflectivity for appliance panels, indoor decorative sheet, and a cleaner base for later polishing.
- **No.4** moves away from brightness into a satin directional grain, better for practical decorative work and kitchens.
- **8K mirror** is true specular reflection, with tighter release, handling, and cost than BA should be asked to carry.

## Match BA to Grade and Use

The grade choice should follow the same logic as the service environment.

- **304 BA** is the standard bright indoor route for appliance panels, decorative sheet, and later polishing.
- **316L BA** is the cleaner-heavy or humid upgrade when the environment asks for more corrosion reserve.
- **430 BA** is often the commercially useful value route for appliance fronts and dry indoor trim.
- **201 BA** belongs only in budget indoor decorative interiors where expectations and exposure stay modest.

For many appliance programs, `430 BA` is the commercially useful combination: bright face, controlled indoor service, and lower alloy cost than 304.

## What to Lock Before Ordering

- Say whether BA is the delivered visible finish or the process base for AFP or mirror.
- Write `bright mill finish` if that is the intent, not `mirror`.
- Match grade to environment before discussing appearance alone.
- Name the film and handling plan if BA is the final visible face.
- For polishing or coating programs, confirm the downstream process in the order.
