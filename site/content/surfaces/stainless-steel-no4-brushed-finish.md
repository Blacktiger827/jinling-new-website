# No.4 Brushed Finish Stainless Steel Sheet & Coil

**No.4 is the stainless finish most people have actually touched — kitchen equipment, elevator doors, appliance skins, public counters.** It is a directional satin grain: practical, forgiving, and calm under daily handling. It is also the finish we have been refining the longest, which is where our Super Finish variant comes in.

If the job needs a clean brushed field that survives handling and matches lot-to-lot on restock, standard No.4 covers it. If the same panels also live in a coastal, cleaner-heavy, or chemical environment, **Super Finish keeps the same visual face but adds a passivation route we can validate against the service condition.**

## Super Finish — Same Steel, More Surface Reserve

Pass the same stainless sheet through a passivation and finishing sequence tuned specifically for it, and a meaningful thing happens: the finished surface can test far above the minimum value published in ASTM, BS, and JIS for the base grade. In validated routes, we have seen results around six times that minimum. Same alloy. Same thickness. Same visible No.4 or Hairline face. A different service-life conversation.

**What Super Finish actually is.** A controlled passivation pass after the brushing line, optimised for surface chemistry rather than just visual grit. The No.4 or Hairline texture sits on top; the protection sits just under it.

**Where it earns its price.**

- Coastal kitchens, rooftop equipment, marine-adjacent interiors
- Hospital and lab surfaces cleaned with aggressive chemistry multiple times a day
- Public transport and commercial spaces where humidity and chlorides combine
- Any job where the buyer has been burned by 304 pitting faster than expected

**Two ways it ships.**

- **Super No.4 HL** — short brushed grain with the enhanced passivation
- **Super No.4 SB** — straight-line brushed variant, same protection profile

We do not recommend Super Finish for dry, low-contamination interior work. Standard No.4 is the right answer there, and the price reflects that.

## When Standard No.4 Is the Right Call

Standard No.4 is usually the best fit when:

- the project needs a directional finish but not a dramatic long-grain effect
- service scratches should fade into the surface, not read immediately
- the panel will be fabricated, bent, welded, or restocked later
- the buyer wants the familiar commercial finish — kitchen equipment, elevator trims, appliance housings, interior architectural panels

If the grain itself is part of the visual concept, [hairline](/surfaces/stainless-steel-hairline-finish) is usually better.

If brightness or reflection is the point, step toward [BA](/surfaces/stainless-steel-ba-finish) first. When the reflection itself has to carry the design, move to [8K mirror](/surfaces/stainless-steel-8k-mirror-finish). If environment is the issue, the answer is Super Finish, not a brighter finish.

## How No.4 Compares With Hairline, BA, and 2B

Use this comparison as a finish decision, not a roughness contest.

- **2B** is the smooth working surface for feedstock, process equipment, and fabricated parts that do not need a decorative brushed face.
- **No.4** is the practical satin grain for kitchens, equipment skins, elevator trims, and general interiors. Specify the grain and match it, not just the Ra band.
- **Super No.4** keeps the same visual language but adds corrosion reserve for coastal, cleaner-heavy, or chemical-adjacent service.
- **Hairline** is longer and more architectural, which is why lot-to-lot mismatch shows faster on large panels.
- **BA** is a bright mill face, useful for appliance fronts and bright decorative sheet, but it marks and reflects differently from brushed work.

Ra bands of No.4 and hairline often overlap, which is why the real choice should be written around grain character and project use, not around Ra alone.

## Why Our No.4 Stays Consistent

No.4 fails most often on *consistency*, not on the first panel. The second batch six months later reads slightly different, and the mismatch is visible when installed next to the original. Three things we lock to prevent that:

- **Prime-only substrate sourcing.** TISCO and BAOSTEEL-equivalent prime material, no secondary stock. Grain uniformity starts with the parent coil.
- **Constant-pressure grinding.** Automated lines keep abrasive pressure, belt speed, and dwell time uniform from head to tail. We record those parameters per lot.
- **Matched film, not generic.** Laser film, PVD-compatible, or standard PE — chosen before production so the same film ships with the restock order two years later.

## Match No.4 to Grade and Product State

No.4 is not one fixed product state. It can ship as sheet, coil, or slit strip, and the grade should follow the service environment.

- **304 / 304L No.4** is the everyday route for commercial kitchens, equipment skins, and practical interiors.
- **316L No.4** is the cleaner-heavy or coastal route when chlorides make ordinary 304 uncomfortable.
- **430 No.4** is useful for dry indoor appliance and trim work where the value case is real and the exposure stays controlled.
- **Sheet, coil, and slit strip** all make sense for No.4 programs; edge condition and film often matter as much as the finish name.

No.4 is often the safest finish when the project still needs forming, welding, or restocking after first release.

## Where No.4 Holds Up Best

- commercial kitchen worktops, splash areas, equipment skins
- elevator doors, trims, and practical public-facing panels
- appliance housings and front panels, often paired with [AFP](/surfaces/stainless-steel-afp-finish)
- interior cladding where a clean brushed field matters more than dramatic grain effect

Large facade cassettes and full-height elevator walls are the places buyers most often step from No.4 into hairline.

## What to Lock Before Ordering

- Specify the finish as `No.4` plus the target grain direction, and Ra band when required.
- If the environment is aggressive, ask explicitly whether Super Finish is worth the upgrade.
- Lock grade and environment together.
- For phased projects, request lot documentation so restocks match the original install.
- Name the film if laser cutting, AFP coating, or sensitive fabrication follows.
- Confirm sheet, coil, or slit-strip state and the required edge condition.

## Why Super Finish Exists, in One Sentence

Standard ASTM numbers tell you the minimum a grade has to do. They do not tell you what it actually does after the right finishing pass. Super Finish exists because the gap between those two numbers is where a lot of service failures live — and closing that gap with passivation chemistry is cheaper than closing it by upgrading the alloy.

If the open decision is grain character, continue with [Hairline](/surfaces/stainless-steel-hairline-finish). For the comparison language, use [No.4 vs Hairline](/knowledge-base/no4-vs-hairline-finish).

If touch performance is the real concern, move next to [AFP anti-fingerprint finish](/surfaces/stainless-steel-afp-finish). If the environment is pushing you toward 316L, read [304 vs 316L coastal framework](/knowledge-base/304-316-coastal-specifier-framework) before you pay for the alloy upgrade.
