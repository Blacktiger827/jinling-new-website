# 2B Finish Stainless Steel Sheet & Coil

**2B is the baseline working surface in the stainless chain. Most of the time the useful buying question is not whether 2B looks finished enough, but whether 2B should stay the delivered face or become something else later.**

That is why 2B belongs in the foundation family. It is the finish that carries most sheet volume and feeds many of the surfaces that look more polished later.

## Keep 2B Honest

2B is the working face. It is often the right answer when the buyer needs a reliable fabrication base, not a decorative promise.

**Functional parts** can stay on 2B when flatness, formability, grade, and MTC evidence matter more than appearance.

**Visible parts** need a second decision. If the face will be judged after installation, decide whether 2B moves to BA, No.4, hairline, mirror, or AFP.

**Unsettled downstream processing** should keep film and packing open until cutting, bending, welding, or finishing is clear.

## Treat 2B as the Default Starting Surface

2B is the standard cold-rolled finish for buyers who need flatness, formability, and reliable supply before they need visual drama.

It is commonly the starting point for:

- general sheet and coil buying
- fabrication programs
- hygienic and process work where smoothness matters more than appearance
- secondary finishing into BA, No.4, hairline, mirror, or AFP when the final face needs more control

## Leave 2B as 2B When the Surface Is Mainly a Working Face

2B is usually the right delivered finish when:

- the sheet is primarily fabrication feedstock
- the project wants a smooth, practical stainless face without decorative expectations
- process equipment, backsides, liners, tanks, or hidden panels matter more than appearance
- the buyer values flatness and forming behavior over brightness or grain

## Move From 2B to BA, No.4, Hairline, or Mirror When the Finish Needs More

Move away from 2B only when the surface has a clearer job to do.

- Step to **BA** when the project wants a brighter mill face or a better polishing base.
- Step to **No.4** when the visible surface needs a practical brushed finish.
- Step to **hairline** when grain direction becomes part of the design language.
- Step to **8K mirror** when reflectivity is part of the brief and the handling discipline is acceptable.

## Why 2B Still Wins in Fabrication

Three reasons keep 2B in the foundation role:

- flatness from skin-pass leveling
- good forming behavior for bending, drawing, and general fabrication
- broad supply range in sheet and coil

2B is often the most honest answer when the buyer does not actually need a finish story yet.

## Match 2B to Grade and Supply Scope

Most 2B orders are decided by grade, thickness, and supply state before anyone talks about appearance.

- **304 / 304L 2B** covers general fabrication in sheet or coil.
- **316L 2B** is the chloride-upgrade route for process equipment, humid service, or cleaner-heavy fabrication.
- **430 or 201 2B** can serve dry indoor value uses when the service limits are understood.

Use the [grade library](/grades) when the alloy is still open.

If the order is moving into sheet or strip, [cut-to-length](/solutions/capabilities/cut-to-length) and [slitting & edging](/solutions/capabilities/slitting-edging) matter as much as finish choice on many 2B orders.

## What to Lock Before Ordering

- Grade and thickness first.
- Sheet, coil, or slit-strip state.
- Width, edge condition, and flatness expectation.
- Whether 2B is the delivered face or only the starting substrate for later finishing.
- Protective film only if the delivered face needs it.
