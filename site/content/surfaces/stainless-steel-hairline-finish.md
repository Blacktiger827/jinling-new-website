# Hairline Finish Stainless Steel Sheet & Coil

**Hairline is the long-grain architectural finish. The point is more than texture. It is controlled visual direction across full panels, elevator walls, and decorative fields where short brushed grain starts to look busy or broken.**

Hairline belongs in the comparison family because most buyers arrive here while deciding between it and [No.4](/surfaces/stainless-steel-no4-brushed-finish), not because they need a long lesson on grinding mechanics.

## Lock the Direction Before the Finish Name

Hairline works when the long, quiet grain is part of the design language. It fails when panels from different lots or orientations meet on the same wall.

**Architectural panels** should lock grain direction, sample approval, and lot continuity before production starts.

**Touch-heavy zones** can keep the hairline look while adding AFP, but the coating and film route should be confirmed early.

**Exterior or coastal exposure** is a grade decision before it is a finish decision. 316L review and maintenance expectations should come before approval of the sample board.

## Choose Hairline When Grain Is Part of the Design

Hairline is usually the better fit when:

- the panel is large enough that grain direction will read from a distance
- the finish should look deliberate and continuous rather than simply practical
- elevator walls, lobby cladding, or facade panels need a calmer architectural line
- the project can manage tighter lot and orientation control

If the job is mainly about durability, easy fabrication, and forgiving restocks, [No.4](/surfaces/stainless-steel-no4-brushed-finish) is often the better fit.

## Compare Hairline With No.4, Mirror, and 2B

The practical split is visual, not just technical.

- **No.4** is the shorter satin grain for equipment, kitchens, and general interiors where the surface needs to be calm but not architectural.
- **Hairline** is the long continuous grain for facade panels, elevator walls, and feature cladding where direction and lot continuity matter.
- **8K mirror** is fully reflective and belongs on landmark accents or mirror work, with stricter handling and maintenance.
- **2B** is the smooth working face for fabrication and process work, not a decorative long-grain finish.

Hairline and No.4 can overlap on roughness. They should still be specified as different visual finishes.

## Match Hairline to the Environment

The installed environment decides the grade before the sample board decides the grain.

- **304 hairline** is the standard route for most indoor architecture and elevator walls.
- **316L hairline** is the better call when chloride, humidity, or cleaner chemistry is real enough to shorten service life.
- **430 hairline** can work for dry indoor decorative value projects, but only where corrosion demand stays low.

Hairline is often chosen for appearance first, but the alloy still decides whether the finish stays clean in service.

## Where Hairline Works Best

- elevator cab walls and door-adjacent decorative fields
- architectural cladding and interior feature walls
- lobby panels where the design wants a calmer, longer grain than No.4
- decorative sheet programs that need a controlled directional look

Hairline is usually a weaker choice where the panel will be heavily abused, repeatedly reworked, or treated like a generic fabrication surface.

## What to Lock Before Ordering

- State grain direction clearly relative to the installed panel.
- Treat panel sequencing and lot continuity as part of the finish plan.
- Match grade to exposure instead of treating hairline as only a cosmetic choice.
- Lock the film choice for fabrication and site handling.
- For large projects, agree how restocks will be matched before the first order ships.
