# China Stainless Steel Market Note: Mar 30 to Apr 5, 2026

This week read more like a pause than a rebound. Holiday timing slowed buying, Wuxi inventory turned up slightly, and cost support kept 300 series from breaking sharply lower.

For overseas buyers, the practical message was modest but important: material was not tight, but the market was not collapsing either. The weakness came from slow demand and holiday caution, not from mills suddenly losing every cost argument.

## What Moved

| Signal | Buyer read |
|---|---|
| 304 softened slightly | demand did not give mills enough support |
| Wuxi inventory moved from destocking to slight accumulation | spot pressure returned before the holiday |
| 200 series eased | low-priced resources increased and stocking slowed |
| 400 series stayed under pressure | cost support loosened while supply stayed available |
| freight remained uneven | route planning still mattered for landed cost |

The 300-series market still had raw-material support, but buyers were not chasing. That combination usually produces a slow market rather than a dramatic one.

## What Not to Overread

Do not treat holiday-soft demand as a full trend reversal. Tomb-Sweeping Day slowed procurement, and downstream buyers were waiting for a cleaner post-holiday signal. A quiet week can tell you that buyers are cautious. It does not always tell you demand has disappeared.

Do not read cost support as a guarantee either. If Wuxi inventory keeps building after the holiday, cost alone may not hold prices.

## Buyer Takeaway

This was not a week that forced rushed buying. It was a week for cleaning the order file: confirm grade, finish, MTC timing, packing, origin, and booking window before the post-holiday market shows its hand.

If you are buying in April, watch whether Wuxi transactions recover after the holiday. If they do, mills may defend offers. If inventory keeps building, there may still be room to negotiate, especially on standard stock.
