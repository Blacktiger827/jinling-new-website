# China Stainless Steel Prices Soften as Indonesian Material Hits a Two-Month High

China’s stainless steel prices softened again after early-November futures gains, while Indonesian 304 material recovered to a two-month high. For [stainless steel coil buyers](/products/stainless-steel-coil), the period showed a divergence between Chinese and Indonesian supply, alongside uncertainty over nickel output, November production schedules and ocean freight costs.

## Stainless steel prices rebound in early November before Chinese material softens

During November 2–6, stainless steel futures continued to rise and market trading was active, although speculative activity also increased. The main stainless steel futures contract closed the week at US$2,148/MT, up 4.59%, after reaching a weekly high of US$2,158/MT.

Jinling Metals’ market tracking recorded stainless steel spot prices rising by US$68/MT during the week. The period’s market commentary linked the rally to a wider commodity move around the US midterm elections, expectations of tighter Indonesian nickel quotas in 2027, and reports of ferronickel production cuts. These factors supported nickel and stainless steel futures sentiment.

### Indonesian nickel production reports influence market sentiment

Bisnis reported on November 6, citing an official from Indonesia’s Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources, that Huayou Cobalt, Harita Nickel and Tsingshan Holding Group were considering nickel production reductions for 2027. The report included a potential 50% reduction at Huayou’s Southeast Sulawesi operations in response to capacity and price pressures. These were reported plans, rather than a confirmed joint production-cut announcement by the companies.

The report also recorded an approximately US$881/MT increase in SHFE nickel over four trading days and a US$38/MT increase in the spot price of [304 stainless steel](/grades/304-stainless-steel).

Separate October 22 reporting said company representatives denied attending a coordinated production-cut meeting. Those responses concerned an earlier meeting rumour, rather than the November report; they did not rule out all company-specific reductions. Discussions about overcapacity and the nickel supply-demand balance remained ongoing.

### Chinese 304 prices ease while Indonesian cold-rolled and hot-rolled prices recover

By Monday, November 9, Chinese stainless steel prices had fallen back by approximately US$7–14/MT. SHFE nickel and stainless steel futures also moved lower as the market returned to a narrow trading range with a weak bias.

Indonesian stainless steel prices moved differently. Gains over the preceding two weeks brought prices back toward late-August levels, recovering the declines recorded since September. As of November 10, Indonesian Tsingshan 304 cold-rolled stainless steel was reported at US$1,975/MT, while hot-rolled material was US$1,905/MT.

Post-period update — November 11: Chinese-produced stainless steel continued to weaken, with the tracked 304 reference declining by a further US$14/MT. This observation falls outside the November 1–10 reporting window.

When comparing [cold-rolled stainless steel quotations](/knowledge-base/what-is-cold-rolling-stainless-steel), buyers should confirm the grade, thickness, width, finish, edge condition and delivery basis. The market references above are trend indicators, not equivalent offers for every specification.

## China stainless steel production schedules point to higher November supply

Mysteel’s survey of 43 Chinese stainless steel mills recorded October crude stainless steel production of 3.4843 million tonnes, down 1.99% month on month. Scheduled November production was 3.5356 million tonnes, up 1.47% from October. The November figure is a production plan, not completed output.

China’s stainless steel exports reached 454,600 tonnes in September, reported as the highest monthly volume of the year to that point. The combination of higher planned mill output and export activity remained relevant to near-term material availability.

## Ocean freight outlook for 2027 points to lower average rates, with route risks remaining

![Shanghai Containerized Freight Index showing 3633.45 points on November 6 2026](/images/insights/china-stainless-steel-prices-inventory-update-november-1-10-2026/shanghai-containerized-freight-index-november-6-2026.png)

*Shanghai Containerized Freight Index, November 6, 2026: 3,633.45 points. Source: Shanghai Shipping Exchange.*

The Shanghai Containerized Freight Index (SCFI) eased to 3,633.45 points on November 6, down from 3,687.35 in the previous assessment. This was a decline of 53.90 points, or approximately 1.46%. SCFI is an index, not a dollar freight quotation for a particular shipment.

New vessel deliveries and a gradual return to Red Sea and Suez Canal routes were increasing effective shipping capacity. Shorter voyages can release capacity previously absorbed by diversions around the Cape of Good Hope, adding pressure to freight rates.

### Xeneta forecasts a 30% decline in average spot rates

At its November 5 summit in Munich, Xeneta presented its Ocean Outlook 2027. The forecast placed full-year 2027 average container spot freight rates 30% below the third-quarter 2026 baseline, with average long-term contract rates 5% lower. These are forecasts against a specified comparison period, not reductions already achieved or guaranteed savings for individual shippers.

Xeneta projected global container fleet growth of 6.8% in 2027, within a forecast range of 5.4%–8.3%. Container transport demand was expected to grow by 2.5%–4%. With fleet growth projected to outpace cargo demand, shipping lines could face more competition for cargo and buyers could have greater negotiating room on some routes.

A lower global average would not mean every trade lane weakens at the same time. US and European routes could still experience substantial demand for capacity, and route-specific conditions would continue to affect booking costs.

### New ship deliveries add capacity, but carriers can adjust supply

The outlook projected approximately 2.9 million TEU of new vessel capacity entering service in 2027 and around 500,000 TEU leaving through demolition. Even after those removals, fleet growth was forecast at 6.8%, compared with Xeneta’s 4.9% estimate for 2026. If cargo growth fails to keep pace, some routes could face excess space.

More vessels would not automatically make every shipment easier to book. Carriers can manage effective capacity through blank sailings, route changes, slower operating speeds and vessel retirement. For procurement planning, carrier capacity management matters alongside the headline delivery figures.

### Red Sea route recovery releases capacity previously tied up in detours

Xeneta reported weekly container capacity arranged through the Red Sea at approximately 467,000 TEU in October 2026, around twice the late-August level. The return toward Suez Canal routings had released approximately 599,000 TEU previously absorbed by longer Cape of Good Hope voyages.

For European importers and exporters, that released capacity, combined with new ship deliveries, could increase available space and place freight rates under pressure. The pace of route recovery nevertheless remained dependent on Red Sea security conditions.

### Slower demand growth may follow earlier shipment front-loading

Global container transport demand grew by approximately 4.7% in the first eight months of 2026. Xeneta linked part of that growth to front-loaded shipments: concerns about Strait of Hormuz disruptions, fuel costs and later supply-chain interruptions encouraged shippers to move cargo earlier.

Earlier shipping can make current volumes appear stronger while drawing forward demand from later periods. The forecast therefore placed 2027 demand growth at 2.5%–4% and anticipated a potentially weaker peak season. If peak-season cargo falls short of expectations, concentrated freight-rate increases may be harder for carriers to sustain.

### Geopolitical developments could change the freight forecast

The forecast of lower average freight rates would not remove market volatility. The 2024 Red Sea crisis, US tariff-policy developments in 2025 and Strait of Hormuz disruption in 2026 each changed transport conditions. Between February 28 and October 31, 2026, spot rates from the Far East to the US West Coast increased by 343%, illustrating how quickly an expected trend can reverse.

A continued return to Suez would shorten voyages and release effective capacity. Renewed security problems could push ships back around the Cape of Good Hope, absorb capacity again and place upward pressure on freight rates. This remains a significant uncertainty in the 2027 outlook.

### Schedule reliability remains a separate procurement concern

Xeneta’s September 2026 schedule reliability scorecard put global on-time arrivals at 27%. On the Far East–Europe route, only 4% of vessels arrived on time in September, with average delay across all vessels reaching 9.9 days.

A return to shorter Suez routings could improve vessel turnaround. During the transition, however, ships arriving after Cape diversions and those using Suez could reach European ports close together, increasing the risk of concentrated arrivals and congestion. Freight budgets and delivery schedules therefore need separate checks.

### Drewry’s November 5 freight assessment shows differences between routes

![Drewry Shanghai export container freight route chart through November 5 2026](/images/insights/china-stainless-steel-prices-inventory-update-november-1-10-2026/drewry-container-freight-routes-november-5-2026.png)

*Drewry route chart through November 5, 2026. Rates are US dollars per 40-foot container.*

Drewry’s World Container Index assessment dated November 5 stood at US$4,389 per 40-foot container (FEU), down 1% week on week. Shanghai–New York rates fell 3% to US$9,303/FEU; Shanghai–Los Angeles rates were unchanged at US$7,539/FEU; and Shanghai–Rotterdam rates declined 2% to US$3,615/FEU.

Drewry’s Red Sea tracking for the week ended November 1 recorded 53 containership transits through Suez in both directions, with approximately 60% of Asia–Europe capacity using the Suez route. This supported the picture of recovering effective capacity, while the November 5 assessments showed that freight movements remained different across trade lanes.

### What the freight outlook means for stainless steel buyers

The 2027 market faces prospective supply pressure, but the 30% spot-rate decline remains a forecast. Buyers should monitor new vessel deployment on relevant routes, blank sailings, Red Sea routings and shipment timing rather than build a purchase decision around a single freight forecast.

Material cost, [export packaging and logistics](/solutions/capabilities/packaging-logistics), destination freight and delivery timing should be assessed separately. An apparently lower freight rate can still coincide with schedule uncertainty or different packing and handling requirements.

## Further stainless steel supply and trade developments

### Krakatau Steel outlines an Indonesian stainless steel slab project

Indonesian state-owned producer PT Krakatau Steel outlined plans for a stainless steel slab project with annual capacity of 1.2 million tonnes at the DTIIF forum held in Jakarta on October 26–28, 2026, according to ANTARA’s reporting. This was a presentation of the planned project, not confirmation that the plant had entered production.

### Vietnam opens an anti-dumping investigation into certain cold-rolled stainless steel

On November 6, Vietnam’s Ministry of Industry and Trade issued Decision No. 2948/QĐ-BCT, initiating investigation AD25 into certain cold-rolled stainless steel from mainland China, Indonesia and Taiwan. The product scope covers coils, [stainless steel sheets](/products/stainless-steel-sheet) and strips no more than 3.5 mm thick under nine HS codes within headings 7219 and 7220.

The applicants were Posco VST and Tan Viet Metal Science and Technology. Their claimed dumping margins were 76.6% for mainland China, 75.5% for Indonesia and 51% for Taiwan. These are allegations submitted by the applicants, not findings or duty rates imposed by the investigating authority.

The dumping investigation period runs from October 1, 2025 to September 30, 2026, while the injury assessment period begins on October 1, 2022. Interested parties have until January 5, 2027 to register. Provisional duties may be considered if the investigation reaches the required preliminary findings; initiation alone does not establish a new duty.
