# China Stainless Steel Prices Ease as Production Cuts Offer Support

China’s stainless steel market remained under pressure during October 12–16, 2026. Falling nickel pig iron (NPI) and high-carbon ferrochrome prices weakened production cost support, while reduced mill production schedules and inventory drawdowns helped limit the downside. Prices for [304 stainless steel](/grades/304-stainless-steel) continued to ease, futures traded weakly, and buyers largely held back from restocking.

The central market tension was between lower raw material costs and tighter supply. With demand below seasonal expectations, the report’s short-term outlook remained cautious.

## Raw material costs fall as NPI and ferrochrome remain weak

### Nickel pig iron prices continue to soften

High-grade NPI prices remained weak during the reporting week. As of October 15, domestic ex-factory prices were reported at US$154.8/NPI unit, down US$2.2 from the previous week. Prices extended their decline from September, while mills’ target purchasing prices moved close to US$154/NPI unit.

Three factors weighed on the market. Increasing NPI and nickel ore supply from Indonesia reinforced expectations of looser raw material availability. Lower stainless steel selling prices squeezed mill margins and increased procurement pressure on suppliers. Weak and volatile nickel prices also reduced support for NPI.

### Ferrochrome prices decline alongside chrome ore

The average high-carbon ferrochrome spot price fell to US$1,162/50 reference ton, down US$10.9 week on week. Mainstream ex-factory quotations declined to US$1,140/50 reference ton. The price of 40–42% Turkish lump chrome ore also fell to US$10.42/dmtu.

For October, TISCO and Tsingshan lowered high-carbon ferrochrome tender prices by US$30/50 reference ton to US$1,147 and US$1,177/50 reference ton, respectively.

Trading remained subdued. Reduced ferrochrome production lowered chrome ore demand, while losses among ferrochrome producers limited their willingness to accept higher ore prices. The report’s short-term outlook for high-carbon ferrochrome remained weak, with a further modest decline possible.

## China stainless steel prices and inventory by grade

### 304 stainless steel prices ease as mills lower opening quotes

Both cold-rolled and hot-rolled 304 stainless steel coil prices moved lower during October 12–16. In Wuxi, the base quotation for 4-foot-wide, untrimmed-edge cold-rolled 304 from private mills declined to US$2,160/MT, while the private-mill hot-rolled quotation fell to US$2,120/MT.

Early in the week, Tsingshan reduced opening quotations for 304 cold-rolled and hot-rolled products by US$30/MT. As traders’ holding costs fell, downstream buyers continued to purchase only for immediate needs. Restocking remained limited, and peak-season demand fell short of expectations.

Buyers comparing these figures can refer to [what to specify when comparing cold-rolled stainless steel quotes](/knowledge-base/what-is-cold-rolling-stainless-steel) before treating different quotations as equivalent.

For October 19–23, the report’s period-end outlook placed mainstream 304 prices in a range of US$2,120–2,180/MT. Weak raw material prices were expected to constrain gains, while reduced production schedules and low inventories could provide downside support.

### 201 stainless steel sees weaker cold-rolled prices and an inventory drawdown

[201 stainless steel](/grades/201-stainless-steel) showed a mixed pattern: cold-rolled prices were weak, while hot-rolled prices were relatively stable. Mainstream base quotations were US$1,450/MT for J1 cold-rolled material, US$1,270/MT for J2/J5 cold-rolled material, and US$1,305/MT for J1 5-foot-wide hot-rolled material.

Slower mill shipments and tighter circulating supply supported hot-rolled quotations. Reported 200-series inventory fell by 6,200 tons to 80,800 tons during the week. However, downstream consumption remained slow, so the drawdown did not eliminate inventory pressure. The near-term outlook remained broadly stable with a weak bias.

### 430 stainless steel cold-rolled quotes hold while hot-rolled prices soften

[430 stainless steel](/grades/430-stainless-steel) showed relative strength in cold-rolled products and weakness in hot-rolled products. In Wuxi, cold-rolled quotations were unchanged week on week at US$1,290/MT. Hot-rolled quotations fell by US$9/MT to US$1,160/MT.

Lower ferrochrome prices weakened cost support, and transaction volumes remained subdued. Reported 400-series inventory fell by 3,500 tons to 139,300 tons. Without a new supply or demand catalyst, the short-term outlook remained broadly stable with a weak bias. A recovery in end-user production schedules, including home appliances, could support procurement.

## China stainless steel exports recover in September but remain lower year to date

China’s stainless steel exports reached 455,000 tons in September 2026, up 9,000 tons, or 2.0%, from August and up 36,000 tons, or 8.6%, year on year. January–September exports totaled approximately 3.351 million tons, down 432,000 tons, or 11.4%, from the same period of 2025.

Monthly net exports reached 329,000 tons, the highest level of the year to date. The report attributes the monthly recovery mainly to deliveries of backlogged orders and restocking ahead of the traditional peak period. The year-to-date decline nevertheless points to continuing export pressure.

### Trade measures and overseas demand remain export considerations

Japan introduced provisional anti-dumping duties of 27.7%–42.1% on covered cold-rolled stainless steel products from mainland China in July 2026. The measure applies to specified products and producers; it should not be read as a blanket duty on every stainless steel product.

Other trade factors include the EU’s steel import framework under Regulation (EU) 2026/1384, the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), the continuation of EAEU anti-dumping measures on Chinese stainless steel welded pipes, and investigations in Brazil and South Africa.

The report describes European and US manufacturing demand as subdued, with overseas end-users focusing on inventory reduction. It also identifies continued expansion of Indonesian stainless steel capacity as a source of competition for Chinese export orders.

## Sea freight rates diverge while the SCFI edges higher

The Shanghai Shipping Exchange’s Shanghai Containerized Freight Index (SCFI) stood at 3,679.58 on October 16, up from 3,664.11 on October 9. The increase of 15.47 points was approximately 0.4% week on week.

## What stainless steel buyers should watch

The reporting period highlights three factors for procurement planning: whether raw material price weakness continues, whether lower mill output translates into sustained inventory reductions, and whether demand improves enough to support restocking. Domestic material prices, product specifications and destination freight should be assessed separately when comparing purchase options.

This report covers market conditions during October 12–16, 2026, with September trade statistics included as background. Prices and outlooks relate to their stated periods and are not current quotations.
