# Stainless Steel for Architecture: 304 vs 316L, Coastal Rules & Finish Selection

**Architectural stainless starts with exposure, not finish.** The same polished sample can be a good lobby choice, a weak coastal facade choice, or an unnecessary cost step depending on where it lives.

Material and finish choices should support the project specification. Structural design, fabrication, and project engineering remain with the architect, engineer, and facade contractor.

## First Mark the Exposure

Architecture is a map before it is a finish schedule. Put the project into the right exposure band first, then decide which surfaces deserve a higher-control finish.

**Inland interiors** can usually start from 304, 430, or selected 201 decorative routes if the panel stays dry and controlled.

**Coastal or exterior work** should move grade first. A better finish helps maintenance, but it does not turn 304 into a coastal material.

**Feature panels** need a separate finish note: grain direction, lot matching, mirror inspection, and protective film should be agreed before the finish sample is approved.

## Read the Exposure Map Before the Finish Schedule

| Exposure zone | Typical use | Common grade |
|---|---|---|
| Indoor dry and climate-controlled | decorative interiors, elevator-adjacent architecture, lobby work | 304, 430, or selected 201 uses |
| Outdoor inland | general facade and trim away from marine chlorides | 304 |
| Near-coastal and coastal | facade, railing, and exposed trim | 316L review |
| Direct splash and waterfront detail | sculpture, handrails, or features in heavy salt exposure | 2205 specialist review |

The 5 km coastal rule is still useful because it forces the conversation toward exposure instead of optimistic cost cutting.

## Choose the Finish After the Grade

| Finish | Best architectural use | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| No.4 | practical interior and exterior brushed work | more functional than dramatic |
| Hairline | facade panels, elevator-adjacent architecture, feature walls | grain matching and lot control matter |
| 8K mirror | landmark interior accents and selected reflective panels | pits and scratches read immediately |
| AFP over the chosen base | touch-heavy visible interiors | adds maintenance logic, not corrosion reserve |

Mirror on a coastal facade is a grade-and-maintenance decision, not a finish preference alone.

## Keep 430 Mirror Strictly Indoors

430 mirror can be commercially useful on dry indoor landmark panels where the brief wants reflectivity without paying for unused corrosion reserve. It is not a coastal, exterior, or humid-route shortcut.

If the environment is uncertain, step back to 304 or move up to 316L before arguing about shine.

## Treat Panel Width, Lot Matching, and Cleaning as Part of the Spec

- Large panels should be sized against available width before the finish schedule is frozen.
- Hairline and brushed routes need lot and grain continuity on phased work.
- Mirror panels need a clearer inspection and cleaning plan than brushed surfaces.
- Protective film and site handling are part of finish performance on architectural work, not packaging afterthoughts.

## What to Write Into the Order

- Exposure zone first.
- Grade second.
- Finish third.
- Panel size, orientation, and lot-matching requirements.
- Protective-film route where the finish is sensitive.
- Cleaning and maintenance reality if the spec is pushing a higher-risk finish.
