# Stainless Steel for Elevator Interiors

**Elevator stainless ages by touch pattern. The useful first question is which panels passengers handle every day, not which finish looked best on the sample board.**

Finish and material choices should support the cab schedule. Cabin assembly, code compliance, and site installation stay with the elevator OEM and installer.

## Read the Cab by Touch

Elevator stainless should be mapped like a cabin, not bought as one decorative finish.

**Hands-on panels** need AFP, hairline, or No.4 logic before mirror is approved. The cleaning load is part of the specification.

**Feature zones** can carry mirror or PVD when they sit away from constant contact and the film, packing, and inspection condition are controlled.

**Building environment** decides the grade. Standard indoor cabs can stay on 304 or selected 430 routes; coastal, pool-adjacent, or humid buildings should move into 316L review before finish approval.

## Draw the Touch Map Before Approving the Sample

| Cab zone | Common choice | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Ceilings and high accents | 8K mirror or PVD over a controlled base | strong visual effect with lower hand contact |
| Main wall panels | hairline or AFP over the selected base | calmer look and better service behavior |
| Door skins and visible trims | hairline, No.4, or selected mirror | balance of appearance, traffic, and mechanism constraints |
| High-touch push-adjacent and lower panels | AFP over brushed or mirror base | easier cleaning and fewer visible prints |

Once the touch map is clear, the finish schedule stops fighting the maintenance reality.

## Put the Finish on the Right Panel

Mirror works best where the design wants a highlight surface and the panel can be protected. Hairline is the default wall-panel route because it reads as intentional and ages better in traffic. No.4 is the practical choice where the cab needs a more forgiving satin finish. PVD is a color route, not a replacement for touch-planning.

If the panel will be touched constantly, the real decision is usually the base finish plus AFP, not mirror versus brushed in isolation.

## Use 304, 316L, or 430 by Building Environment

| Grade | Elevator use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 304 | standard cabs, lobbies, and interior decorative work | mainstream choice for most projects |
| 316L | coastal, pool-adjacent, or humid buildings | the serious review point where chlorides are real |
| 430 | selected indoor door skins and controlled decorative work | useful value choice, but not for humid or chloride-heavy service |

The right grade is driven more by the building environment than by the cab style sheet.

## Keep Restocks, Grain Direction, and Film Under Control

- Hairline and brushed routes need grain direction locked before production.
- Multi-batch elevator packages need lot control so restocks do not stand out.
- Mirror and AFP routes need the right protective film for fabrication and site handling.
- Door skins should not be treated as interchangeable with wall panels if magnetic or system constraints change the substrate choice.

## What to Write Into the Order

- Zone-by-zone finish map.
- Grade by building environment.
- Which panels are high-touch and should move into AFP.
- Grain direction for hairline or No.4 routes.
- PVD only where the contact risk and repair expectations are understood.
- Protective-film route for fabrication and delivery.
