# Stainless Steel for Medical & Pharmaceutical

**An auditor asks for the Ra certificate at the welded internal surface, not at the parent pipe — and the order arrived with a parent-pipe Ra only.** Pharmaceutical and medical stainless gets judged on audit trail, internal weld hygiene, and surface release at the joint, not on bulk-coupon corrosion data.

Material supply has to support the validation route. GMP qualification, biocompatibility, ISO 13485, and finished-device approvals remain with the device maker, OEM, or plant operator.

## Let Validation Define the Route

Medical and pharmaceutical stainless has to be separated by what will be validated later.

**Product-contact process lines** start with 316L sanitary tube, interior finish, weld condition, and traceability. The question is not only alloy; it is what proof survives audit.

**Cleanroom panels and housings** follow sheet logic. They need cleanability and finish control, but not every non-wetted panel needs a pharma tube standard.

**Instrument stock** belongs to another route entirely. 17-4 PH, 440C, and precision bar decisions should not be mixed into WFI or bioprocess piping language.

## Name the Validated Requirement Before the Material

| Requirement | Typical material logic | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Pharmaceutical and bioprocess product-contact systems | 316L tube with hygienic standard | weld cleanliness, Ra, and traceability drive release |
| WFI and higher-purity piping | 316L plus tighter finish and documentation | validation and surface release are stricter |
| Cleanroom panels and non-wetted housings | 304 or 316L sheet by environment | not every medical panel needs the full pharma tube standard |
| Surgical and cutting instruments | 17-4 PH or 440C bar stock | different logic from hygienic piping entirely |

The route becomes easier once those needs are not mixed together.

## Keep 316L and Surface Finish Together

| Surface requirement | Typical target | Practical note |
|---|---|---|
| General hygienic pharmaceutical service | Ra <= 0.4 µm | common baseline for many process lines |
| Tighter pharma and high-purity service | Ra <= 0.25 µm after finishing | usually tied to downstream electropolish or tighter fabrication control |
| Cleanroom exterior panels | finish by cleanability and appearance | sheet logic, not sanitary-tube logic |

For regulated hygienic work, the finish route should be written together with the grade. Treating them as separate late decisions creates requalification risk.

## Choose the Standard Before the Tube

| Standard | Use it for |
|---|---|
| ASTM A270 | sanitary and hygienic stainless tube |
| ASTM A270 S2 | tighter pharmaceutical-facing sanitary work |
| ASME BPE | system and fabrication standard for high-purity process work |
| ASTM A240 | cleanroom sheet, housings, and non-tube product forms |

The mill-supplied route and the finished-system route are related, but they are not the same thing.

## Reserve 17-4 PH and 440C for Instrument Logic

Medical and pharmaceutical pages often get flattened into 316L everywhere, but surgical frames, forceps, and cutting tools follow a different route. 17-4 PH and 440C belong to instrument logic, not to WFI loop or bioprocess piping logic.

## Documentation QA Will Ask For

- Heat traceability and material identity.
- Interior Ra where the route requires it.
- Hydro or other release checks tied to the product standard.
- Delivery state and any post-fabrication finishing responsibility.
- Clear separation between mill certificate scope and downstream electropolish / system validation scope.

## What to Put on the Order

- Route type: pharma piping, WFI, cleanroom panel, or instrument stock.
- Grade.
- Applicable standard.
- Required surface target.
- Documentation and traceability package.
- Whether downstream electropolish, BPE, or other validation is part of the project.
