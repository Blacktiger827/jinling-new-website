# Stainless Steel for Commercial Kitchen Equipment

**Commercial kitchen stainless should be specified by zone. The mistake is rarely the finish name by itself. It is letting one grade spill from a dry backing area into a wet or chloride-heavy work zone where it does not belong.**

Material choice can support the equipment route, but NSF 51 certification and finished-equipment qualification still sit with the equipment maker.

## Read the Kitchen by Zone

A kitchen schedule should not use one stainless answer everywhere. Read the zone that will punish the material first.

**Wet core.** Worktops, prep counters, splash areas, and wash stations usually begin with 304 / 304L. Move the sink, brine, seafood, or chloride-heavy corners into 316L review instead of upgrading the whole kitchen blindly.

**Visible face.** Appliance fronts and counters are judged by touch and cleaning marks. No.4, BA, or AFP should be chosen with film and cleaning expectations attached.

**Dry sheltered parts.** 430 and 201 only make sense when the panel stays dry, shielded, and away from routine chloride cleaning.

## Build the Kitchen Around a Zone Map

| Kitchen zone | Usual grade | Typical finish | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worktops, prep counters, splash areas | 304 | No.4 or 2B | good everyday wet-zone choice |
| Sink bowls, brine tables, chloride-heavy cleaning areas | 316L review | 2B or No.4 | better margin where 304 gets pushed |
| Appliance fronts and visible equipment skins | 304 or 430 | No.4, BA, or AFP | appearance and touch pattern matter more |
| Dry back panels, liners, sheltered shelving | 430 or 201 | 2B | only where wet cleaning and chlorides stay limited |

The useful discipline is to map the zones first and let the material schedule follow.

## Keep 304 in the Wet Core and Use 316L Only Where Chlorides Justify It

304 covers most commercial kitchen structures and work surfaces. Move to 316L when the service includes brine, salt-heavy prep, harsher cleaning chemistry, or coastal humidity that stays on the equipment.

That upgrade should be targeted, not spread across the whole kitchen by default.

## Put 430 and 201 Only in Dry or Shielded Areas

430 and 201 both have a place, but the boundary needs to be explicit.

- Use them on dry back panels, sheltered internals, and some appliance skins.
- Keep them out of sink zones, chlorinated cleaning routes, and repeated wet service.
- If the buyer expects the panel to survive routine wet cleaning, move back to 304 review.

## Choose the Visible Finish by Use Pattern

| Finish choice | Best use |
|---|---|
| No.4 | work surfaces, canopies, counters, and practical visible faces |
| 2B | process-facing, smoother, or less appearance-driven zones |
| BA | brighter appliance skins in controlled indoor service |
| AFP over No.4 or BA | high-touch appliance fronts that should stay cleaner in use |

In kitchens, the finish should follow the use pattern, not the sample-board appearance alone.

## What to Write Into the Order

- Zone-by-zone grade map, not one grade for the whole program.
- Thickness by component.
- Finish by visible use pattern.
- Whether the panel is food-contact, wet-zone, splash-zone, or dry sheltered.
- Any AFP route for appliance fronts.
- Documentation needed for the finished-equipment certification path.
