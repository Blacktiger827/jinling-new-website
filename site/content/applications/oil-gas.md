# Stainless Steel for Oil & Gas

**Oil and gas material selection changes once the service turns sour.** A grade that survives sweet topsides can fail quickly when H2S, chlorides, or splash exposure increase, so qualification has to come before product names.

Material supply and qualification need to line up early. Threading, coupling manufacture, and field-joint qualification remain with the specialized OEM or service company.

## Pass the Qualification Gate First

Oil and gas stainless should not start with a product name. It starts with the service gate.

**Sweet topside work** can often begin with a 316L screen, then pressure, chloride, temperature, and proof requirements decide whether it stays there.

**Sour or escalation-prone service** moves into NACE / ISO 15156 review before the quote is treated as release-ready. Hardness, heat treatment, PMI, and PREN language matter together.

**Severe sour, subsea, or specialist CRA routes** should stay aligned with the OEM or field qualification path. Material supply can support that route, but it cannot replace it.

## Split Sweet, Sour, and Chloride Exposure Early

| Service condition | Usual grade path | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Sweet topside and moderate chloride service | 316L | practical austenitic choice when H2S stays low |
| Mild sour service with strict hardness control | 316L by qualification | only when the service window is honestly limited |
| Sour, chloride-heavy, or future-escalation service | 2205 duplex | common step-up once qualification tightens |
| Severe sour, subsea, or harsher CRA service | 2507 or higher alloy review | used when duplex baseline is no longer enough |

The key commercial question is not today's field chemistry alone. It is whether the asset is likely to move from sweet to sour during service life.

## Treat NACE and API as Release Gates

NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 is the first gate because it controls composition, heat treatment, hardness, and service limits for sour exposure. API 5CRA and the relevant ASTM pipe standards then control the product route and delivery state.

SA-spec language and ASME / NACE alignment need to sit together. If the route is called sour service, the order should already know which qualification documents and hardness limits will be reviewed later.

## Split Topside, Downhole, and Splash-Zone Service

| Location | Common form | Usual material logic |
|---|---|---|
| Topside sweet process lines | industrial pipe | 316L or 2205 by service margin |
| Sour topside or aggressive chloride service | industrial pipe / CRA supply | 2205 often enters practical review |
| Downhole or specialized CRA tubulars | tubular supply via API / OEM qualification | duplex or higher alloy by well conditions |
| Splash-zone and marine-adjacent details | pipe, tube, bar | duplex logic often replaces routine 316L thinking |

Topside, downhole, and splash-zone routes should not be collapsed into one generic stainless line item.

- chloride load and temperature rise together
- the service sits in splash-zone or marine-adjacent conditions
- the project wants more strength and better sour-service margin from the same material decision

In many oil and gas orders, the real debate is not 316L versus something exotic. It is 316L versus 2205, with qualification discipline deciding the answer.

## What to Put on the Order

- Sweet or sour service declaration.
- Key chemistry: H2S, chloride, temperature, and pressure.
- Grade and product standard.
- NACE / ISO qualification path and hardness requirement.
- Any PREN, NDT, or pickling requirement tied to the route.
- Whether the product is topside, downhole, or splash-zone service.
