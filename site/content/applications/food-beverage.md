# Stainless Steel for Food & Beverage Equipment

**Food and beverage work gets clearer when the first split is product-contact versus utility. Once that line is clear, grade, tube standard, Ra target, and paperwork stop fighting each other.**

The material route can support hygienic design, but it does not replace it. Orbital welding, electropolishing, and final validation still belong to the fabricator or OEM.

## Make the First Split Before Price

Food and beverage orders become clear when the buyer separates product-contact duty from utility duty.

**Product-contact lines** need the sanitary route first: ASTM A270, interior finish, weld condition, and traceability. Grade follows the cleaning chemistry.

**Utility lines** can be honest pressure pipe when they carry steam, glycol, CIP supply, or non-product service. They should not borrow sanitary language unless bore hygiene is explicitly required.

**Exterior sheet and covers** are a different conversation again: cleanability, grade, and finish matter, but they do not need to carry tube rules.

## Separate Product-Contact From Utility Service

| Service type | Typical product form | Usual standard | What matters first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product-contact transfer and process lines | sanitary tube | ASTM A270 | interior finish, weld cleanliness, traceability |
| Dairy and high-cleanability service | sanitary tube | ASTM A270 + tighter Ra target | chloride/CIP chemistry, documented Ra |
| Utility, CIP supply, glycol, and steam | industrial pipe | ASTM A312 | pressure basis and wall schedule |
| Exterior shells and non-contact covers | sheet | ASTM A240 | cleanability and finish choice, not sanitary tubing rules |

The most common mistake is using pressure-rated pipe where the real requirement is a hygienic interior surface.

## Pick 304L or 316L From the Cleaning Chemistry

316L is the safer default for product-contact routes that see chlorides, salty products, aggressive CIP chemistry, or repeated sanitizing cycles. 304L still works well in lower-chloride beverage and general food service where the chemistry stays milder.

Use the L route for welded assemblies. The lower carbon limit protects the heat-affected zone during fabrication and later cleaning cycles.

## Choose A270 or A312 Before You Talk Price

| Standard | Use it for | Not a substitute for |
|---|---|---|
| ASTM A270 | sanitary and hygienic product-contact tube | pressure pipe without interior-finish control |
| ASTM A312 | utility, pressure, steam, and non-product service | sanitary tube with a defined interior Ra requirement |

If the line touches product, A270 is usually the starting point. If the line is utility-only, A312 may be the more honest route.

## Set the Interior Finish and Documentation

| Service band | Typical interior target | Practical note |
|---|---|---|
| General food contact | Ra <= 0.8 µm | normal hygienic baseline |
| Dairy and tighter cleanability service | Ra <= 0.4 µm | common sanitary benchmark |
| Pharma-adjacent or very high-purity service | Ra <= 0.25 µm after downstream finishing | electropolish is usually handled by the fabricator |
| Utility lines | no sanitary Ra target | pressure and service conditions matter more |

For most buying conversations, the document route matters almost as much as the Ra route: heat traceability, hydro result, and measured interior finish should all be easy to release later.

## Match the Material to Dairy, Brewery, and General Beverage

| Process line | Typical material logic |
|---|---|
| Dairy | 316L + ASTM A270 + tighter Ra and paperwork |
| Brewery and many beverage lines | 304L or 316L + ASTM A270, chosen by chemistry |
| General food and juice | 304L where chlorides stay modest, 316L where CIP is harsher |
| Utility and support lines | ASTM A312, with grade chosen by service chemistry |

## What to Write Into the Order

- Grade: 304L or 316L.
- Product-contact or utility route.
- ASTM A270 or ASTM A312.
- Interior Ra target where applicable.
- Required documentation and traceability.
- Whether downstream electropolish, 3-A, or other system qualification is part of the project.
