# Packaging & Logistics

Packaging and logistics are the last control point before the buyer sees the surface. If the shipment moves badly, the right material can still become a claim.

The route has to protect the finish, hold off corrosion, and leave enough evidence to prove how the goods were packed and loaded.

<p class="article-note"><em>The pack should show its logic before loading: face separation, edge protection, moisture control, and proof that the lot stayed readable.</em></p>

## Choose the Pack by What Can Fail in Transit

Packaging should be chosen by what the shipment must protect: surface, edge, bore, moisture condition, or traceability. The right stainless can still become a claim if the load shifts, sweats, or marks itself in transit.

| Open decision | Pack route | Release check |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror or polished faces? | face separation and edge protection | prevent sliding contact before the buyer ever inspects finish |
| Long sea freight or humid transit? | VCI, barrier, desiccant review | control container sweat and storage risk |
| Mixed forms in one shipment? | load-plan review | separate coil, sheet, tube, and bar so proof and surfaces stay readable |

## What Transit Protection Must Cover

- **Moisture:** VCI, barrier, and desiccant work as one route. The goal is to slow rust, block container sweat, and reduce sealed-container humidity.
- **Faces and edges:** polished material needs separation before movement. Edge wrap and face separation protect mirror, AFP, and brushed material from sliding contact.
- **Load stability:** crate, pallet, and strapping keep the lot still. Export wood and strap tension should match the route, weight, and receiving condition.
- **Traceability:** labels keep the lot readable after transit. Heat number, handling rule, and loading proof should travel with the shipment.

## Typical Pack Logic

- Mirror and polished faces get edge protection plus face separation.
- Sanitary tube gets end protection and clean internal handling.
- Heavy plate gets stronger crating, moisture control, and tighter strapping.
- Slit or cut material gets separation so faces do not mark one another.

## Loading and Proof

Loading is release evidence, not warehouse work alone.

The receiving side should know what was loaded, how it was braced, and how the faces were kept apart. Loading photos are the simplest proof layer for that.

## When Shipments Need Extra Care

- Mirror and AFP faces
- Long inland or ocean transit
- High-humidity destination storage
- Mixed-destination LCL moves
- Export lots that need buyer-side QC evidence

## Release Checklist

1. Confirm the face type and film condition.
2. Choose barrier, edge wrap, and crate logic for the route.
3. Verify strap tension, labels, and desiccant before dispatch.
4. Capture loading photos before the container leaves.

Packaging is where a correct order either stays correct or becomes a dispute. Treat it as part of release control, not as an afterthought.
