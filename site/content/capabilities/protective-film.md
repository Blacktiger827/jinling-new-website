# Protective Film Capability

Protective film is part of the process route. If the film does not match the next step, the surface can leave the line correctly and still arrive wrong.

The rule is simple: laser, bending, mirror, AFP, storage, and export do not all want the same tack, adhesive, or removal behavior.

<p class="article-note"><em>The film is part of the process route. It has to peel cleanly after the real handling, not only look right at application.</em></p>

## Choose Film from the Next Process Backward

Protective film should be selected from the next process backward. Jinling uses imported film options when mirror, AFP, laser cutting, long transit, or sensitive release needs a known adhesive and peel profile.

| Open decision | Film route | Release check |
|---|---|---|
| Laser cutting follows delivery | laser-compatible film | confirm cut-through behavior and residue limit |
| Mirror or AFP face must stay clean | low-residue imported film | protect the surface without haze, print, or coating damage |
| Transit or storage will be long | dwell-time review | match tack, UV exposure, and peel point before packing |

## What Must Be Matched

- **Next process:** laser, bending, storage, and installation ask for different film. The route starts from what the buyer will do next, not from the warehouse default.
- **Face type:** mirror and coated faces need cleaner release. The film has to protect without haze, print, adhesive transfer, or coating damage.
- **Dwell time:** long storage changes residue risk. UV exposure, heat, and delayed peel-off should be known before packing.
- **Removal point:** peeling too early or too late can mark the face. The release condition should say where the film is expected to come off.

## Film Selection Matrix

| Downstream process | Film behavior to look for | Control note |
|---|---|---|
| Fiber laser cutting | clean cut-through, low residue | specify laser-compatible film |
| Press-brake bending | stretch without fracture | match tack to bend severity |
| Mirror surfaces | easy peel, no haze or residue | use the cleanest release film |
| AFP-coated faces | coating-safe adhesive | confirm coating compatibility |
| Export storage | stable over time | check UV and residue limits |

Named systems such as Novacel, Nitto, and Polyfilm are selected when the route needs a known film profile. Standard PE or PVC is fine only when the downstream process is simple.

## Application Process

Film should be applied before the first handling risk, not after the first mark.

That means finish, film, cutting plan, and packing need to be decided together.

## When Film Must Change

- If the part will be laser cut, the film must survive the kerf cleanly.
- If the part will be bent, the film must stretch or break predictably.
- If the part will sit in storage, the film must hold without leaving residue.
- If the part is mirror or AFP, the film must protect the face without printing into it.

## Practical Rules

1. State the next process at order stage.
2. Match the film to that process, not to the warehouse default.
3. Keep the film on until the last realistic handling point.
4. Peel and inspect in the same viewing condition that the buyer will use.

## Film Grades

- Low-tack film belongs on mirror and other sensitive faces where residue control matters most.
- Medium-tack film is the common middle choice for general fabrication.
- High-tack film helps deeper handling and forming, but it can print into the face if held too long.

## Transit and Storage

Indoor storage and long transit are not the same as immediate shop use. If dwell time stretches, check a sample peel before full removal.

Outdoor exposure needs UV-stable film. Standard PE is not a long outdoor storage plan.

Heat and welding are a separate risk. Remove film in the heat zone before work starts.

Film is a control layer. If the next step changes, the film should change with it.
