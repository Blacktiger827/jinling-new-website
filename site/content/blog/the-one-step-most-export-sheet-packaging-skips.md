# Which Five-Second Packing Step Saves Stainless Sheet Claims?

In a cut-to-length stainless pack, the first and last sheets take the punishment. They meet the table, straps, covers, forklift handling, and unloading tools before the protected middle sheets do.

The small fix is to turn those two sheets face-in before the pack is closed.

## Why It Works

If the finished face points outward, the top and bottom sheets become sacrificial without anyone saying so. Film helps, but it cannot stop strap pressure, edge scuffing, or dirty contact during handling.

Turning the first and last sheet face-in puts the finished surface against the safer side of the pack. It is a simple habit, and habits are what separate export packing from warehouse packing.

## It Does Not Replace the Whole Pack

Turning the sheets face-in is one detail. It still needs the rest of the system: dry packing, edge protection, correct film, controlled strapping pressure, and photos before loading.

If the pack slides in the container or absorbs moisture, the five-second step will not save the shipment by itself.

## Where Buyers Can Check It

This detail is easy to verify before shipment. Ask for one photo before the cover sheet goes on and one photo after the pack is strapped. The first photo shows whether the finished face was protected. The second shows whether the pack can survive handling.

If the factory cannot show that sequence, the packing method may exist only in the quotation text.

## What a Good Sheet Pack Shows

The packing record should show:

- finished faces protected
- first and last sheets turned face-in where the finish requires it
- interleaf or film matched to the surface
- edge and corner protection
- waterproof or moisture barrier where needed
- labels tied to heat, size, and finish
- photos before loading

For mirror, BA, AFP, and No.4 export sheets, this is not overcare. It is the cost of avoiding a claim that nobody can prove cleanly after unloading.

## PO Wording

Use a direct line:

> "For visible-finish sheet packs, first and last sheets to be turned face-in or protected with sacrificial cover sheets. Packing photos required before loading."

If the supplier says this is unnecessary, ask for photos of the finished face of the top and bottom sheet before loading. The answer usually tells you enough.
