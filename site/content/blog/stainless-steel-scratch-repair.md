# Can This Stainless Scratch Be Repaired, or Should the Panel Be Replaced?

The safest scratch repair starts with a hard stop: some stainless finishes can be blended locally, and some cannot.

Directional finishes such as No.4 and hairline can often absorb a shallow scratch if the repair follows the grain. Mirror, BA, 2B, and PVD usually do not forgive local hand work. A small scratch can become a larger visible repair zone.

## First Check the Finish

| Finish | Field repair outlook | Why |
|---|---|---|
| No.4 | often possible | grain hides careful blending |
| Hairline | possible but harder | grain must align precisely |
| 2B | poor | repair creates a new directional mark |
| BA | poor | local abrasion changes reflectance |
| Mirror | professional repolish or replacement | any dull spot changes the optical plane |
| PVD | replacement likely | coating cannot be patched invisibly |

If the finish is mirror, BA, or PVD and the panel is visible, stop early and ask whether replacement is cheaper than a bad repair. On mirror, the scratch may be smaller than the cloudy repair patch around it.

## Then Check Depth

Use the fingernail test.

- nail glides over the mark: surface mark, high chance of blending on No.4 or hairline
- nail catches slightly: repair may work on directional finishes
- nail catches clearly: professional grinding or replacement

Once the scratch has real depth, abrasive blending removes surrounding metal faster than it removes the defect. The repaired area grows and starts to look worse than the original mark.

## How to Repair Directional Finishes

Work only with the grain. Never use a circular motion on a brushed panel.

For No.4, start with the lightest abrasive that moves the mark, then step finer until the repair blends into the surrounding surface. For hairline, the final strokes must follow the original line very closely; even a small angle error can be visible.

Avoid steel wool, carbon-steel brushes, chlorine bleach, and coarse grinding discs. They either contaminate the surface or create marks that require full-panel refinishing.

## Do Not Forget Corrosion

Repair is more than visual. Abrasion removes the passive film and may leave embedded particles behind. For dry decorative interiors, the surface usually repassivates naturally. For food, pharma, coastal, or wet-cleaned service, passivation after repair should be part of the work instruction.

If the repaired area will face chloride or cleaning chemicals, document the repair and passivation route. Otherwise the first rust spot may appear exactly where the scratch was "fixed."

## Claim Evidence Before Repair

If the scratch may become a supplier claim, photograph the panel before touching it. Record whether the protective film was intact, where the scratch sits, and whether the mark is visible through the film.

If a mirror panel was scratched after film removal, the issue is often site handling, not factory finish. The evidence window closes quickly once the installer starts blending, polishing, or cutting.

For visible mirror, do not approve on-site polishing compound as a casual fix. A factory repolish may still be possible before fabrication. Once the panel is installed, replacement is often the cleaner answer.
