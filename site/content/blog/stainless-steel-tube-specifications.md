# How Do I Spec a Stainless Steel Tube Correctly?

Most tube-order mistakes come from mixing three things: service standard, geometry language, and inspection requirement. The grade may be correct, but the order still fails because the PO says "pipe" while the drawing needs tube, or because sanitary surface requirements were never written.

The first decision is not 304L vs 316L. It is what the tube has to do.

---

## Choose the Standard by Service

| Standard | Use it for | Do not use it for |
|---|---|---|
| ASTM A213 | Seamless boiler, heat exchanger, high-temperature tube | General cheap tube buying |
| ASTM A249 | Welded boiler and heat-exchanger tube | Sanitary food-contact lines |
| ASTM A269 | General seamless/welded instrumentation and process tube | Code pressure pipe where A312 is required |
| ASTM A270 | Sanitary food, dairy, pharma tube | Industrial pressure piping |
| ASTM A312 | Industrial pressure pipe | Hygienic tube with Ra and bead requirements |
| ASTM A554 | Structural and ornamental tube | Pressure or process-fluid service |

A554 rail tube and A312 pressure pipe may both be stainless. They are not interchangeable products. A270 sanitary tube and A312 pipe may both carry 316L. They are released against different risks.

---

## Tube Language vs Pipe Language

Tube is ordered by actual outer diameter and wall thickness. Pipe is ordered by NPS and schedule.

| Product language | Example | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Tube | `50.8 mm OD × 1.65 mm wall` | Actual dimensions drive the order |
| Pipe | `NPS 2 Sch 10S` | Nominal size plus schedule; actual OD is fixed by pipe standard |

Do not write `50 mm OD Sch 40S` unless you want the supplier to stop and ask what you mean. OD × wall and NPS schedule are different systems.

---

## What Must Be on the PO

A complete tube order should name:

- standard: A269, A270, A312, A554, etc.
- grade: 304L, 316L, 2205, 321, or project grade
- geometry: OD × wall for tube, or NPS + schedule for pipe
- welded or seamless route
- finish and internal surface requirement
- inspection: hydrostatic, eddy current, Ra, bead condition, PMI, or project NDE
- cut length, tolerance, marking, and certificate type

Without these fields, the supplier is forced to guess the release basis. Guessing is where tube orders become rework.

---

## Sanitary Tube Is More Than 316L

ASTM A270 sanitary tube usually needs more than a grade callout. The inside surface, weld bead, cleanliness, and documentation are part of the product.

| Requirement | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Internal Ra | Cleanability and CIP performance |
| Weld bead condition | Product-contact surface and drainage |
| Passivation / cleaning | Removes free iron and restores passive film |
| End condition | Fit-up with sanitary fittings |
| Traceability | Audit and food/pharma release |

For food and dairy work, Ra ≤ 0.8 µm is common. For higher hygienic or pharma-adjacent service, tighter Ra or electropolish may be required. The mill cannot meet a roughness requirement that the PO never names.

---

## Pressure Pipe Is a Different Route

ASTM A312 pressure pipe is ordered by NPS and schedule, usually with hydrostatic test or agreed NDE. It is for industrial pressure service, not hygienic design by default.

If the order carries process pressure, fluid containment, ASME code, or industrial piping scope, start with A312 or the project pipe standard. If the order carries direct food contact and sanitary fittings, start with A270.

That separation prevents the most common mistake: buying a product that is dimensionally close but released under the wrong inspection logic.

---

## Grade Selection Still Follows Service

| Service condition | Usual grade direction |
|---|---|
| General dry or mild service | 304L |
| Food, beverage, mild chemical | 316L when cleaners or chlorides matter |
| Chloride above 316L comfort zone | 2205 or higher review |
| High-temperature service | 321, 347, 310S depending on temperature |
| Structural decorative tube | 304 or 316 by environment |

The grade does not fix a wrong standard. A 316L A554 tube is still not pressure pipe. A 316L A312 pipe is still not automatically sanitary tube.

---

## Tube-Spec Release Checklist

Before placing the order, confirm:

- Is this tube or pipe?
- Is the standard matched to the service?
- Are dimensions written in the right language?
- Is the inspection scope named?
- Does the grade match the environment?
- Is surface roughness named where food, pharma, or cleanability matters?
- Does the MTC need 3.1, 3.2, PMI, or project-specific tests?

If those answers are clear, most stainless tube orders become straightforward.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between A270 and A312?** A270 is sanitary tube with surface and hygiene focus. A312 is pressure pipe with NPS/schedule geometry and pressure-test logic.

**Is tube the same as pipe?** No. Tube uses actual OD × wall. Pipe uses NPS + schedule.

**Can A554 be used for railings?** Yes. It is appropriate for structural and ornamental tube, not pressure service.

**Is 316L always required for sanitary tube?** Not always, but it is common where cleaning chemistry, chlorides, or higher audit expectations exist. 304L can work for milder food-contact service.

**What is the most common PO error?** Mixing tube and pipe language in one line item.
