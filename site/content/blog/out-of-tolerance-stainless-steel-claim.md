# Is It Really Out of Tolerance, or Just Below Nominal?

Many thickness disputes start with a true reading and a wrong comparison.

A 2.0 mm sheet that measures 1.94 mm may look short to a buyer, but it may still be inside the tolerance table named on the PO. The claim does not begin with nominal thickness. It begins with the permitted minimum.

If the material is truly outside tolerance, move fast: hold the lot, measure properly, photograph the evidence, and notify before cutting.

## Step 1: Find the Standard on the PO

The governing standard may be ASTM A480, EN, GB/T, or a customer-specific tolerance. Use the one named in the order.

Ask:

- Which dimensional standard applies?
- Which tolerance class applies?
- Was no-minus or minimum thickness written explicitly?
- Does the standard vary by width, thickness, or product form?

If the PO only says "2.0 mm" and no tolerance class, the supplier will usually fall back to the quoted or mill-default standard.

## Step 2: Measure Like You May Need to Defend It

Use calibrated tools and record the pattern.

For sheet or plate:

- measure multiple points, not the thinnest spot alone
- avoid edge zones if the standard excludes them
- photograph the reading and location together
- record heat number, piece ID, instrument, calibration date, and operator

For flatness, use the agreed reference method. A handheld impression is not a flatness claim.

## Step 3: Decide the Claim Type

| Finding | What it means | Usual remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Below nominal but inside standard | Not a formal tolerance claim | Consider tighter PO wording next time. |
| Outside standard tolerance | Valid dimensional non-conformance | Credit, replacement, return, or sort. |
| No-minus required but not met | Contract non-conformance | Hold and claim. |
| Mixed readings across lot | Sampling dispute | Expand sample or bring third party. |
| Already processed | Return is harder | Financial claim or concession. |

The phrase "out of tolerance" should be used only after this comparison is done.

## Step 4: Notify Clearly

A good first notice says:

> Material from PO [number], heat [number], received [date], is held for dimensional non-conformance. Thickness readings on [sample count] pieces fall below the permitted minimum under [standard/table/class]. Measurement sheet, calibration record, and photos attached. Please respond with replacement, credit, or third-party inspection proposal within 10 business days.

That is enough to start the claim without turning the email into a fight.

## When to Use Third-Party Measurement

Use an independent inspector when:

- claim value is high
- supplier disputes your readings
- tools or measurement method are being questioned
- a customer is waiting on your evidence
- the lot must be released or rejected quickly

The inspector should measure against the same standard and class named on the PO. Otherwise the report creates a second argument.

## Tolerance Shortcut

Nominal thickness is the label. Tolerance is the contract. Before filing a claim, find the permitted minimum, measure defensibly, and keep the lot untouched.
