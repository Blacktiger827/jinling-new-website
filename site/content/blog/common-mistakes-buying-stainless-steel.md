# What Words Are Missing From Most Bad Stainless POs?

Most stainless disputes begin as small missing words. The grade is named, but the environment is not. The finish is named, but the inspection condition is not. The size is quoted, but the tolerance rule that matters is left for later.

Later is when the argument becomes expensive.

## The Mistakes That Keep Repeating

| Missing from PO | What goes wrong later |
|---|---|
| service environment | 304 is blamed for a chloride problem it was never told about |
| finish target | 2B, No.4, mirror, or AFP is judged by different expectations |
| tolerance rule | nominal size becomes a claim about minimum usable size |
| MTC timing | document review happens after payment pressure starts |
| packing method | damage becomes a debate at unloading |
| inspection hold point | the buyer sees the issue after the container has shipped |

The fix is not a longer PO. It is a sharper one.

## Ask for the Real Use

The supplier does not need a novel. It needs the facts that change the material route:

- dry or wet service
- chloride, acid, heat, or food-contact exposure
- visible or hidden surface
- forming, welding, polishing, or slitting after supply
- document and customs requirements
- packing and unloading conditions

Those details decide grade, finish, tolerance, packaging, and lead time.

## Make Exceptions Visible

If a buyer accepts secondary stock, mixed lots, substitute thickness, or a different packing method, write it down. Hidden exceptions become bad claims because each side remembers the compromise differently.

There is nothing wrong with a commercial compromise. There is plenty wrong with pretending the compromise was never made.

## Do Not Let a Sample Carry the Whole Order

A sample can show finish direction, polish quality, or handling expectation. It cannot prove every future heat, document, tolerance, or container condition.

Use the sample as one approval point, then write the route into the PO. If the sample was 304 No.4 with vertical grain and film retained, the order should say that. Otherwise the supplier may ship something technically close and visually wrong.

## A Better PO Line

Instead of:

> "304 stainless sheet, No.4 finish"

write:

> "304 stainless sheet, No.4 finish, vertical grain, visible interior panels, protective film retained through fabrication, MTC 3.1 required, tolerance and packing agreed before shipment."

That is still a short line. It just gives the order enough spine.
