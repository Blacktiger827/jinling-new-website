# What Does China's 2026 Steel Export Licence Change for Buyers?

China's Ministry of Commerce and General Administration of Customs issued Announcement No. 79 in December 2025. From 1 January 2026, selected steel products listed in the annex were added to export licence administration.

For buyers, the important point is practical: this is a documentation gate before export clearance. It is not a tariff, not a ban, and not a new steel grade standard.

## What the Licence Changes

The official notice says exporters of covered goods should apply for the export licence using the export contract and a product quality inspection certificate issued by the producer.

That means the shipment needs a cleaner document chain before it reaches customs:

- contract description
- HS code and goods-name scope
- producer quality document or MTC route
- packing and quantity details
- licence application timing
- customs declaration alignment

If any of those pieces is loose, the shipment can lose time even when the steel is ready.

## Scope Must Be Checked, Not Assumed

The announcement added selected steel products from the annex to licence administration. Buyers should not rely on a broad phrase like `all stainless` or `not stainless`. The practical check is the contracted goods name and HS code.

That scope check should happen before the PO is released, because it affects document timing and booking confidence.

For final scope, use the current official catalogue and the exporter's customs route, not a saved screenshot or a loose market summary. The wording on the quote should match the wording that will later appear in the declaration file.

## What It Does Not Change

The licence does not replace ASTM, EN, JIS, or GB material standards. It does not prove the surface finish is acceptable. It does not remove the need for MTC review, inspection photos, origin documents, or packing evidence.

It also does not mean every product with stainless in the name is handled the same way. The buyer and exporter still need to check the exact HS code and goods description against the official catalogue.

## What Buyers Should Put on the PO

Use wording like:

> "Supplier to confirm whether the contracted HS code and goods name fall under China export licence administration. If covered, supplier to confirm licence application timing and provide licence reference or export-clearance evidence before vessel sailing."

For covered products, the document pack should be aligned before container booking, not after the vessel date is already close.

## What This Filters

The licence makes document-light routes harder. It does not magically remove every weak supplier, but it raises the cost of shipping material whose contract, heat evidence, product description, and customs declaration do not line up.

That is good for serious buyers. It also means very cheap quotes should be checked more carefully, not celebrated too quickly.
