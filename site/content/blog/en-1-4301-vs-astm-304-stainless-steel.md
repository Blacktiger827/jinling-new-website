# Can EN 1.4301 and ASTM 304 Share the Same Certificate?

EN 1.4301 and ASTM 304 are close enough for many real products to behave the same in service. They are not word-for-word identical standards. That difference matters when a buyer needs a certificate to satisfy an EN project, an ASTM project, or both at the same time.

The practical question is not "are they similar?" They are. The question is whether the actual heat sits inside the overlap window.

## The Three Lines That Matter

| Chemistry line | ASTM 304 | EN 1.4301 | Why buyers should check |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Sulfur max | 0.030% | 0.015% | EN is tighter; borderline ASTM heats may fail EN |
| Silicon max | 0.75% | 1.00% | EN allows more; ASTM-bound projects need the lower limit |
| Chromium | 18.00-20.00% | 17.50-19.50% | Dual certification needs 18.00-19.50% |

Most modern 304 heats land comfortably inside these limits. Problems appear when stock is old, sourced from a different regional route, or re-certified after delivery instead of ordered correctly at the start.

## What Dual Certification Means

A dual-certified heat must satisfy both standards at the same time. The MTC should name both ASTM A240 Type 304 / UNS S30400 and EN 10088-2 1.4301.

Do not accept "equivalent to 304" as a substitute for a dual-standard certificate when the project dossier needs both. Equivalence language may be true in casual service, but auditors read standards, not habits.

## Where Cross-Region Orders Go Wrong

The common failure is a European project buying material from an ASTM or GB supply route and noticing the sulfur line too late. A heat can be perfectly valid ASTM 304 and still fail EN 1.4301 if sulfur is above 0.015%.

The reverse can happen with silicon. EN may allow a heat that ASTM will not accept if the silicon sits above 0.75%. It is less common, but it is real enough to check before issuing ASTM paperwork.

## What to Put on the PO

For cross-region work, write the standard requirement clearly:

> ASTM A240 Type 304 / UNS S30400 and EN 10088-2 1.4301, dual-certified on the same MTC.

If the weldment needs low carbon, do not rely on 1.4301 or standard 304. Specify 304L / EN 1.4307 instead.

## Order the Certificate Before the Coil Ships

EN 1.4301 and ASTM 304 are the same stainless family, but the certificate only works when the heat chemistry sits inside both windows.

For most projects, this is easy to handle. Ask for dual certification before production or shipment, not after the material is already on the floor.
