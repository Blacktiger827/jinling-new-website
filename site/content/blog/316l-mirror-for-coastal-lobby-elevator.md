# When Does a Mirror Panel Need 316L?

Most mirror panels do not need 316L. Dry interior lobbies, retail walls, and many elevator features can start with 304.

The upgrade becomes real when chloride, humidity, or wet cleaning reaches the surface. Mirror makes that decision sharper because early staining is highly visible on a reflective panel.

316L is not a luxury label for mirror. It is corrosion margin. If the project is dry and controlled, 304 often gives the better balance. If chloride is part of daily life, 316L becomes easier to defend because the first tea stain on mirror looks worse than the same stain on a brushed panel.

## When 304 Mirror Is Still Enough

304 mirror is normally suitable for:

- dry indoor lobby panels
- controlled retail interiors
- hotel feature walls away from entrances
- elevator accents in non-coastal buildings
- low-touch decorative ceilings or trims

If the panel remains dry, is cleaned with mild chemistry, and does not sit in marine air, 304 is usually the best balance of polishability, cost, and corrosion margin.

## When 316L Becomes Easier to Defend

Move to 316L when the project includes:

- coastal or island locations
- semi-exterior lobbies that breathe marine air
- pool-adjacent interiors
- humid entrances with frequent door cycling
- chloride-bearing cleaning chemicals
- mirror panels near thresholds, doors, or open canopies

A panel can be indoors and still behave like a coastal panel. If salt-laden humidity reaches the surface, 304 mirror can tea-stain or pit earlier than the owner expects.

The decision should be based on exposure, not postcode alone. A covered lobby that breathes marine air every evening may be harder on mirror than a dry interior wall a few streets away.

## Elevators Need a Touch and Exposure Map

For dry urban elevators, 304 mirror can be a normal choice on feature panels. For coastal hotels, transport hubs, seafront residences, or hospital routes, 316L should be reviewed on door skins, jamb-adjacent panels, and threshold-adjacent trims.

If the same panel is also high-touch, the likely route becomes 316L mirror plus AFP, not 304 mirror with wishful cleaning instructions.

## When a Brushed Finish Is Smarter

Sometimes 316L mirror is technically right and still too maintenance-heavy. The alloy can improve corrosion reserve, but it cannot make fingerprints, trolley marks, cleaning streaks, or poor wiping disappear.

If washing will be irregular, airborne salt is frequent, or the owner does not want visible wipe burden, a brushed finish may be the better design answer. Hairline and No.4 do not remove corrosion risk, but they hide daily service marks better than mirror.

## PO Wording

Do not hide the grade behind the finish.

Write:

> "304 No.8 mirror, dry interior decorative service."

or:

> "316L No.8 mirror, coastal lobby service, chloride-cleaner restriction included in maintenance handover."

For high-touch areas:

> "316L No.8 mirror + AFP, touch-zone panels only, reference sample approved before production."

The grade upgrade is necessary in chloride service, but it is not sufficient if the cleaning team uses the wrong chemicals. The handover document matters.
