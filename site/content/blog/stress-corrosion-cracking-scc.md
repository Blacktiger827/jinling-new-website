# Why Does Stainless Crack in Chloride Service?

Chloride stress corrosion cracking is uncomfortable because the part may not look badly corroded before it leaks. A heat exchanger shell, welded pipe, or insulated line can run clean for years, then crack after temperature, chloride, or stress moves just far enough.

The mechanism needs three things at the same time: chloride, tensile stress, and a susceptible material at elevated temperature. Remove one of the three, and the cracking route closes. Ignore the overlap, and upgrading from 304 to 316L may only make the failure more expensive.

## The Three Conditions That Matter

| Condition | What counts in real projects | What to do first |
|---|---|---|
| Chloride | Process water, seawater, cleaners, cooling tower drift, wet insulation | Measure the actual chloride source, not the nominal fluid |
| Tensile stress | Service load, weld residual stress, cold work, thermal cycling | Reduce stress where possible; assume welds carry residual stress |
| Temperature | Often becomes serious above about 60 °C for austenitic stainless | Check maximum metal temperature, not the process average alone |

This is why SCC shows up in practical places: welded heat exchangers, hot chloride piping, evaporators, coastal process equipment, and stainless lines under wet insulation.

## Why 316L Does Not Solve It

[316L](/grades/316l-stainless-steel) improves pitting resistance because molybdenum helps the passive film resist chloride attack. That is useful, but it is not the same as chloride SCC resistance.

304 and 316L are both austenitic. Once hot chloride and tensile stress are present, both remain susceptible. The failure may initiate at a pit or weld toe, but the crack growth is controlled by the stress-corrosion mechanism, not by ordinary surface rust.

If the service is hot and chloride-bearing, do not let "marine grade" language hide the SCC question.

## Why Duplex Helps

Duplex stainless such as [2205](/grades/2205-duplex-stainless-steel) carries a mixed austenite-ferrite microstructure. The ferrite phase interrupts crack propagation and gives duplex grades much better resistance to chloride SCC than fully austenitic 304 or 316L.

That is why duplex often becomes the default for hot chloride service where temperature cannot be lowered and chloride cannot be removed. Ferritic grades can also resist chloride SCC, though they may not meet the strength, toughness, weldability, or corrosion requirements of every job.

## Corrosion Under Insulation Is a Common Trap

Wet insulation can quietly supply all three SCC conditions. The pipe is hot, chloride is trapped and concentrated, and weld or service stress is already present. The outside of the jacket may look normal while the stainless underneath is cracking.

For insulated stainless lines in coastal, chemical, or cooling-tower areas, the material choice and insulation system should be reviewed together. Better cladding and vapor barriers reduce risk. They do not make an under-specified alloy equivalent to the right one.

## Prevention That Actually Changes the Risk

- Lower the metal temperature if the process allows it.
- Remove or reduce chloride through water treatment, cleaning chemistry control, or insulation selection.
- Reduce tensile stress by design, fabrication discipline, and stress-relief routes where applicable.
- Avoid sharp notches, bad weld profiles, stagnant pockets, and wet insulation traps.
- Move to duplex or another qualified alloy when the three SCC conditions cannot be separated.

Passivation helps general surface condition. It does not remove the SCC mechanism when chloride, stress, and temperature remain.

## Do the SCC Check Before the PO

SCC is not random bad luck. It is a threshold problem. A small temperature rise, a small chloride source, and residual weld stress can become enough once they arrive together.

If austenitic stainless is carrying stress in chloride service above roughly 60 °C, ask the SCC question before the purchase order is closed. It is much easier to choose duplex on paper than to explain a clean-looking cracked pipe after startup.
