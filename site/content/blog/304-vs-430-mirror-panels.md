# Should Indoor Mirror Panels Use 304 or 430?

304 and 430 can both be polished into a bright mirror. A small sample may not tell you which alloy is underneath. The difference appears after the panel is installed, cleaned, touched, and exposed to humidity.

Use 430 mirror for dry, low-touch indoor decorative work. Use 304 mirror when the panel sits in a public interior that has humidity, repeated wiping, entrances, or real touch.

## The Quick Split

| Service condition | Better route |
|---|---|
| appliance front, dry interior | 430 mirror can work |
| retail decorative strip, low touch | 430 mirror can work |
| elevator ceiling accent | 430 or 304, depending on building level and exposure |
| lobby wall or column wrap | 304 mirror |
| elevator door skin or touch zone | 304 mirror, often AFP review |
| coastal or wet-clean route | 316L review |

430 is the value route. 304 is the safer interior route. 316L is the chloride route.

## Why Mirror Raises the Risk

Mirror shows small problems fast. Edge staining, wipe marks, rust at a joint, and slight dullness are easier to see on mirror than on a brushed finish.

That is why 430 should not be chosen only because the sample looks the same as 304. The question is whether the installed panel will stay clean enough for the owner to accept it after a year.

In a dry appliance panel, 430 can be the right answer. In a busy lobby near an entrance, 304 gives more margin. In a coastal lobby, even 304 may be optimistic.

## Do Not Judge by Sample or Magnet Alone

The sample tells you the polish quality. It does not prove the service margin.

430 is normally magnetic. 304 can also show some magnetism after cold work, polishing, or forming, so a magnet is not a release method for visible architectural panels. If grade matters, check the mill certificate and use PMI when the order risk justifies it.

Also check the parts that do not show on a sample card:

- whether the edge will be exposed to cleaners or humidity
- whether replacement panels may be ordered later
- whether the same lot can cover adjacent visible panels
- whether the protective film stays on until installation

## What About Cost?

430 usually lowers material cost because it has no nickel. That saving is meaningful on volume decorative work. It is less meaningful on one-off panels where replacement, mismatch, and complaint risk outweigh the material difference.

The buying rule is simple:

- choose 430 when environment is controlled and volume makes the saving real
- choose 304 when public use and cleaning make appearance risk more expensive
- choose 316L when chloride enters the service life

## What to Write on the PO

Avoid the vague line:

> "mirror stainless panel"

Use service-based wording:

> "430 No.8 mirror, dry indoor decorative panel, low-touch service."

or:

> "304 No.8 mirror, public lobby panel, same-lot visible panels, protective film retained through installation."

or:

> "316L No.8 mirror, coastal or chloride-cleaned service, cleaning chemistry confirmed."

That wording stops the grade from being silently swapped behind a finish name.
