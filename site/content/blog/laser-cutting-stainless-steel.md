# Is the Laser-Cut Stainless Edge Ready for the Next Process?

Most laser-cut stainless problems are not about whether the beam can cut through the sheet. They are about what the edge is expected to do next. An edge going straight to welding, coating, passivation, food contact, or visible architectural use needs a different cutting route from an edge that will be ground away.

The assist gas decision often matters more than a small speed gain.

## Nitrogen or Oxygen?

| Next process after cutting | Better assist gas | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Welding without heavy edge prep | Nitrogen | Bright edge, no adherent oxide |
| Visible decorative edge | Nitrogen | Cleaner color and less polishing work |
| Food, pharma, marine, or passivated parts | Nitrogen | Avoid oxide and corrosion-prone residue |
| Thick hidden structural plate | Oxygen may be acceptable | Edge will be ground or machined anyway |
| Lowest cutting cost with planned cleanup | Oxygen | Faster or cheaper, but cleanup must be real |

Nitrogen leaves a bright silver edge when purity and pressure are right. Oxygen cuts aggressively but leaves a dark oxide layer. On stainless, that oxide is not harmless. It can interfere with welding, coating, corrosion resistance, and appearance.

## Fiber Laser Is the Normal Route

For thin and medium stainless sheet, fiber laser is now the normal production route. It cuts faster than older CO2 systems on most sheet thicknesses and uses energy more efficiently.

The more useful production question is not fiber versus CO2. It is whether the machine power, focal setup, gas purity, pressure, and speed are matched to the thickness and required edge quality.

## Watch the Edge as Well as Nest Time

Three edge defects decide whether the part is ready:

- Dross on the bottom edge means the melt was not expelled cleanly.
- Dark oxide means oxygen assist or contamination has left a surface that needs removal.
- Heavy striation or taper means the cut class may not match the drawing.

For contractual work, specify the required ISO 9013 cut quality class or an agreed visual/edge standard. "Laser cut" alone does not say whether the edge is weld-ready or just separated from the sheet.

## HAZ Is Small, but Not Zero

Laser HAZ on stainless is narrow compared with plasma or flame cutting, but it still disrupts the passive film at the edge. On ordinary 304L or 316L sheet, the effect is usually manageable. On duplex, thick sections, martensitic grades, or corrosion-critical service, qualify the parameter window before the production batch.

If the edge will remain exposed in chloride service, passivation after cutting may be part of the release route. If oxygen assist was used, oxide removal comes first.

## What to Put on the Order

For clean downstream processing:

> Fiber laser cut with nitrogen assist, bright oxide-free edge, no adherent dross, cut quality to agreed class, passivate after cutting where corrosion service requires.

For thick structural work where grinding is planned:

> Oxygen assist acceptable only where cut edge will be ground or machined before welding or service.

That wording prevents the cheapest cut from quietly becoming the most expensive cleanup.

## The Production Rule

Choose the cut edge by the next process, not by the fastest nest time. Nitrogen costs more at the cutting machine. Oxygen can cost more after the part leaves it.
