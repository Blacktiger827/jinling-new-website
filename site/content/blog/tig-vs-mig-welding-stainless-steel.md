# Should Stainless Be TIG Welded, MIG Welded, or Both?

TIG and MIG are not better or worse in the abstract. They answer different stainless jobs.

TIG is the finish-first route: clean root, low spatter, better control on thin wall, easier release for visible or hygienic welds. MIG is the throughput route: faster deposition on thicker work, but only when the shop can control transfer mode, heat input, spatter, and cleanup.

## The Fast Choice

| If the job cares most about... | Start with |
|---|---|
| Sanitary bore quality | TIG, often orbital |
| Visible polished or brushed welds | TIG |
| Thin sheet below about 3 mm | TIG |
| Root pass on pipe | TIG |
| Thick brackets, frames, and long seams | MIG or pulse-MIG |
| Pressure-vessel groove fill | TIG root, MIG fill, TIG cap |
| Duplex plate | Qualified TIG or pulse-MIG with heat input control |

The process should follow the release requirement. Stainless defects often come from choosing the method that is fastest at welding, then discovering it is slow at cleanup.

## When TIG Is Worth the Time

Use TIG when the weld will remain visible, contact product, or sit inside tube where cleanup is hard. The arc is calmer, spatter is minimal, and the operator can control the puddle and root profile more precisely.

This is why food, pharmaceutical, dairy, and high-finish architectural work often specify TIG. The slower arc time is cheaper than grinding, re-polishing, or rejecting sugared roots later.

Back purge still matters. A bright cap does not save an oxidized root.

## When MIG Earns Its Place

MIG belongs where section thickness and weld length make TIG too slow. On stainless above about 3 mm, especially brackets, tank shells, frames, and structural seams, pulse-MIG can give good production speed with controlled heat input.

Avoid treating short-circuit MIG as the easy stainless answer. It can create spatter, cold lap, and cleanup work that erases the speed advantage. For stainless production, spray or pulse transfer is usually the safer route.

## Why Hybrid Routes Are Common

On thicker pressure work, the best answer is often not TIG or MIG. It is TIG root, MIG fill, TIG cap.

The TIG root protects the internal surface. The MIG fill deposits metal quickly. The TIG cap gives a cleaner visible finish for inspection. This route puts each process where it earns its keep.

For sanitary or pharmaceutical piping, full TIG may still be required because documentation and root quality are tighter.

## Filler and Gas Still Have to Match

- Use ER308L for 304/304L.
- Use ER316L for 316/316L.
- Use ER2209 for 2205 duplex.
- Do not use carbon-steel shielding gas on stainless.
- Use proper back purge on tube, pipe, and closed sections.

Wrong gas or filler can leave a weld that looks acceptable at first and corrodes first in service.

## Decide the Whole Route Together

Choose the welding process together with filler, purge, heat input, interpass temperature, and post-weld cleanup. They are one fabrication route, not separate decisions.
