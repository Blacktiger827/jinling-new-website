# Why Does Stainless Corrode Under Gaskets and Washers?

Crevice corrosion is what happens when stainless is forced to protect itself in a place oxygen cannot reach.

The open surface may look clean. Under a gasket, washer, clamp, deposit, or lap joint, the trapped liquid becomes oxygen-poor, acidic, and chloride-rich. Once that local chemistry turns aggressive, even a grade that works on the open surface can fail inside the gap.

That is why crevice failures often look unfair: the stainless was fine everywhere except the hidden joint.

## Where It Starts

Common crevice sites:

- gasket faces
- washers and bolt heads
- lap joints and spot welds
- tube-to-tubesheet joints
- clamps and supports
- scale, biofilm, or dirt deposits
- unsealed overlaps on outdoor structures

The tighter and wetter the gap, the higher the risk. Chloride and heat make it worse.

## Crevice vs Pitting

| Question | Crevice corrosion | Pitting corrosion |
|---|---|---|
| Where does it start? | Hidden gap or deposit | Open surface defect or inclusion |
| Why does it start? | Oxygen depletion and trapped chemistry | Chloride attack on weak passive film |
| Is it easy to see early? | Usually no | Sometimes, but often late |
| Can surface polish alone fix it? | Rarely | Sometimes helps initiation resistance |

Crevice corrosion can start at lower severity than open-surface pitting because the geometry creates its own aggressive chemistry.

For pitting, see [what causes pitting corrosion](/knowledge-base/what-is-pitting-corrosion).

## How to Prevent It

Design beats after-the-fact cleaning:

- use full-penetration welds instead of lap joints where possible
- avoid flat trapped gasket faces in chloride service
- choose gasket materials and profiles that reduce stagnant gaps
- seal or drain joints exposed to rain or washdown
- remove deposits before they create artificial crevices
- upgrade alloy when crevices cannot be designed out

If the gap must remain and the service contains chloride, 316L may not be enough. Duplex or higher alloys may be required, depending on temperature and exposure.

## When It Becomes a Claim

A crevice-corrosion complaint needs geometry photos. Without them, the discussion often turns into "the steel rusted" when the real issue is that the steel was trapped in the wrong joint.

Collect:

- photo before disassembly
- photo after removing gasket/washer/clamp
- fluid chemistry, especially chloride
- operating temperature
- grade and MTC
- cleaning and maintenance history

If the corrosion appears only under a gasket and the open surface is clean, the material may be correct while the joint design is not.

## Crevice Shortcut

Crevice corrosion is a geometry problem as much as a material problem. Higher alloy helps, but it does not turn a bad wet gap into a good detail. If the joint stays tight, wet, and chloride-rich, design the gap out or specify for it honestly.
