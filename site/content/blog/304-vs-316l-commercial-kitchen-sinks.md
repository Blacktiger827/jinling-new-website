# When Does a Commercial Kitchen Sink Really Need 316L?

Most commercial kitchen sinks do not need 316L. A well-made 304 sink is the normal answer for restaurants, catering kitchens, prep areas, and dish routes where cleaning chemistry stays moderate and the sink is rinsed properly.

The upgrade question starts when the sink lives with chloride every day.

## Use 304 for Normal Sink Service

[304](/grades/304-stainless-steel) is usually the right baseline when:

- the site uses normal detergent and sanitizer routines
- food chemistry is not strongly acidic or salty
- the sink is rinsed after cleaning
- the location is not coastal or pool-adjacent
- fabrication quality is controlled

In that service, the practical concerns are gauge, weld quality, bowl geometry, drain slope, and finish. Moving to 316L will not rescue a thin, poorly welded sink.

## Move to 316L When Chloride Is Routine

[316L](/grades/316l-stainless-steel) earns its place when the sink repeatedly sees:

- brine, seafood, curing salt, or salty prep work
- bleach or hypochlorite sanitizer that is not fully rinsed
- coastal air plus wet service
- warm chloride residue sitting in corners or creases
- aggressive cleaning used under hygiene pressure

This is not about a premium label. It is about corrosion margin at the places where sinks actually stain first.

## Watch Welds, Corners, and Drain Areas

Sink complaints rarely start in the open middle of the bowl. They start where water and residue sit:

- drain corners
- folded creases
- splashback joints
- welded brackets
- underside seams that stay damp

That is why 304L or 316L wording may matter on welded sink routes. The lower carbon route reduces sensitization risk around weld heat-affected zones, especially when post-weld treatment is not perfect. For simple formed bowls with minimal welding, fabrication quality and cleaning practice may matter more than changing the suffix.

## Finish and Gauge Still Matter

No.4 is common on visible decks and aprons because scratches blend into the grain. 2B can work on formed or hidden areas. A polished show finish may look better on day one but can become harder to maintain in a working kitchen.

Gauge is a separate conversation. A thicker bowl can reduce denting and oil-canning, but it does not change chloride resistance. Do not let thickness language hide a chemistry problem.

## PO Wording

For standard service:

> "304 or 304L commercial kitchen sink, No.4 visible finish, welded areas cleaned and passivated where applicable, protective film retained through fabrication."

For chloride-heavy service:

> "316L commercial kitchen sink, brine/chloride service, welds cleaned and passivated, cleaning chemistry confirmed with owner."
