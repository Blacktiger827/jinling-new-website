# What Should Be on a Stainless Steel Incoming-Inspection Checklist?

Incoming inspection is a release decision, not a box-ticking exercise.

The order matters. If the documents are wrong, there is no point measuring surface roughness. If the heat number does not match, the cleanest sheet in the warehouse is still unverified. A good IQC routine moves from paper to identity to measurements to surface to chemistry, and it stops whenever a gate fails.

Use this sequence:

1. documents
2. traceability
3. dimensions
4. surface condition
5. PMI or chemistry spot-check

## Gate 1: Documents

Check the paperwork before unloading into stock.

| Document | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Purchase order | Grade, standard, finish, dimensions, tolerance, special clauses. |
| Packing list | Quantity, bundle count, coil number, gross/net weight. |
| Mill MTC | Heat number, chemistry, mechanical data, certificate type. |
| Inspection report, if any | Scope matches the PO, rather than a generic pass statement. |

If the MTC is missing, wrong, or not heat-specific, quarantine the lot. Do not let material with a paperwork problem become a production problem.

For certificate review, use [how to read a Mill Test Certificate](/knowledge-base/how-to-read-mill-test-certificate).

## Gate 2: Traceability

The heat number on the MTC must match the physical material. Check tags, stencil marks, bundle labels, coil labels, and any retained markings after repacking.

Hold the lot if:

- heat numbers do not match
- tags are missing or mixed
- one bundle has no identity
- the packing list and physical count disagree
- certificate values look copied or generic

Traceability is not admin work. It is how you prove the steel in front of you is the steel on the certificate.

## Gate 3: Dimensions

Measure against the tolerance standard on the PO, not against nominal thickness alone.

For sheet, record at least five thickness points: four corners and center. For coil, take readings across the width near the outer wrap, then add more if results sit near the minus limit. For tube or pipe, measure OD, wall thickness, length, ovality where relevant, and end condition.

| Finding | Release action |
|---|---|
| All readings inside tolerance | Continue inspection. |
| One borderline reading | Add more points; do not decide from one reading. |
| Repeated under-thickness | Hold and prepare claim evidence. |
| Wrong width, length, or wall | Hold unless concession is approved in writing. |

For tolerance disputes, see [out-of-tolerance stainless steel claims](/knowledge-base/out-of-tolerance-stainless-steel-claim).

## Gate 4: Surface and Packaging

Inspect the surface before the lot is cut, bent, polished, or mixed with other material. Photograph packaging damage before opening if there is crush, water, broken film, or edge impact.

Check for:

- pits, deep scratches, roller marks, rust stain, edge cracks
- film imprint, adhesive residue, wet packing, or missing interleaving
- finish mismatch on visible faces
- burrs or damaged slit edges
- product-contact defects on food or sanitary material

Use written acceptance criteria where the surface matters. If the PO only says "good surface," the receiving team has very little leverage. The surface-defect guide is here: [when is a stainless steel surface defect acceptable](/knowledge-base/stainless-steel-surface-defect-standards).

## Gate 5: PMI or Chemistry Check

PMI is the final confirmation that the released material looks like the ordered alloy family. It is especially useful on new suppliers, high-value lots, 316L orders, duplex orders, or anything where substitution would be expensive.

Use handheld XRF to check Cr, Ni, Mo, and Mn. It catches 201 sold as 304 and 304 sold as 316L quickly. It does not verify carbon, so it cannot prove 304L versus 304 by itself.

Sample by heat number as well as bundle count. A simple baseline is 10% of pieces or at least three readings per heat, with higher coverage for critical service.

For the limits of PMI, see [PMI testing on stainless steel](/knowledge-base/pmi-testing-stainless-steel).

## What Counts as Release, Hold, or Reject?

| Result | Meaning | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Release | Documents, identity, dimensions, surface, and PMI are acceptable | Move to stock with inspection record. |
| Hold | Evidence is incomplete or one gate needs clarification | Segregate physically; no cutting or use. |
| Reject | Confirmed grade, dimension, surface, or document nonconformance | Issue NCR and supplier notice. |
| Concession | Nonconformance is accepted commercially or technically | Record buyer approval before use. |

The dangerous option is informal use. Once the steel has been cut, welded, or sold forward, the evidence is weaker and the cost of correction rises.

## A Checklist People Actually Use

Keep the form short enough that the receiving team will fill it out under real dock pressure:

- PO, MTC, packing list present and matching
- heat number verified on material
- quantity and bundle condition photographed
- thickness/width/length sampled and recorded
- surface and edge condition checked
- PMI completed or marked pending
- release/hold/reject decision signed

That is enough for routine receipt. Critical orders can add AQL level, Ra readings, third-party witness reports, or lab confirmation. The routine should not become so heavy that people skip it.

## Release Shortcut

Release only what you can defend later. If the lot cannot survive document review, heat-number traceability, dimensional sampling, surface inspection, and a sensible chemistry spot-check, it should not quietly enter stock.
