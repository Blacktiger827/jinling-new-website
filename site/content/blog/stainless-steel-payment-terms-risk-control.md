# Which Payment Terms Actually Control Stainless Order Risk?

Payment terms matter, but they are rarely the first thing that protects a stainless order. The first protection is a purchase order that leaves less room for surprise.

A 30/70 split can be safe on a defined route and dangerous on a vague route. The difference is what the buyer confirms before the deposit leaves.

## What to Confirm Before Deposit

Confirm these items before paying:

- supplier legal entity and verified bank details
- exact grade, standard, thickness, tolerance, finish, and quantity
- sample or reference route for visible finishes
- MTC and document-pack expectation
- packing method and loading-photo requirement
- inspection hold point, if any
- HS code and origin route where customs risk matters

If those items are still "to be confirmed later", the payment term is doing too much work.

## Match Terms to Order Risk

| Order situation | Safer control |
|---|---|
| first order with standard stock | deposit plus balance against agreed evidence |
| visible finish or processed material | sample approval before production release |
| high-value or new supplier | inspection hold or LC discussion |
| custom route or origin-sensitive order | document review before booking |
| repeat proven route | more flexible terms can be discussed |

Open account terms usually come after the route is proven. They should not be used to compensate for weak supplier screening.

## What Should Release the Balance?

The balance should be tied to evidence, not a calendar date alone. Useful evidence includes final MTC, packing list, loading photos, inspection release if agreed, export licence reference where applicable, and B/L copy once shipped.

One extra habit is worth keeping: if bank details change after the proforma invoice, pause. Verify the change through the original contact route before any second payment leaves.

## When Stricter Terms Help

An LC, inspection hold, or staged balance release can help when the supplier is new, the shipment is high value, the product is custom, or the destination has strict customs paperwork. But stricter terms are not a cure for a vague specification.

If the PO does not define finish, tolerance, document pack, and packing evidence, even a strict payment structure may only delay the argument.

For visible or damage-sensitive material, loading photos are not decoration. They show whether the goods left in the agreed packing condition.

## PO Wording

Use wording like:

> "Deposit payable after PO, sample/document route, and bank details are confirmed. Balance payable against final MTC, packing list, loading photos, and B/L copy, with export licence reference where applicable."

That sentence will not solve every dispute, but it moves the risk conversation to the right place: before the order is released.
