# Which Thickness Tolerance Standard Is Strictest: GB, ASTM, or EN?

GB/T 3280, ASTM A480, and EN standards can all describe cold-rolled stainless sheet. They do not create the same purchase contract by default.

The safest tolerance is not always the strictest table. The safest tolerance is the one named clearly on the PO, measured in the correct width band, and tied to a minimum delivered thickness when under-gauge material creates risk.

## The Short Comparison

| Route | How it is usually used | Buyer caution |
|---|---|---|
| ASTM A480 | Common for US and international ASTM orders | Standard tolerance is often acceptable, but no-minus must be written separately. |
| EN 10088 / EN ISO route | Common for European customers | Use the correct EN document for product width and form. |
| GB/T 3280 | Common Chinese cold-rolled sheet and coil route | Normal precision may be the default unless higher precision is specified. |

The numbers often sit close together at common gauges. The document route matters because inspectors release against the standard named in the contract, not against a buyer's memory of a similar table.

## Which One Is Tightest?

At common cold-rolled gauges, EN special/precision classes and GB higher precision are usually the tightest options. ASTM A480 and normal-class routes are usually looser but still acceptable for many sheet and fabrication jobs.

| Nominal thickness | Standard class idea | Tighter class idea | When the tighter class is worth it |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0.5 mm | Normal tolerance can feel wide | Precision / higher precision | Connector strip, fine stamping, visible fit lines. |
| 1.0 mm | Normal is often workable | Precision / higher precision | Appliance panels, welded tube feedstock, tight assemblies. |
| 2.0 mm | Most standards are close | Tighter class if fit-up is sensitive | Formed panels, tanks, controlled weight. |
| 3.0 mm | Normal often sufficient | Tighter if machining or welding needs it | Laser-cut assemblies, flange fit-up. |
| 6.0 mm | Plate/sheet route must be clear | Project-specific minimum thickness | Structural, pressure, or machining allowance. |

Do not compare standards without checking width. Wider sheet normally carries a wider tolerance band.

## The Clause That Prevents Most Arguments

If actual minimum thickness matters, write a floor:

> Nominal thickness 2.0 mm. Minimum delivered thickness 1.95 mm at any measured point. Thickness tolerance per ASTM A480 unless otherwise agreed. Actual readings to be recorded on inspection report.

That clause is stronger than debating whether GB, ASTM, or EN is stricter in general. It gives the receiving inspector a pass/fail point.

## When Cross-Standard Orders Go Wrong

Three situations create most disputes:

- The supplier quotes GB/T 3280 normal precision while the end customer expects EN documentation.
- The buyer names a grade standard but forgets to name the dimensional standard.
- The material is technically within tolerance but the project needed no-minus or a tighter minimum thickness.

The word "standard" is not enough. A PO should name the material standard and the dimensional standard separately.

For more purchase language, see [stainless steel tolerance contract terms](/knowledge-base/stainless-steel-tolerance-contract-terms).

## What to Put on the PO

Use one of these patterns:

- **ASTM route:** 304 stainless sheet per ASTM A240, dimensional tolerance per ASTM A480, thickness readings recorded.
- **European route:** 316L / 1.4404 per EN 10088, dimensional tolerance per applicable EN sheet/strip standard, class stated.
- **China route:** 304 per GB/T route or dual-certified ASTM/EN route, GB/T 3280 higher precision if needed, MTC and inspection readings supplied.
- **Risk route:** add minimum delivered thickness or no-minus requirement with agreed price effect.

The last line matters because tighter tolerance is not free. It may require selection, heavier input, slower rolling control, or more rejection at the mill.

## Inspection Method

Measure more than one point. A single reading near the edge or center does not describe the whole sheet or coil.

For routine release, use a calibrated micrometer and record readings across width and along length. For dispute work, agree the measurement positions, tool calibration, and whether edge exclusion applies before arguing about numbers.

## Thickness Tolerance Shortcut

Do not ask which standard is strictest in the abstract. Ask which standard your customer will accept, which tolerance class the forming or welding process needs, and what minimum delivered thickness the design cannot go below.

Then write those answers into the PO. The table only helps after the contract language is clear.
