# Which Stainless Series Are You Really Buying?

200, 300, and 400 series are not quality levels. A 400-series grade is not automatically worse than a 300-series grade, and 201 is not simply fake 304. The series number tells you the alloy family and the tradeoff you are entering.

That matters when a quote sheet lists 201, 304, and 430 for the same panel. The cheapest line may be correct, or it may be a substitution that moves the risk from the supplier to the buyer.

## The Fast Series Map

| Series | Typical structure | Common grades | Best fit | Main caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | Austenitic with Mn/N replacing some Ni | 201, 202 | Dry indoor decorative work | Lower corrosion margin than 304 |
| 300 | Austenitic with Cr/Ni, sometimes Mo | 304, 316L, 321 | General fabrication, food, outdoor, corrosion service | Higher nickel and Mo cost |
| 400 ferritic | Magnetic, low or no nickel | 430, 409, 441 | Appliances, indoor panels, exhaust | Lower ductility and chloride margin |
| 400 martensitic | Magnetic and hardenable | 410, 420, 440C | Blades, shafts, wear parts | Lower corrosion resistance |

The first digit narrows the family. It does not approve the service.

## When 200 Series Makes Sense

200 series can be a practical cost choice for dry indoor decorative work. It keeps an austenitic structure with less nickel by using manganese and nitrogen.

Use it for controlled indoor panels, shelving, and cost-sensitive decorative parts where chloride, food contact, and long-term wet service are not part of the job. Do not let it quietly replace 304 in coastal, kitchen, humid, or chemical environments.

If the decision is really 201 versus 304, read it as a service envelope question, not a price question.

## Why 300 Series Is the Default

300 series dominates because it is forgiving. [304](/grades/304-stainless-steel) forms, welds, and finishes well. [316L](/grades/316l-stainless-steel) adds molybdenum for chloride resistance. 321 and 310S cover special heat-service routes.

This family is usually the floor for food equipment, general fabrication, outdoor architecture, tanks, pipe, and parts where a supplier cannot control every downstream fabrication detail.

## Why 400 Series Is Not One Thing

430 and 410 both sit in the 400 series, but they do very different work. [430](/grades/430-stainless-steel) is ferritic: magnetic, lower cost, good for indoor appliances and decorative surfaces. 410 and 420 are martensitic: hardenable, useful for shafts, blades, seats, and wear parts.

The magnetic response is normal. It does not mean poor quality. It means the crystal structure is ferritic or martensitic.

## Where Duplex Fits

Duplex grades such as [2205](/grades/2205-duplex-stainless-steel) sit outside the simple 200/300/400 habit. They combine austenite and ferrite, giving higher strength and better chloride SCC resistance than ordinary 300 series.

When the job is seawater, hot chloride, or pressure plus corrosion, do not force the answer into 304, 316L, or 430. Check duplex directly.

## Series Names Are Only a First Filter

Use the series number as a first filter only. Then ask the real questions: Is the service wet? Is chloride present? Will it be welded? Does it need magnetism, hardness, or deep forming?

If those questions are not answered, "200 series" and "400 series" are only cheaper words on a quotation, not safer materials in service.
