# Stainless Steel for Food Equipment: 304 vs 316L, Ra Targets, Welding, and Audit Checks

"Food grade stainless" is not a complete specification.

Food equipment works when four decisions line up: grade, surface roughness, weld quality, and traceability. If one is missing, the phrase "food grade" becomes too soft to protect the buyer, the fabricator, or the auditor.

Most projects still come down to 304 versus 316L. The right answer depends on product chemistry, cleaning chemistry, temperature, and whether the surface can be cleaned without leaving residue.

## Choose the Grade by Product and Cleaning Route

| Equipment or service | Typical grade | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Dry food contact, neutral products, tables, guards | 304 | Good hygiene baseline when chloride and acid load are low. |
| General tanks, conveyors, work surfaces | 304 | Cost-effective when cleaning chemicals are controlled. |
| Dairy, citrus, vinegar, sauces, wine | 316L | Acid and cleaning cycles need more corrosion margin. |
| Brine, seafood, salt-cured products | 316L as the starting point | Chloride exposure raises pitting risk quickly. |
| Pharmaceutical or high-purity process contact | 316L, project-specific | Surface class and validation often matter as much as grade. |
| Low-cost wet food contact | Avoid 201 | Lower corrosion margin and migration concerns make it a poor shortcut. |

304 is not a poor material. It is simply not a salt, acid, or aggressive-sanitizer solution. 316L is not luxury in those cases; it is the practical baseline.

For grade comparison, see [304 vs 316 stainless steel](/knowledge-base/304-vs-316-stainless-steel).

## Specify Ra Alongside the Finish Name

Food equipment needs cleanability. A nice-looking finish is not enough.

| Surface duty | Practical Ra target |
|---|---:|
| General food contact | ≤ 0.8 µm |
| Dairy and protein-rich wet contact | ≤ 0.4 µm |
| Pharma / biotech product contact | ≤ 0.25 µm or project requirement |

The Ra value should be measured after fabrication where possible, because welding, polishing, bending, and repair can change the final surface. A sheet certificate does not automatically describe the finished tank or tube spool.

For the surface-specific guide, read [food-grade stainless Ra requirements](/knowledge-base/surface-roughness-ra-food-equipment).

## Welding Can Undo the Right Grade

Food-contact welds fail quietly when they leave crevices, heat tint, rough roots, or uncleanable transitions. The base metal can be correct while the weld becomes the hygiene risk.

Write the weld expectation plainly:

- use L-grade base material and matching low-carbon filler where needed
- use TIG/GTAW for clean product-contact joints
- back-purge internal welds so the root does not oxidize
- remove unacceptable heat tint
- passivate after weld cleanup
- inspect the product-contact surface, not the outside bead alone

For sanitary weld execution, see [food-grade pipe welding vs industrial pipe welding](/knowledge-base/stainless-pipe-welding-food-industrial).

## What Auditors Actually Need

The mill does not certify a finished machine as NSF, 3-A, or EHEDG compliant. The equipment builder owns the finished-equipment compliance file. The raw material still has to support that file.

The useful material package includes:

- heat-traceable MTC for sheet, coil, tube, or fittings
- grade and standard named clearly
- surface finish and Ra readings where required
- weld procedure and treatment record if supplied as fabrication
- cleaning/passivation record when specified
- packaging record for protected polished surfaces

This is why the raw material order should be written for the audit file as well as incoming purchasing.

## A Better Purchase Line

Instead of:

> Food-grade 304 sheet, polished.

Use:

> 304L stainless sheet per ASTM A240, product-contact side No.4 brushed, final Ra ≤ 0.8 µm after fabrication, PVC film on visible face, heat-traceable MTC supplied.

For stricter wet service:

> 316L stainless tube/sheet per ASTM A270 or ASTM A240 as applicable, product-contact surface Ra ≤ 0.4 µm, weld tint removed and passivated, MTC and surface inspection record supplied.

That language gives the supplier something measurable and gives the auditor a trail.

## Food-Equipment Shortcut

Do not buy "food grade." Buy the conditions that make the equipment food-safe: correct alloy, cleanable surface, clean welds, and traceable documents. The grade name starts the decision. It does not finish it.
