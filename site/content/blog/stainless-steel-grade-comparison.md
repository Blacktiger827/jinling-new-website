# How Do Stainless Steel Grades Cross-Reference Across ASTM, EN, GB, JIS, and ISO?

**Short answer:** grade names can translate cleanly, but they are not proof by themselves. ASTM 304, EN 1.4301, GB 06Cr19Ni10, and JIS SUS304 point to the same commercial family. The actual order is still approved by chemistry window, suffix, product standard, certificate wording, and service condition.

Use cross-reference tables to speak the same language. Use the mill test certificate and the purchase order wording to decide whether the substitution is safe.

This page is intentionally not a full grade catalogue. It focuses on the grades that most often appear in stainless sheet, coil, plate, tube, and export sourcing work: 201, 304/304L, 316/316L, 430, and 2205 duplex.

---

## How to Use This Page

Start with three checks before reading any table:

1. **Match the exact suffix.** 304, 304L, 304H, 316, and 316L are not interchangeable just because the base number looks familiar.
2. **Check the heat against both standards.** A material can be close in trade language but still miss one limit in EN, ASTM, GB, or JIS.
3. **Tie the name to the application.** A near-equivalent grade may still be wrong for welding, chloride exposure, magnetic behavior, high temperature, or pressure service.

For document-level checks, the most useful companion article is [How to Read a Mill Test Certificate](/knowledge-base/how-to-read-mill-test-certificate).

## Core Working Map

This compact map is enough for most commercial comparisons. If the project involves pressure equipment, sour service, food contact, or a regulated design code, do not stop at the trade name.

| Buying name | Names to verify on the drawing or MTC | Use and approval note |
|---|---|---|
| 201 | UNS S20100 / EN 1.4372 / GB 12Cr17Mn6Ni5N / SUS201 | Indoor decorative and budget trim only; do not let it pass as a casual 304 substitute. |
| 304 / 304L | S30400 or S30403 / 1.4301 or 1.4307 / 06Cr19Ni10 or 022Cr19Ni10 / SUS304 or SUS304L | General fabrication, kitchen equipment, and inland architecture; check L-grade, weld route, and EN sulphur limits. |
| 316 / 316L | S31600 or S31603 / 1.4401 or 1.4404 / 06Cr17Ni12Mo2 or 022Cr17Ni12Mo2 / SUS316 or SUS316L | Chloride margin, food, and marine-adjacent work; verify Mo range, L-grade, and dual certification. |
| 430 | S43000 / EN 1.4016 / GB 10Cr17 / SUS430 | Appliances, elevator decoration, and dry interiors; confirm magnetic behavior and lower corrosion reserve are acceptable. |
| 2205 duplex | S31803 or S32205 / EN 1.4462 / GB 022Cr23Ni5Mo3N | Higher strength, tanks, brine, and chloride service; state S32205 when the tighter chemistry is required. |

The table should be read as a buying map, not as a substitute for the standard. It tells you which names belong in the same conversation; it does not prove that a specific heat meets every limit.

## Where Standards Can Diverge

Most mistakes happen in the quiet spaces: sulphur, carbon, molybdenum, nitrogen, or the exact UNS suffix. These differences are small on paper but serious when a bank, inspector, or project engineer reviews the documents.

| Check point | Where the difference appears | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 304 vs EN 1.4301 | EN can be tighter on sulphur and slightly different on chromium limits | A heat that looks like 304 in trade language still needs EN confirmation |
| 304L vs 304 | Carbon limit changes the welding risk | Welded assemblies normally need the L-grade or a documented equivalent route |
| 316L vs EN 1.4404 | Mo, Ni, sulphur, and certificate wording must all line up | "316L" on an offer is not enough for an EN drawing |
| S31803 vs S32205 | Both are sold as 2205, but S32205 has tighter chemistry | Duplex orders should state the required UNS grade clearly |

If the drawing says EN 1.4404 and the offer says ASTM 316L, the right question is not "are they similar?" The right question is "does this heat and certificate meet both sets of requirements?"

## Decision by Application

Grade equivalence only matters after the service condition is clear. This is the simpler decision path we use before going into full chemical comparison.

| Service condition | Sensible starting point | Upgrade trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Dry decorative interiors | 430 or 304 | Choose 304 when forming, non-magnetic behavior, or better corrosion reserve matters |
| General fabrication | 304 / 304L | Use 304L when welding is central to the assembly |
| Kitchen and food equipment | 304 / 304L | Move to 316L for salt, acidic cleaning, or more aggressive food media |
| Coastal or chloride exposure | 316L | Move to 2205 or higher alloy when chloride, temperature, or stress rises |
| Tanks, pressure, or brine service | 316L or 2205 | Let the design code and corrosion review decide the final grade |

For kitchen decisions, see [304 vs 430 for Kitchen Equipment](/knowledge-base/304-vs-430-kitchen-equipment). For chloride-driven upgrades, see [PRE Value Guide](/knowledge-base/pre-value-stainless-steel-selection).

## What to Put on the Purchase Order

A clean PO prevents both technical and documentary disputes. A useful line includes:

1. Trade grade and exact standard designation.
2. Product standard and delivery condition.
3. Certificate type and dual-certification requirement.
4. Any extra condition: finish, tolerance, PMI, NACE, food-contact declaration, or inspection hold point.

Example:

> 316L stainless steel plate per ASTM A240/A240M UNS S31603 and EN 10088-2 1.4404, dual-certified on one EN 10204 Type 3.1 MTC.

This wording gives the mill, trader, buyer, inspector, and bank the same strings to verify. It also avoids the common letter-of-credit problem where the material is technically acceptable but the documents do not match the PO language.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Mistake 1: Accepting a familiar grade name without the suffix.** "304" and "304L" may both appear in the same discussion, but welded equipment usually needs the lower-carbon route documented.

**Mistake 2: Treating 201 as a cheaper 304.** 201 can be useful for controlled indoor decorative work, but it should not be presented as the same corrosion-resistance class as 304.

**Mistake 3: Calling every 2205 heat equivalent.** If the project requires S32205, do not accept S31803-only paperwork without engineering approval.

**Mistake 4: Letting internal links replace the decision.** A cross-reference page should guide the buyer to the right next check, not force them through a wall of grade pages.

## FAQ

**Is ISO 15510 enough for ordering stainless steel?** No. ISO 15510 is a cross-reference and composition reference. A real order still needs the product standard, delivery condition, certificate route, and any project-specific requirements.

**Is EN 1.4307 the same as ASTM 304L?** Functionally close, but not automatically identical for procurement. Confirm the actual heat against both standards and require dual certification when both names matter.

**Does JIS SUS304 mean the same thing as ASTM 304?** It usually points to the same commercial grade family. The final approval still depends on the chemistry values, product standard, and MTC wording.

**What should I do when two standards are both required?** Put both names on the PO and require one MTC that lists both standards. This is usually cleaner than asking the supplier to explain equivalence after the shipment is finished.

## Bottom Line

Cross-reference names help teams talk faster. They do not replace verification.

For everyday purchasing, compare the buying name, exact suffix, chemistry window, and certificate route first. Only then decide whether ASTM, EN, GB, JIS, or ISO language can safely travel across the order without creating a technical or documentary dispute.
