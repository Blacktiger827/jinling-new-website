# Where Can 430 Replace 304 in Kitchen Equipment?

The 304-versus-430 question should not be asked by room name. A kitchen contains dry appliance skins, wet prep zones, food-contact surfaces, hot grease areas, splash zones, and decorative fronts. They do not need the same alloy.

430 can save money in the right dry zone. In the wrong zone, it becomes the stain people notice first.

This is why mixed-material kitchen orders need a zone map before pricing. If every part is quoted as one grade, the buyer either overpays for dry panels or under-specifies the wet parts that will get blamed later.

## The Zone Split

| Kitchen area | Better starting grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| sink bowl, drainboard, wet prep counter | 304 | water, sanitizer, and food residue are routine |
| backsplash near washdown | 304 | repeated wiping and spray |
| hood skin and grease-cleaned areas | 304 | cleaning chemistry and condensate |
| refrigerator side or outer appliance skin | 430 can work | dry service and lower corrosion demand |
| decorative front away from splash | 430 or 304 | depends on touch, cleaning, and brand level |
| coastal or chloride-heavy kitchen | 316L review | 304 may be narrow if chloride is routine |

The real question is simple: will this part stay dry most of its life?

## Where 430 Works

[430](/grades/430-stainless-steel) is ferritic, nickel-free, magnetic, and cost-effective. It can be a sensible choice for dry appliance skins, side panels, housings, and decorative areas that are not repeatedly wetted or scrubbed with strong cleaner.

It is a route choice, not a shameful downgrade, when the service is honest.

The drawing should make that honesty visible. If a dry 430 panel will sit next to a 304 wet zone, mark the service boundary so a later buyer does not reorder the wrong grade for the wrong part.

## Where 304 Should Stay

[304](/grades/304-stainless-steel) should remain the baseline for food-contact, washdown, sink, prep, splash, and wet-service areas. It carries more corrosion margin and handles daily kitchen cleaning better.

Indoor does not mean mild. Bleach residue, steam, salt, acidic food, warm water, and rough cleaning can make an indoor kitchen harsher than an outdoor decorative panel.

If a part is routinely rinsed, sprayed, soaked, or wiped with sanitizer, treat it as a wet service part even if the equipment sits indoors.

## Appliance Fronts Need Finish Logic Too

Some appliance fronts are dry enough for 430 but touched constantly. In that case, corrosion may not be the first problem. Fingerprints, scratches, and wipe marks may be.

A better line item may be:

> "430 No.4 + AFP, dry appliance front, low-chloride cleaning only."

For a wet or public-facing premium route:

> "304 No.4 or hairline + AFP, touch-zone panel, reference sample retained."

## PO Wording

Avoid a blanket line such as:

> "all kitchen equipment stainless steel"

Write by component:

> "Wet food-contact parts: 304 2B or No.4, welds cleaned and passivated."

> "Dry appliance skins: 430 No.4, dry indoor service only."
