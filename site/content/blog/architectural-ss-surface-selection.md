# How Should an Architectural Stainless Finish Be Chosen?

Architectural stainless fails in public. If the finish traps salt, reflects scratches, mismatches across panels, or needs cleaning nobody budgeted for, the building owner sees it long after the sample board has been approved.

So the finish should be chosen after five questions: where is the panel, who touches it, how is it lit, how often is it cleaned, and what environment reaches the surface?

## Choose by Zone, Not by Finish Name

| Building zone | Better starting point | Watch carefully |
|---|---|---|
| Interior wall panels | 304 No.4 or hairline | grain direction and panel matching |
| Handrails, doors, push zones | No.4 or No.4 + AFP | fingerprints, field repair, cleaning marks |
| Feature ceilings and sheltered accents | BA, mirror, PVD | glare, scratch visibility, replacement matching |
| Urban exterior facade | 304 or 316L No.4 / 2B | pollution deposits and cleaning interval |
| Coastal exterior | 316L, sometimes duplex review | chloride, sheltered deposits, tea staining |
| Sheltered soffits | smoother finish such as 2B | grain can hold dirt where rain cannot wash |

The first trap is using a finish because it looked premium on a hand sample. The second trap is treating interior and exterior as the only split. A sheltered coastal soffit and a rain-washed coastal wall are not the same service.

## What the Main Finishes Really Do

No.4 gives a satin, practical grain. It is forgiving in public interiors because light scratches can be blended with the direction of the finish.

Hairline gives a longer, calmer line. It looks more refined on large panels, but grain direction and lot matching become more important. If adjacent panels run in different directions, the wall looks wrong even when every sheet meets the finish code.

2B is visually quieter and smoother. It is not glamorous, but it can be the better choice where deposits, rain washing, or hidden surfaces matter more than decorative texture.

BA and mirror create brightness and depth. They also show every handling mark. Use them where the panel is low-touch or where maintenance is part of the owner handover.

PVD adds color. AFP adds easier cleaning. Neither one fixes the wrong substrate, the wrong grade, or a careless installation route.

## Grade Comes Before Surface Tricks

Finish affects deposit retention and visibility. Grade decides whether the alloy has enough corrosion reserve.

304 is a good interior and mild exterior grade. 316L should enter the discussion when the project is coastal, humid, industrial, pool-adjacent, or exposed to aggressive cleaners. A smoother 304 panel is still not the same as a 316L panel when chloride exposure is real.

For visible welded architectural work, specify the L-grade route where needed. Weld heat and edge exposure are where appearance problems usually begin.

## The Drawing Must Control Grain

On decorative stainless, grain direction is not a detail for the installer to guess. It should be shown on drawings and confirmed against a retained reference sample.

Write the order with:

- finish name and target visual sample
- grain direction on every visible panel
- grade and certificate requirement
- protective film type and removal window
- inspection face, viewing distance, and lighting condition
- cleaning interval and approved cleaner

That sounds like more work than writing `304 brushed stainless`, but it prevents the problems that create expensive reorders: one panel rotated, one batch slightly different, one wrong cleaner used after handover.
