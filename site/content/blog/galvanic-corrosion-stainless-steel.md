# Can Stainless Steel Touch Carbon Steel Safely?

Yes, if the joint stays dry. Be careful if it stays wet.

Stainless steel is more noble than carbon steel. When the two metals touch electrically and a conductive liquid bridges them, the carbon steel becomes the metal that corrodes faster. The stainless usually looks fine, which can make the failure confusing: the "better" metal helped consume the cheaper one.

The decision is not simply "mixed metals are bad." The decision is whether the three galvanic conditions exist.

## The Three Conditions

Galvanic corrosion needs all three:

- two different metals
- electrical contact between them
- an electrolyte such as rainwater, seawater, condensation, or process liquid

Remove one condition and the cell stops. Dry indoor contact is usually safe. Outdoor, coastal, humid, or washed-down assemblies need design care.

## The Area Ratio Is the Trap

The worst arrangement is a small piece of carbon steel attached to a large stainless surface. The carbon steel becomes a small anode feeding a large cathode, so attack concentrates on the small part.

| Joint | Typical risk |
|---|---|
| Stainless bolt in large carbon steel frame | Lower, especially if coated and drained. |
| Carbon steel bolt through stainless plate | High in wet service. |
| Carbon steel insert inside stainless pipe | High, often hidden until failure. |
| Stainless and carbon steel in dry interior | Usually low. |

That is why carbon steel fasteners in stainless assemblies fail so often near the coast.

## How to Design Around It

Use one or more of these controls:

- isolate the metals with nylon, EPDM, PTFE, or similar washers/sleeves
- coat the carbon steel side carefully
- avoid water traps at the joint
- use stainless fasteners in stainless assemblies
- avoid small anodic parts connected to large cathodic surfaces
- inspect mixed-metal joints more often in marine or washdown zones

Do not rely on paint over the stainless side to solve the problem. If the coating fails locally, it can make the area-ratio problem worse.

## When It Becomes a Complaint

If carbon steel rusts beside stainless, the supplier of the stainless is not automatically responsible. Check:

- did the supplied stainless match the order?
- was carbon steel in direct electrical contact?
- was the joint wet or exposed to chlorides?
- was isolation specified and installed?
- which metal is actually losing section?

If the stainless grade is correct and the carbon steel side is corroding, this is usually a joint-design or installation issue, not a stainless material non-conformance.

## Galvanic Shortcut

Stainless can touch carbon steel when the joint is dry or deliberately isolated. In wet service, design the joint so the small, vulnerable metal is not the one feeding the whole assembly.
