# Does the Spec Need Mechanical Polishing or Electropolishing?

Mechanical polishing and electropolishing are often discussed as if one is simply a more premium version of the other. They are different answers.

Mechanical polishing cuts and smooths the surface. Electropolishing dissolves a thin outer layer and changes the surface chemistry at the same time. The difference matters when the application is judged by hygiene, corrosion margin, or particle control rather than shine alone.

## What Each Process Does

| Question | Mechanical polishing | Electropolishing |
|---|---|---|
| Main action | abrasive smoothing | controlled surface dissolution |
| Visual result | brighter, directional | clean, softer reflection |
| Ra control | good | very good after proper prep |
| Embedded abrasive risk | possible | removed |
| Passive surface | mostly unchanged | chromium-rich surface improves |
| Cost | baseline | usually much higher |

Mechanical polishing is the right route when the buyer wants a No.4, hairline, or mirror visual surface. Electropolishing is the right route when the buyer needs a cleaner, more passive surface for process duty.

## When Mechanical Polishing Is Enough

Use mechanical polishing for:

- architectural panels
- elevator interiors
- kitchen exterior panels
- No.4 and hairline finishes
- decorative mirror sheet
- parts where appearance and roughness are the main acceptance points

If the spec is visual and the service is not hygienic or chemically demanding, electropolishing may add cost without adding useful value.

## When Electropolishing Is Worth It

Use electropolishing when the surface must release product, resist corrosion initiation, reduce particle retention, or satisfy a sanitary/process standard.

Common routes include pharmaceutical equipment, cleanroom tubing, high-purity process parts, premium food equipment, and some medical or semiconductor components.

EP does not rescue poor weld geometry or the wrong grade. It works best after the surface has already been prepared correctly. A rough hot-rolled surface cannot become a clean pharma surface by EP alone; it usually needs mechanical polish first, then EP.

## The Starting Surface Still Matters

BA or a well-controlled mechanically polished surface is a better EP starting point than a rough, inconsistent surface. Typical EP removes only a thin layer. It improves the outer skin; it does not rewrite the part geometry.

For hygienic tube or equipment, a practical route is:

- choose the correct grade, often 316L
- mechanically polish to the required baseline
- electropolish if the spec needs passive chemistry or lower retention
- verify Ra and release documents

## What to Write on the PO

If the job needs appearance:

> "304 No.4 finish, grain direction stated, visual reference sample approved."

If the job needs hygienic or high-purity release:

> "316L mechanically polished and electropolished, final Ra target stated, inspection record required."

Do not use `mirror` and `electropolished` as interchangeable words. Mirror is a visual finish. Electropolishing is a surface-chemistry process.
