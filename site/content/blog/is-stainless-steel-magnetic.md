# Why Does a Magnet Test Mislead Stainless Buyers?

A magnet can tell you something about stainless steel. It cannot certify the grade.

Annealed 304 and 316 are usually non-magnetic. Ferritic grades such as 430 are magnetic. Martensitic grades are magnetic. Duplex is partly magnetic by design. Cold-worked 304 can also attract a magnet, even when the chemistry is perfectly correct.

That is why magnet disputes are common at receiving: the test is quick, but the conclusion is often too confident.

## What Magnetism Actually Shows

| Stainless family or condition | Magnetic response | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Annealed austenitic 304 / 316 | Very low | Normal |
| Cold-worked 304 | Weak to noticeable | Strain-induced martensite, not necessarily substitution |
| Ferritic 430 | Strong | Normal ferritic structure |
| Martensitic 410 / 420 | Strong | Normal hardenable structure |
| Duplex 2205 | Moderate | Ferrite is part of the design |
| Carbon steel | Strong | Magnet alone cannot separate it from ferritic stainless |

The magnet reads structure. It does not read chromium, nickel, or molybdenum.

## Why 304 Can Become Magnetic

304 is austenitic when annealed. After bending, drawing, stamping, or heavy polishing, part of that austenite can transform into strain-induced martensite. Martensite is magnetic.

So a formed 304 bracket that attracts a magnet may still be real 304. The right check is PMI or the heat-numbered MTC, not a stronger argument over magnet pull.

## What a Magnet Cannot Prove

A non-magnetic sheet is not automatically 304. 201 and 202 can also be non-magnetic when annealed, and they carry different corrosion resistance.

A magnetic sheet is not automatically carbon steel. It may be 430, 409, 410, duplex, or cold-worked 304.

That is the trap: the magnet can rule some things in or out, but it cannot approve a grade substitution.

## When Magnetism Is Actually Useful

Use a magnet as a quick screen:

- Strong pull on supposed annealed 304 sheet: investigate.
- Slight pull on formed 304 parts: normal enough to verify, not reject blindly.
- No pull on a supposed 430 order: investigate.
- Any grade dispute with money or corrosion risk: move to PMI.

PMI reads elements directly. For 304, look for chromium and nickel. For 316L, molybdenum must be present. For 201, nickel will be lower and manganese higher.

## Let the Magnet Start the Question

Use the magnet as a first filter, not the final word. It is good at starting a question and bad at closing one.

When grade, price, or corrosion risk matters, confirm the heat by [PMI testing](/knowledge-base/pmi-testing-stainless-steel) or by matching the MTC to the delivered bundle. Magnetism belongs in receiving inspection, not in place of chemistry.
