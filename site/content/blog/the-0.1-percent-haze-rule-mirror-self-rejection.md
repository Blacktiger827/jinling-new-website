# How We Catch Mirror Haze Before a Sheet Leaves the Line

A mirror sheet can pass the instruments and still fail the eye.

That is the uncomfortable truth of 8K mirror work. A roughness meter reads a tiny area. A gloss meter reads one angle. But a lobby wall, elevator door, or display panel is seen as a whole surface. A small cloudy patch can ruin the way the panel feels in place.

So we use a simple internal rule: if visible haze crosses our fixed raking-light rejection line, the sheet is pulled.

## What Haze Looks Like

Haze is not a scratch. It is not a pinhole. It is the soft, milky character that makes a reflection lose its clean edge, like a window that has not quite cleared after mist.

Under soft warehouse light, it may barely show. Under raking light, it appears fast. That is why the last inspection station is more than a meter. It is a trained inspector, a fixed light, and a surface viewed as a whole.

## Why the Internal Wall Exists

The haze threshold is not a public standard. It is an internal wall.

It does three useful things:

- gives operators a hard stop instead of a mood-based judgement
- keeps different shifts from drifting into different quality levels
- protects lot-to-lot consistency when sheets from different days sit together on one project

The rule is not there because we enjoy rejecting sheets. It is there because "looks acceptable" becomes too soft when mirror panels are installed side by side.

## The QC Route Before That Final Call

Before raking-light review, a mirror sheet has already passed:

| Stage | What it checks |
|---|---|
| incoming substrate review | base coil cleanliness, visible defects, chemistry risk |
| polishing control | line speed, pressure, compound, water and residue control |
| roughness / gloss sampling | measurable surface consistency |
| cutting and handling check | damage introduced after polishing |
| final raking-light review | haze, pinholes, residual lines, film marks |

The final human inspection does not replace instruments. It catches what the instruments are too small or too narrow to see.

## What Buyers Should Ask a Mirror Supplier

Ask three questions:

- What is your haze rejection rule?
- Do you inspect every sheet under raking light or only sample?
- Do you keep a retained sample or release photo for the lot?

If the answer is only "we follow customer requirements," the line probably does not have a fixed internal wall. That does not guarantee bad material. It does mean the lot may depend too much on who happened to inspect that day.

## Haze Shortcut

Mirror quality needs numbers and eyes. Ra and gloss matter, but the installed surface still has to look clean under real light. A named haze threshold gives the factory a habit, not a promise alone.
