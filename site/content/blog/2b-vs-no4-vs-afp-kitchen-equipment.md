# Which Stainless Finish Belongs on Each Kitchen Equipment Zone?

A kitchen project rarely needs one stainless finish everywhere. Food-contact liners, worktops, splash zones, appliance fronts, and decorative panels all live different lives.

The cleanest spec splits the finish by zone.

## The Zone Map

| Kitchen zone | Better finish | Why |
|---|---|---|
| food-contact liner | 2B | smooth, simple to clean, fabrication-friendly |
| hidden internal panel | 2B | appearance is secondary |
| worktop or prep deck | No.4 | scratches blend into the grain |
| splashback and hood skin | No.4 | visible but repeatedly wiped |
| appliance front | No.4 + AFP | fingerprints become the main complaint |
| premium customer-facing panel | hairline, PVD, or AFP route | appearance-led, but still service-bound |

The mistake is specifying `304 brushed stainless` across the whole project. It sounds clean, but it often gives one zone the wrong surface.

## Where 2B Works Best

2B is not glamorous. That is often the point. It is a controlled mill finish with a smooth surface and no decorative grain. It belongs where hygiene, forming, welding, or hidden service matters more than showroom appearance.

Use 2B for liners, channels, internal surfaces, and parts where the finish will be formed, welded, or covered.

If the project needs a measured hygienic limit, state the Ra target. A finish name alone is not a sanitary release rule.

## Where No.4 Works Best

No.4 is the kitchen workhorse. It hides the normal marks that happen around prep, wipe-down, trolleys, trays, and daily handling.

Use No.4 on visible worktops, splash zones, hood canopies, service counters, and exposed equipment skins. It is not always smoother than 2B, but it often looks better after real use.

## Where AFP Works Best

AFP belongs on touch-heavy exterior panels, not direct food-contact work surfaces.

Use it for refrigerator fronts, oven fronts, dishwasher fronts, control-adjacent panels, and appliance cladding where fingerprints are the complaint. It should sit over the right base finish, usually No.4, hairline, or mirror.

Do not use AFP as a shortcut for abrasion resistance. Cutting, scrubbing, and direct food prep belong on uncoated stainless.

## PO Wording

Write the finish by component:

> "Food-contact liner: 304 2B, Ra target if required."

> "Worktop and splashback: 304 No.4, grain direction stated."

> "Appliance front: 304 or 430 No.4 + AFP, reference sample retained."

That format lets the material match the real cleaning and touch pattern.
