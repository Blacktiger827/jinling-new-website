# Food-Grade Stainless Ra: What Surface Roughness Should You Specify?

For food equipment, Ra is not a decoration number. It is a cleanability number.

A surface can be the correct grade, pass a visual check, and still hold product residue in microscopic valleys. That is why food-contact specifications should state a measured Ra limit, rather than relying on a finish name such as 2B, BA, or No.4.

The usual baseline is simple: **Ra ≤ 0.8 µm for general food contact, ≤ 0.4 µm for wet dairy or stricter hygienic zones, and ≤ 0.25 µm for many pharma/biotech surfaces.**

## Start With the Product Contact Duty

| Service surface | Practical Ra target | Why it matters |
|---|---:|---|
| Dry food contact, guards, non-product splash | ≤ 1.0 µm | Easy to clean, lower hygiene load. |
| General wet food contact | ≤ 0.8 µm | Common baseline for tanks, tables, and conveyors. |
| Dairy, protein-rich, frequent CIP | ≤ 0.4 µm | Reduces residue retention in wet service. |
| Pharma / biotech product contact | ≤ 0.25 µm or project-specific | Often tied to ASME BPE surface classes and validation. |
| Welded product-contact zones | Same as surrounding surface | The weld should not become the dirtiest part of the system. |

The number should follow the hygiene risk. A dry bakery table and a dairy CIP line should not carry the same surface requirement simply because both are "food grade."

## Finish Name Is Not Enough

2B, BA, No.4, and electropolished surfaces can all appear in hygienic equipment, but the finish name does not guarantee one exact Ra.

| Finish wording | What it tells you | What is still missing |
|---|---|---|
| 2B | Mill finish, generally smooth | Actual Ra and scratch condition. |
| BA | Bright annealed surface | Ra range, gloss, and handling marks. |
| No.4 brushed | Directional polished appearance | Grit, grain consistency, and measured Ra. |
| Electropolished | Surface peaks chemically reduced | Pre-polish condition and final Ra verification. |

For food-contact work, write both: finish + Ra limit. For example: "No.4 brushed, product-contact surface Ra ≤ 0.8 µm, measured after fabrication."

## Measure the Surface After the Work That Can Damage It

Ra measured on incoming sheet is useful, but it may not represent the final equipment. Forming, welding, grinding, polishing, cleaning, and transport can all change the surface.

Measure where the risk sits:

- product-contact faces after polishing
- weld toe and heat-affected zone after treatment
- corners, bends, drain points, and low-flow areas
- repaired areas after grinding or blending
- a minimum agreed number of points per tank, spool, or batch

The measurement method should state the instrument, cut-off, direction, and sampling points. If the inspector and supplier measure different directions across a brushed surface, they may get different numbers and both think they are right.

## The Weld Zone Cannot Be Exempt

Many hygienic failures start at the weld, not the parent sheet. A polished sheet at Ra 0.4 µm does not help if the weld root has tint, undercut, sugar, or rough blending marks.

For food and dairy work, the weld release should include:

- full penetration where the design requires it
- no crevice, undercut, or unblended rough edge on the product side
- heat tint removed or accepted only to the specified color level
- passivation after weld cleanup
- Ra check at representative weld zones when hygiene risk is high

For the broader material decision, pair this with [stainless steel for food equipment](/knowledge-base/stainless-steel-food-equipment).

## A Cleaner Specification Line

Instead of writing:

> Food-grade 304, polished finish.

Write:

> 304L stainless product-contact surfaces, No.4 brushed or equivalent, final Ra ≤ 0.8 µm measured after fabrication, weld zones blended and passivated, MTC and surface inspection record supplied.

For dairy or stricter wet-contact work:

> 316L stainless product-contact surfaces, Ra ≤ 0.4 µm after fabrication, weld heat tint removed, passivated, representative weld-zone Ra readings recorded.

Those lines are not longer for decoration. They remove the ambiguity that causes hygiene and audit disputes.

## Ra Shortcut

Use Ra to define cleanability, not prestige. Choose the grade for corrosion, choose the finish for fabrication and appearance, and choose the Ra limit for hygiene. When the equipment will be welded, cleaned, and audited, the final measured surface is the only surface that matters.
