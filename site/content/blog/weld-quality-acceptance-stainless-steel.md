# How Do I Accept or Reject a Stainless Steel Weld?

A stainless weld is accepted when the service code, visual condition, corrosion condition, and paperwork all agree.

A smooth bead helps, but appearance is not enough. A weld can look tidy and still be unacceptable because the filler was wrong, the root was oxidized, the welder qualification was missing, or the heat tint was never removed for a chloride or food-contact service.

Start with the duty of the weld. Then inspect the joint.

## First, Name the Governing Standard

| Service | Common acceptance route |
|---|---|
| Structural stainless fabrication | AWS D1.6 or EN ISO 5817 quality level |
| Pressure vessel | ASME Section VIII with ASME Section IX qualification |
| Process piping | ASME B31.3 with ASME Section IX qualification |
| Food and dairy equipment | AWS D18.1 plus hygienic project requirements |
| Pharma / biotech | ASME BPE plus project validation requirements |

"Inspect all welds" is not a release criterion. The PO or inspection plan should state the code, quality level, NDT scope, and documents required.

## Defects That Should Stop Release

Some weld findings are not negotiation items:

- crack, crater crack, or visible tear
- incomplete fusion
- incomplete penetration where full penetration was required
- burn-through or heavy root oxidation on product-contact surfaces
- undercut beyond the specified code limit
- visible slag or contamination
- wrong filler, missing filler traceability, or unexplained bead color mismatch

For sanitary, marine, chemical, and chloride service, add unacceptable heat tint to that list unless the treatment and verification record is clear.

## Heat Tint Is a Corrosion Signal

Heat tint is a corrosion warning, not a color issue alone. It shows that the weld zone was oxidized and the surface chemistry was changed.

| Tint condition | Typical action |
|---|---|
| Bright / silver | Usually acceptable after normal cleaning. |
| Light straw / gold | Often acceptable for general work; passivation may be specified. |
| Brown / bronze | Treat before chloride, food, or visible architectural service. |
| Blue / dark blue | Reject for hygienic, marine, or corrosion-critical service unless removed and verified. |
| Black scale / sugar | Reject; rework and inspect. |

For more detail, see [heat tint and post-weld oxide scale](/knowledge-base/heat-tint-oxide-scale-stainless-steel).

## NDT Should Follow Service Risk

Not every weld needs the same inspection burden. The inspection scope should match what failure would cost.

| Risk level | Sensible inspection |
|---|---|
| General non-pressure fabrication | 100% visual, sampled PT if specified. |
| Pressure or process piping | Visual plus PT/RT/UT per code and joint class. |
| Food-contact welds | Visual on product side, tint control, PT or borescope where specified. |
| Duplex stainless welds | PT, filler verification, ferrite control where required. |
| Repair welds after rejection | Re-inspect the repaired area with the same or stricter method. |

Sampling is fine when the service allows it. It is not fine when the defect mode is local and release-critical.

## Documents Are Part of the Weld

The document pack is not clerical decoration. It proves the weld was made under a qualified route.

| Document | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| WPS | Defines process, filler, joint, heat input, gas, and essential variables. |
| PQR | Proves the procedure was tested before production use. |
| Welder qualification | Confirms the welder was qualified for the process and position. |
| Base metal and filler MTC | Confirms both sides of the weld chemistry story. |
| Post-weld treatment record | Shows pickling, passivation, or polishing was completed when required. |
| NDT report | Records method, scope, acceptance class, and result. |

A clean bead with no WPS, no filler MTC, and no treatment record is not fully released. It is only visually pleasing.

## A Practical Acceptance Sequence

Use this order:

1. Confirm the applicable code and service risk.
2. Match base metal, filler, and heat numbers.
3. Check WPS, PQR, and welder qualification.
4. Inspect visual defects and heat tint.
5. Apply NDT scope.
6. Confirm post-weld cleanup and passivation where required.
7. Close the release report before shipment.

This sequence prevents the common late-stage problem: arguing over a weld after the part is already installed, when repair is more expensive and evidence is weaker.

## Weld Acceptance Shortcut

A stainless weld is not accepted because it looks smooth. It is accepted because it meets the code, fits the service, carries the right chemistry, and has a document trail that survives review.
