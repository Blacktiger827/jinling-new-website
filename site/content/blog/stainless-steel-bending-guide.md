# What Bend Radius Keeps Stainless From Fighting Back?

Stainless bending is not a single thickness rule. Grade, rolling direction, temper, springback, and edge condition all change the safe radius. Copying a mild-steel setup onto stainless is how good sheet turns into cracked parts.

For annealed 304 and 316L, 1t is a common starting point when the bend line runs across the rolling direction. Duplex 2205 needs more room. Harder temper or parallel-to-grain bending needs more again.

## Start With a Realistic Radius

| Grade, annealed sheet | Bend across rolling direction | Bend parallel to rolling direction |
|---|---:|---:|
| 304 / 316L | 1t | 2t |
| 201 | 1.5t | 2.5t |
| 430 | 1t to 1.5t | 2t |
| 2205 duplex | 2t | 3t |

These are starting radii, not promises. If the coil is skin-passed, 1/4 hard, laser-hardened at the edge, or previously formed, open the radius and test before production.

## Direction Is Not a Detail

The rolling direction runs along the coil length. Bends are safer when the bend line crosses that direction. When the bend line runs parallel to the rolling direction, the sheet cracks more easily because the outer fiber opens along the worked structure.

For tight-radius work, ask the supplier to mark rolling direction on the protective film. It is a small shop-floor detail that prevents many "bad material" arguments.

## Springback Needs Its Own Setup

Austenitic stainless springs back more than mild steel. 304 and 316L may need 10-15 degrees more compensation, depending on thickness and tooling. Duplex can spring back even more because its yield strength is higher.

Do not confuse springback with cracking. Overbending solves angle recovery. It does not solve a radius that is too tight, a hard temper, or a bad edge.

## Edge Condition Can Decide the Batch

Cracks often start at the edge, not the middle of the bend. Sheared and slit edges can carry burrs and work-hardened zones. Laser-cut edges can carry a hardened recast band. Either can become the crack start under tensile strain.

Deburr tight-radius parts before bending, especially ferritic 430 and any part bent near the minimum radius. Burr removal often buys more yield than another round of press-brake guessing.

## Press-Brake Checks Before the Run

- Confirm grade and temper on the MTC.
- Mark rolling direction before blanking.
- Set radius from grade plus direction, not thickness alone.
- Check edge condition and deburr where needed.
- Run a scrap test before committing the batch.
- Record the springback compensation that actually worked.

## Control the Four Variables

If a stainless part cracks on the brake, do not start by blaming the steel. Start with the four controllable variables: radius, direction, temper, and edge.
