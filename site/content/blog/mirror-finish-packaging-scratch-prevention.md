# How Should Mirror Stainless Be Packed So It Arrives Clean?

Mirror stainless is often lost after polishing, not during polishing. The sheet leaves the line clean, then arrives with sliding marks, edge bruising, film imprint, wet-pack staining, or handling scratches.

That is a packaging failure. Mirror needs a pack that stops movement, protects edges, and preserves evidence through unloading.

## What Damages Mirror in Transit

| Damage mode | Where it happens | What prevents it |
|---|---|---|
| face-to-face sliding | sea freight vibration, truck movement | interleaf or anti-slip separation |
| edge crush | forklift and warehouse handling | steel angle or rigid edge protection |
| film pressure marks | tight stacking or poor film choice | correct film and controlled stacking pressure |
| wet pack staining | humid route or condensation | moisture barrier and dry packing |
| mixed-lot confusion | consolidation or site storage | clear labels and lot separation |

Film is necessary, but it is not the whole system. Film protects the face from light contact. It cannot stop a stack from sliding, a corner from taking impact, or condensation from sitting inside a closed pack.

## What a Serious Mirror Pack Includes

A visible-finish export pack should usually include:

- full-face protective film matched to the next process
- interleaf paper or anti-slip separation between sheets
- sacrificial cover sheets where the pack needs a buffer
- rigid bottom support
- edge and corner protection
- controlled strapping pressure
- lot labels for adjacent-install panels
- removal-window note for film, especially on AFP-coated panels or hot-climate routes
- loading photos before the container closes

None of this is exotic. It is simply the difference between industrial sheet packing and mirror packing.

## Why Evidence Matters

Mirror claims often fail because inspection happens too late. Once the pack is opened, sheets are moved, film is removed, or panels enter fabrication, the evidence chain becomes weak.

The buyer should photograph:

- the unopened pack at unloading
- any crate, strap, or corner damage
- the film condition before removal
- the first sheet separation under raking light
- the lot label before sheets are mixed on site

If scratches are visible through intact film, the supplier has a different problem than if scratches appear after film removal and site handling.

## PO Wording

Use clear lines:

> "304 No.8 mirror, full-face protective film matched to next process, interleaf separation, rigid edge and corner protection."

> "Loading photos required before container close. Visible-panel lots to be marked and kept together. Film removal window to be stated."

> "Pack designed for long-haul ocean shipment and site unloading."

That wording gives receiving inspection something to compare against.
