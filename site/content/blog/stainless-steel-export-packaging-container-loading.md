# What Should Be Proven Before a Stainless Container Leaves?

Export packaging is part of stainless quality. A sheet that was perfect at inspection can still arrive with edge crush, water stains, strap marks, or mixed-lot confusion if the pack was treated as an afterthought.

The buyer should specify the packing system before shipment, not debate it after unloading.

## Match the Pack to the Risk

| Product route | Main transit risk | Packing focus |
|---|---|---|
| mirror, BA, AFP, decorative sheet | face marks and film imprint | film, interleaf, face protection |
| cut-to-length sheet | top/bottom sheet damage | reversed face-in sheets and edge protection |
| coil | edge crush and moisture | coil eye support, VCI, waterproof wrap |
| tube and pipe | end damage and bundle rubbing | end protection, bundle separation |
| bar and angle | impact and mixed lengths | rigid bundling and clear labels |
| mixed container | cross-damage | loading plan and segregation |

The pack is more than plastic. It is film, interleaf, moisture control, wood or steel support, labels, strapping, loading order, and photographic evidence.

## Finish Decides the Protection

A hot-rolled plate can tolerate handling that would ruin mirror sheet. A sanitary tube may arrive structurally fine but fail the buyer's receiving check if the ends are dirty or unprotected. AFP-coated panels may need a different film from bare No.4.

That is why a generic line like `standard export packing` is weak. The finish and product form should decide the pack.

## What Loading Photos Should Show

Ask for photos of:

- labels and heat or lot marks
- pack condition before loading
- edge and corner protection
- moisture barrier or VCI where used
- container floor and wall condition
- final blocking, bracing, and door-close view

Photos do not prevent all damage. They keep the evidence chain alive when a claim has to be sorted honestly.

## When Wood Packaging Matters

If wooden pallets or crates are used, confirm destination requirements before shipment. When the destination requires ISPM 15, the wood needs to be treated and marked before packing. Do not assume this can be fixed after the container has arrived.

For LCL shipments, packing has to be stronger because the cargo will be handled more often and may sit beside unknown goods. For FCL, the loading plan matters more because the entire container movement is under one route.

## PO Wording

Use wording like:

> "Export pack to include finish-matched film, moisture protection, edge/corner protection, clear lot labels, and loading photos before container close. Wood packaging to meet destination requirements where applicable."
