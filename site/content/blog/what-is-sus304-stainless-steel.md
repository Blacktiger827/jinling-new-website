# Is SUS304 the Same as 304?

Yes, for most buying and engineering purposes. SUS304 is the Japanese JIS name for the same 18-8 austenitic stainless that ASTM calls 304 and EN calls 1.4301.

The alloy is usually not the problem. The paperwork can be. A drawing may say SUS304, a Chinese mill may quote ASTM 304, and a European customer may ask for EN 1.4301. Those names point to the same family, but the certificate still needs to match the project.

## What SUS Means

SUS is the JIS prefix for stainless steel. The familiar numbers map closely to the old AISI/ASTM grade names:

| JIS name | Common ASTM name | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| SUS304 | 304 / S30400 | General 18-8 stainless |
| SUS304L | 304L / S30403 | Low-carbon welded service |
| SUS316L | 316L / S31603 | Chloride and welded corrosion service |
| SUS430 | 430 / S43000 | Ferritic indoor and appliance work |

There is no hidden second alloy behind the SUS prefix. It is a standards language.

## What Still Needs Checking

SUS304 and ASTM 304 are very close, but cross-certification should still be checked on the MTC:

- Product standard: JIS G4304 for hot-rolled product, JIS G4305 for cold-rolled product.
- Silicon: JIS permits up to 1.00%, while ASTM 304 caps silicon at 0.75%.
- Nitrogen reporting: ASTM gives a nitrogen limit; JIS may not list it the same way.
- Finish wording: 2B, BA, No.4, and No.8 are familiar, but the PO should match the customer's naming habit.

These are not usually service-life issues. They are release-document issues.

## When SUS304 Is Not the Right Answer

If the part will be welded and then placed in corrosive service, ask whether SUS304L is required. Low carbon is the important change, not the regional name.

If the part sees chloride, coastal air, or aggressive cleaners, ask whether [316L](/grades/316l-stainless-steel) is needed. SUS304 and ASTM 304 share the same limitation: neither has molybdenum.

If the buyer needs a European dossier, ask whether EN 1.4301 or 1.4307 must appear on the same certificate.

## Match the Name to the Dossier

SUS304 is usually a naming issue, not a material mystery. Treat it as JIS 304, then make the PO and MTC speak the same language.

If the grade, product form, finish, and certificate all line up, SUS304 and 304 can move through the same supply route without drama.
