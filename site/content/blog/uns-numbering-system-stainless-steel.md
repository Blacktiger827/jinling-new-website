# Why Does the UNS Number Matter More Than the Trade Name?

"304 stainless" is useful shop language. It is not enough for a controlled purchase order.

UNS, the Unified Numbering System, gives each alloy a clearer identity. For stainless steel, the number starts with S and then five digits. S30400 means standard 304. S30403 means 304L. S31603 means 316L. S32205 is a common 2205 duplex route.

The difference may look small. In a weld procedure, pressure-vessel file, or export certificate, it can decide whether the material is acceptable.

## The Last Digits Are Often the Risk

The first part of the number usually points to the familiar grade family. The last digits often separate variants.

| Trade name | UNS | What the number clarifies |
|---|---|---|
| 304 | S30400 | standard 304 chemistry |
| 304L | S30403 | low carbon variant for welding |
| 304H | S30409 | higher carbon route for elevated-temperature work |
| 316L | S31603 | low carbon molybdenum-bearing grade |
| 2205 duplex | S31803 or S32205 | not always the same chemistry window |

That is why a PO saying only "304" leaves space for misunderstanding. The supplier may ship a conforming 304 family material, while the project actually needed 304L, 304H, or a dual-certified heat.

## UNS Alone Is Still Not the Full Specification

The UNS number identifies the alloy chemistry. It does not define product form, thickness tolerance, surface finish, test route, or certificate requirement.

Use the UNS number together with the product standard:

> "ASTM A240 / ASME SA-240 UNS S31603, EN 10204 Type 3.1 MTC."

That sentence tells the mill: plate or sheet route, ASME acceptance if required, alloy identity, and certificate type. "316L stainless" does not do that.

For pipe, bar, forgings, and fittings, the standard changes. The UNS number may stay the same, but the product-form requirement does not.

## Where UNS Prevents Real Mistakes

UNS helps most when names travel across systems.

EN 1.4307 and ASTM A240 UNS S30403 are close enough that buyers often treat them as equivalents. They are not automatically the same certificate route. If the order requires ASTM A240 S30403, an EN-only MTC may need dual certification before it can be accepted.

Duplex is another common trap. Many people say "2205" as if it were one exact thing. In controlled orders, S31803 and S32205 should not be treated casually. If the project specification names S32205, the certificate should show S32205 or an explicitly accepted dual designation.

UNS does not make the material better. It makes substitution visible.

## How to Check the MTC

When the certificate arrives, check these lines before releasing the lot:

- grade name and UNS number agree with the PO
- product standard matches the ordered form
- heat number appears on both material and certificate
- chemistry falls inside the listed UNS range
- dual certification is explicit if the order crosses ASTM, ASME, EN, or JIS systems

If the MTC says only "304 stainless steel" with no UNS number or product standard, it is too vague for serious procurement. Ask for the corrected certificate before the material is cut.

## What Should the PO Say?

Write the familiar grade for readability, then write the exact identity for release:

> "316L stainless steel, ASTM A240 / ASME SA-240 UNS S31603, 2B finish, EN 10204 Type 3.1 MTC."

That line is not fancy. It prevents the wrong 316, the wrong product standard, and the wrong certificate from hiding behind a trade name.
