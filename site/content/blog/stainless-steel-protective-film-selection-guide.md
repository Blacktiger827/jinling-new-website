# How to Choose Protective Film for Stainless Steel

When you order [stainless steel sheets](/products/stainless-steel-sheet), think about when the protective film will come off. Will it be removed on arrival, left on during laser cutting, or kept in place through bending and installation? That decision gives you a useful starting point for choosing the film.

For material that will be laser cut with the film attached, the choice depends on the sheet finish, steel thickness, cutting process and later forming operations. The film needs to stay in place during processing and peel away without spoiling the surface you paid for.

This guide compares five options available through Jinling: Novacel 4236, Novacel 4228, Novacel 4318, Nitto 511M10P and POLIFILM PF584 (100). The comparison focuses on published laser-film grades and explains where each belongs on a buyer's shortlist.

|---|---|---|---|
| Novacel 4236 | 60 µm | High | The 4236RET variant is listed for protection of the sheet underside |
| Novacel 4228 REF | 100 µm | Very High | Broad stainless steel finish coverage for fibre and CO₂ laser cutting |
| Novacel 4318 | 75 µm | Medium | The 4318REM variant is listed for glossy finishes on sheets below 1 mm |
| Nitto FIBERGUARD 511M10P Light | 100 µm total thickness | Medium | Stainless steel laser cutting up to 2 mm; check the heavy-forming restriction |
| POLIFILM PF584 C S | 80 and 100 µm versions | High | Broad stainless steel finish coverage for fibre and CO₂ laser cutting |

A quotation may shorten a reference to “4228” or “PF584 (100).” Match it to the complete supplied product code before ordering. In particular, the underside and thin-sheet applications above refer to **4236RET** and **4318REM** in the Novacel laser-film range; the POLIFILM data refer to **PF584 C S**.

Thickness and adhesion labels help identify a product. They do not provide a shared performance ranking across brands: Nitto reports total thickness, and “High” and “Medium” are each manufacturer's categories.

## Which Film Should You Choose?

### For thin, glossy stainless steel sheets

**Start by considering Novacel 4318REM when the sheet is below 1 mm.** Novacel specifically lists that variant for glossy finishes on thin sheets.

For example, a 0.8 mm glossy panel intended for laser cutting fits that published application description. Film removal deserves attention on such a thin part: POLIFILM also identifies unwanted bending during peeling as a concern in thin-sheet applications. Include peeling in the trial, alongside the cut itself.

### For laser cutting across several surface finishes

**Novacel 4228 REF and POLIFILM PF584 C S are useful candidates for a broader finish range.** Both manufacturers list broad stainless steel finish coverage. This makes them relevant options when your orders include different finishes, rather than one thin, glossy product.

The published information gives you a reason to shortlist both. To choose between them, compare cutting, adhesion and peeling on your own material. The available data do not establish which brand will perform better on a particular production line.

### For protection on the underside of the sheet

**Consider Novacel 4236RET for the bottom face.** This is a specific application identified by Novacel, and a more useful selection clue than the 60 µm thickness alone.

Specify the film on each face separately. A decorative front face and the underside resting on the cutting bed serve different roles; the purchase order should make clear which film goes where.

### For stainless steel laser cutting up to 2 mm

**Nitto 511M10P Light is a candidate within that published thickness range.** Its technical sheet covers both fibre and CO₂ laser cutting. Two details affect the buying decision: Nitto advises against heavy forming because of possible visual imprints, and recommends waiting one week after lamination to reach ideal adhesion for laser cutting.

That waiting period matters if your fabricator plans to laminate and cut immediately. Discuss the production schedule before choosing the film. These details are specific to the 511M10P technical sheet.

## How Does the Surface Finish Change the Choice?

The finished appearance should guide what you inspect during a film trial.

For [8K mirror stainless steel](/surfaces/stainless-steel-8k-mirror-finish), examine the exposed surface for haze, scratches, adhesive traces and pressure marks. Use consistent lighting so small changes are easier to see.

For [No.4 brushed stainless steel](/surfaces/stainless-steel-no4-brushed-finish) and [hairline stainless steel](/surfaces/stainless-steel-hairline-finish), use a sample with the finish and grain direction you intend to buy. Check that the film stays attached through processing and that the surface appearance remains acceptable after removal.

For [AFP-coated stainless steel](/surfaces/stainless-steel-afp-finish), include the actual coating in the trial. A manufacturer's broad stainless steel finish description does not settle compatibility with every coating system. Inspect both film adhesion and the coating after peeling.

## Will the Film Suit Your Cutting and Forming Process?

Tell the supplier whether you use fibre or CO₂ laser cutting, the steel thickness, and what happens after cutting. A panel that is cut and lightly folded presents a different selection question from a part that will be deeply drawn.

If heavy forming or deep drawing is involved, select for that operation as well. Nitto's restriction above is one concrete example; Novacel also offers a separate deep-drawing film range. Laser compatibility alone does not answer the forming question.

If you intend to peel the film before cutting, explain that too. Your selection can then focus on the handling, storage and removal stages the film will actually experience.

## Is a Thicker or Stronger-Adhesion Film Always Better?

A 100 µm film is not automatically the right upgrade from a 60 or 75 µm option. In this comparison, the products have different application descriptions: 4236RET is identified for underside protection, while 4318REM is identified for thin, glossy sheets.

Adhesion also serves a purpose. The film must resist lifting during the intended operation, while remaining suitable for removal from the finished part. Choose against those two outcomes rather than treating the strongest adhesion label as the winner.

For purchasing, compare the price of a defined film application. Include any effect on processing schedules, peeling and surface inspection. A lower film price offers little saving if the chosen combination creates extra work on the part.

## How Long Can You Leave the Film On?

Give the supplier a realistic timeline: application, processing, transport, storage and final removal. Mention sunlight or outdoor storage separately.

Keep three periods distinct: the storage life of an unused roll, the time the film stays on the sheet, and its permitted outdoor exposure. A statement about one does not establish the others.

For example, POLIFILM lists six-month UV resistance for PF584 C S with a Western European climate qualification. Nitto reports an accelerated weatherability test on BA steel in hours. Those are different measures; neither should be converted into a universal peeling deadline. Use the conditions for the supplied grade and your planned route.

## Test One Representative Sheet Before a Large Order

A useful trial follows the part through the operations that matter:

1. **Apply the proposed film to the intended finish.** Look for contamination, trapped air, wrinkles and lifted edges.
2. **Run the relevant cutting and forming operations.** Include small holes, corners or folds that are important to the part.
3. **Peel and inspect.** Check tearing, adhesive traces, pressure marks and changes in surface appearance. If removal will be delayed in production, include that delay in the trial plan.
4. **Keep the reference.** Record the film code, sheet finish, process settings and photographs so future orders can be compared with the approved sample.

This turns a general claim such as “good for stainless steel” into evidence for the material and process you are buying.

## What Should You Send with Your Enquiry?

You can make the first conversation useful with six details:

- Stainless steel grade, sheet thickness and size.
- Surface finish and any coating.
- The face or faces needing protection.
- Laser type and subsequent forming operations.
- Expected storage, outdoor exposure and removal timing.
- Any required film brand or complete product code.

Once a sample is approved, record the selected film and thickness for each face on the order. Include the sample reference and how any proposed substitution will be approved.
