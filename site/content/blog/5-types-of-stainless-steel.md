# Which Stainless Family Should You Start With?

Most grade mistakes begin before the grade comparison. The wrong family gets chosen, then everyone tries to make the grade fit a job it was never built for.

There are five stainless families. The names sound academic, but the buying logic is practical: formability, magnetism, corrosion margin, hardness, strength, and welding difficulty all move with the family.

## The Five-Family Screen

| Family | Common grades | Best for | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Austenitic | 304, 316L, 321, 310S | General fabrication, food, forming, welding | Severe chloride strength or hardness is the main need |
| Ferritic | 430, 409, 441 | Indoor decorative, appliances, exhaust, magnetic parts | Deep drawing, cryogenic, severe chloride |
| Duplex | 2205, 2507 | Chloride plus strength, marine, chemical, pressure | Loose welding control or deep forming |
| Martensitic | 410, 420, 440C | Hardness, blades, shafts, wear parts | Corrosion-first service |
| PH | 17-4PH, 15-5PH | High-strength machined parts | Commodity sheet or simple corrosion service |

This is the first filter. After the family is right, the exact grade becomes easier.

## Austenitic: The Default Workhorse

Austenitic stainless is what most people picture first. [304](/grades/304-stainless-steel) and [316L](/grades/316l-stainless-steel) form well, weld well, and stay tough at low temperature. They are usually non-magnetic when annealed.

Start here for food equipment, tanks, sheet fabrication, architectural panels, general pipe, and most corrosion-service parts. Move away only when cost, magnetism, hardness, or chloride severity gives you a clear reason.

## Ferritic: Lower Cost, Magnetic, More Limited

Ferritic grades such as [430](/grades/430-stainless-steel) use chromium with little or no nickel. That lowers cost and makes them magnetic. They are useful for appliances, indoor decorative panels, induction-compatible parts, and automotive exhaust grades.

The caution is formability and toughness. Ferritic grades are less forgiving than 304 in deep drawing and low-temperature service.

## Duplex: When 316L Starts Running Out

Duplex grades combine austenite and ferrite. [2205](/grades/2205-duplex-stainless-steel) gives much higher yield strength than 304 or 316L and better resistance to chloride stress corrosion cracking.

Use duplex when the job has chloride, pressure, wall-thickness pressure, seawater exposure, or high consequence. Do not use it casually. Welding discipline matters.

## Martensitic and PH: Strength First

Martensitic grades are for hardness: blades, shafts, valves, and wear parts. Corrosion resistance is lower, but heat treatment can make them hard.

PH grades such as 17-4PH are for high-strength precision parts. They need controlled heat treatment and are usually bought for a specific design, not as a commodity stainless substitute.

## The Family Rule

Start with environment and fabrication:

- Need easy forming and welding: austenitic.
- Need magnetic, indoor, lower-cost sheet: ferritic.
- Need chloride plus strength: duplex.
- Need hardness: martensitic.
- Need very high strength in machined hardware: PH.
