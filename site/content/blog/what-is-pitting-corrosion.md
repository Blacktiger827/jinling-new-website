# What Causes Pitting Corrosion, and How Do I Prevent It?

Pitting corrosion is dangerous because it looks small from the outside and grows deep from the inside.

The surrounding stainless surface may still look clean. The wall may still look bright. Then a pinhole appears, and the section that failed is only a few millimeters wide. That is the nature of pitting: local chloride attack at a weak point in the passive film.

The prevention logic is not complicated, but it has to be complete: chloride level, temperature, alloy margin, surface condition, and crevice-free design must all agree.

## Why Pitting Starts

Stainless steel protects itself with a thin chromium-rich passive film. Chlorides can break that film at vulnerable points: scratches, inclusions, heat-tinted welds, rough surfaces, stagnant deposits, or crevices.

Once a pit starts, the chemistry inside the pit becomes more acidic and chloride-rich than the surrounding water. The pit then feeds itself. The outside surface may repassivate while the inside cavity keeps dissolving.

That is why visual inspection alone is weak protection. By the time a pit is obvious, it may already be deep.

## The Four Controls

| Control | What to check | Practical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Chloride level | Water, product, sanitizer, brine, marine splash | Higher chloride needs higher alloy margin. |
| Temperature | Normal and upset temperature | Warm chloride service is much more aggressive than cold service. |
| Grade / PRE value | Cr, Mo, and N content | 316L improves on 304; duplex improves on 316L. |
| Surface and geometry | Weld tint, Ra, deposits, crevices | A rough or trapped zone can pit even when the grade looks correct on paper. |

Grade is only one control. A polished 304 surface still does not become seawater-grade material. A 316L weld with blue heat tint can fail locally even if the parent sheet is suitable.

## Grade Margin by Environment

| Environment | Common baseline | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh water, indoor condensation | 304 | Keep surfaces clean; avoid chloride cleaners. |
| Mild food, beverage, low chloride cleaning | 304 or 316L | Upgrade when sanitizer, acid, or temperature rises. |
| Brackish water, coastal air, salt spray | 316L minimum | Detail and maintenance decide whether the margin holds. |
| Seawater, desalination, hot brine | Duplex or higher alloy | 316L is often not enough for long service. |
| Crevice-heavy assemblies | Higher grade plus redesign | Alloy alone cannot solve trapped stagnant chemistry. |

For a more numerical alloy screen, see [PRE value stainless steel selection](/knowledge-base/pre-value-stainless-steel-selection).

## Prevention That Actually Works

The best prevention is to remove the initiation conditions before the equipment ships.

- Match the grade to real chloride and temperature, not to a generic environment name.
- Remove weld heat tint and embedded iron; passivate after the surface is clean.
- Specify a smoother surface where deposits or cleaning residues can sit.
- Avoid lap joints, dead legs, unsealed overlaps, and horizontal dirt traps.
- Keep cleaning chemicals inside the limits the grade can tolerate.
- Inspect high-risk welds and crevices before installation, not after leaks appear.

For weld-related corrosion, read [weld corrosion prevention](/knowledge-base/weld-corrosion-prevention).

## How to Investigate a Pit

When a pit appears, do not stop at "the grade failed." The grade may be wrong, but the failure point often sits somewhere more specific.

Check the location first. Is the pit near a weld, bend, crevice, drain point, or deposit line? Then check the service history: chloride concentration, cleaning chemical, temperature, stagnant time, and whether the part was scratched or ground.

The useful evidence package includes:

- MTC chemistry and grade confirmation
- photos showing pit position and surrounding geometry
- water or product chemistry, including chloride and pH
- cleaning chemical concentration, temperature, and contact time
- weld-treatment record if the pit is near a heat-affected zone
- surface condition and roughness if deposits were present

Pitting is local. The investigation should be local too.

## Pitting Shortcut

Pitting prevention is a margin problem. If chloride, temperature, surface condition, and geometry are all kind, 304 may serve for years. If any one of them becomes severe, the grade needs help. If two or three become severe together, the design needs more than a grade upgrade.

Do not let a clean-looking surface give false confidence. Pitting is the corrosion mode that hides its progress best.
