# Where Did the Stainless Bend Crack Start?

A bending crack usually tells the story before anyone cuts a sample for lab work. The origin matters: center of the outer radius, one slit edge, only bends parallel to the coil, or only the last bend in a multi-step part. Each one points to a different fix.

Re-bending the same setup usually just repeats the failure more neatly. Read the crack first.

## Four Common Crack Signatures

| What the crack looks like | Likely cause | First fix to try |
|---|---|---|
| Full-length crack at outer radius center | Radius too tight | Open radius by 0.5t to 1t |
| Crack starts at one edge and runs inward | Burr, shear damage, or edge hardening | Deburr or change cut route |
| Only bends parallel to coil length crack | Rolling direction problem | Rotate blank or increase radius |
| Later bends crack, earlier bends pass | Ductility already spent by prior forming | Anneal between stages or order softer temper |

This simple visual check prevents a lot of expensive blame. Material can be fine and still crack if the forming route uses the wrong margin.

## Radius Still Comes First

Annealed [304](/grades/304-stainless-steel) and [316L](/grades/316l-stainless-steel) can often bend around 1t across the rolling direction. [2205 duplex](/grades/2205-duplex-stainless-steel) needs more room. Ferritic [430](/grades/430-stainless-steel) is more edge-sensitive even when the radius looks acceptable.

If the radius is below the practical margin for the grade and direction, solve that before chasing more complicated explanations.

## Direction Can Change the Same Setup

One coil can pass and the next can fail if the blanks are nested differently. Bending across the rolling direction is safer. Bending parallel to it reduces the available ductility and makes cracks open along the worked structure.

When the radius is tight, mark rolling direction before cutting blanks. If a parallel bend cannot be avoided, open the radius and test scrap first.

## Temper and Prior Work Spend Ductility

The MTC may describe the sheet as delivered by the mill. The part on the brake may already have been leveled, drawn, punched, trimmed, or bent once. Every cold-working step spends part of the ductility budget.

If cracks appear only on later bends, the problem may not be the final radius alone. The earlier operations may have left too little ductility for the last bend. Intermediate annealing or a different forming sequence can be cheaper than scrapping the batch.

## Edge Prep Is Not Cosmetic

Sheared edges can carry burrs and micro-tears. Under bend tension, those flaws become crack starters. Laser-cut edges can also need review if the recast layer is hard and the bend is tight.

For tight bends, deburring is part of the forming process. It is not a finishing nicety.

## The Diagnosis Order

1. Photograph the crack before handling changes it.
2. Locate the origin: center, edge, direction-specific, or sequence-specific.
3. Check grade and temper on the MTC.
4. Check bend direction against rolling direction.
5. Check edge condition.
6. Change one variable and retest on scrap.

That last step matters. Changing radius, direction, edge prep, and tooling all at once may save the batch, but it teaches the shop nothing.

## The Better Fix

Most bending cracks come from four variables: radius, rolling direction, temper, and edge. If those are controlled, stainless is predictable.
