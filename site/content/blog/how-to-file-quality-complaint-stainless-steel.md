# How Do You File a Stainless Steel Quality Complaint That Holds Up?

A strong quality complaint is not an angry email. It is a clean evidence file.

The file needs three things: a measurable non-conformance, preserved material, and written notice before the claim window closes. If the material is already cut, mixed, polished, or installed, the conversation becomes softer almost immediately.

The goal is not to sound aggressive. The goal is to leave less room for the dispute to drift.

## Stop the Material First

Before writing the complaint:

- quarantine the affected lot
- label it with PO, heat number, and issue
- stop cutting, welding, polishing, or shipping it forward
- photograph packaging and material condition
- keep retain samples if testing will follow

This is the quiet step that decides whether the claim has teeth.

## Know What Type of Complaint You Have

| Complaint type | Evidence that matters |
|---|---|
| Wrong grade | MTC, heat number, PMI/OES/lab chemistry |
| Out of tolerance | calibrated measurements, standard clause, photos |
| Surface defect | agreed inspection condition, defect photos with scale |
| Missing/wrong documents | PO requirement, supplied certificate, heat traceability |
| Corrosion after service | service environment, grade check, failure pattern |

Do not file every issue as "bad quality." Name the defect. Suppliers respond faster when the failure mode is clear.

## What Goes Into the NCR

A usable NCR should fit on one or two pages:

- NCR number and date
- supplier, PO, invoice, heat number, product form, quantity
- specified requirement
- actual finding
- measurement or test method
- attached evidence
- requested remedy
- response deadline

Example wording:

> PO 2487 required 316L sheet per ASTM A240. PMI on 12 pieces from heat JL24083 shows no Mo reading consistent with 316L. Material is quarantined and unprocessed. Full PMI record and heat-label photos attached. We request replacement or credit proposal within 10 business days.

Keep emotion out. Put evidence in.

## Attach the Evidence Early

Attach the useful documents in the first formal complaint, not the fifth email:

- PO and supplier confirmation
- MTC and packing list
- photos of heat marks and labels
- measurement sheet with instrument calibration
- PMI or lab report
- surface defect photos with ruler and location
- delivery note, especially if signed with reservation

If lab testing is still pending, file the initial NCR anyway and state that the lab report will follow. Notification timing matters.

## What If the Supplier Delays?

Escalate by evidence, not volume.

1. Ask for written acknowledgement and a response date.
2. Copy the supplier's quality manager as well as sales.
3. Offer third-party inspection if measurements are disputed.
4. Keep the lot available and unprocessed.
5. If the claim is large, send a formal letter on company letterhead.

Silence is not a technical position. It is a reason to move the conversation into a more formal track.

## Complaint Shortcut

Freeze the material, name the defect, attach the proof, and ask for a specific remedy. That is the difference between a complaint that gets handled and a complaint that becomes a long chain of messages nobody wants to own.
