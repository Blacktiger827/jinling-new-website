# When Is 304 Stainless the Right Default?

304 is the default stainless for good reasons. It forms well, welds well, finishes well, and is available almost everywhere. For indoor, freshwater, mild outdoor, and many food applications, it gives enough corrosion resistance without paying for alloy content the job does not need.

The mistake is treating default as universal. 304 is not a coastal, seawater, hot-chloride, or high-temperature creep grade.

## What 304 Is

304 is an austenitic 18-8 stainless: roughly 18% chromium and 8% nickel. Chromium builds the passive film. Nickel keeps the structure formable, tough, and mostly non-magnetic in the annealed state.

Common equivalents include:

| System | Designation |
|---|---|
| ASTM / UNS | 304 / S30400 |
| EN | 1.4301 |
| JIS | SUS304 |
| GB | 06Cr19Ni10 |

For cross-standard orders, the exact certificate still matters. Equivalent names do not replace MTC review.

## Why It Became the Workhorse

304 sits at a useful balance point:

- Good corrosion resistance in ordinary air, freshwater, and mild food service.
- High formability for sheet, sink, tank, and appliance work.
- Familiar welding and finishing behavior.
- Deep supply chain, short lead times, broad product forms.
- Enough polishability for 2B, BA, No.4, hairline, and mirror routes.

That combination is hard to beat when the environment is not asking for more.

## Where 304 Performs Well

Use 304 for indoor architecture, commercial kitchen structures, general tanks, appliance panels, brewery equipment, freshwater lines, furniture, and non-coastal decorative sheet.

If the part is welded and will see corrosive service, use 304L or dual-certified 304/304L. If it is non-welded or only mildly exposed, standard 304 may be enough.

## Where 304 Stops

304 runs out of comfort when one of these appears:

| Service condition | Better route |
|---|---|
| Coastal salt air or chloride cleaners | 316L |
| Direct seawater, splash, or immersion | 2205 or higher |
| Welded corrosive service | 304L, 316L, or stabilized grade |
| Sustained high-temperature pressure service | 304H, 321, 347, or heat-service grade |
| Strong reducing acids | Higher alloy or non-stainless route |

The upgrade should be tied to the failure mode. Do not upgrade to 316L for heat service just because it sounds better. Do not push 304 into saltwater because it is familiar.

## What to Check Before Ordering

- Is the environment indoor, freshwater, or low-chloride?
- Will the part be welded after delivery?
- Does the finish need to be decorative or functional?
- Is the customer asking for ASTM, EN, JIS, or dual certification?
- Is any chloride, coastal exposure, or hot service present?

If the answers stay mild, 304 is not a compromise. It is the right default.

## Use the Default Where It Still Fits

304 earns its place because it covers a huge amount of ordinary stainless work cleanly. Use it with confidence where the environment fits. Step away from it quickly when chloride, welding risk, seawater, or hot loaded service becomes the real design driver.
