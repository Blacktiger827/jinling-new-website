# What Makes PVD Colored Stainless Look Consistent After Installation?

PVD colored stainless is usually durable enough. The harder question is whether all panels from all batches will still look like one project when they are installed under real light.

The color layer is thin. It does not hide the stainless below it. A mirror base stays a colored mirror. A hairline base stays a colored hairline. A weak substrate, mixed coil source, or loose batch approval will show through the color.

## PVD Is a Stack, Not a Paint Color

Physical Vapor Deposition puts a ceramic-like metallic layer over a prepared stainless finish. Gold, rose gold, champagne, bronze, gunmetal, and black are usually built from compounds such as TiN, TiCN, CrN, TiAlN, or ZrN.

Unlike paint or powder, PVD preserves the texture below it. That is its strength and its risk.

| Base finish | What PVD becomes | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror | vivid, deep, highly reflective color | feature walls, ceilings, low-touch luxury panels |
| Hairline | soft directional color | elevator panels, facade cladding, lobby walls |
| No.4 | practical brushed color | doors, trims, appliance fronts |
| 2B | subdued but less predictable | only when appearance demand is low |

If the project needs gold hairline, approve gold hairline. A gold mirror sample does not prove how the hairline batch will read.

## Color Consistency Starts Before Coating

The coating chamber matters, but it cannot correct a bad substrate.

Color drift often comes from:

- different base coils with different reflectance
- hairline grain direction changing between sheets
- mirror base with haze or edge-to-center variation
- coating runs split across different chambers or days
- approval based only on visual samples instead of measured color

For a project with adjacent panels, ask for a retained reference panel and a Delta E target. Delta E is the measured color difference between the approved standard and the production lot. A practical architectural target is often Delta E <= 2; premium retail or branded interiors may need tighter.

## What Grade Should Sit Under PVD?

304 is the normal architectural substrate because it gives stable surface quality and enough corrosion reserve for most interiors. 316L should be reviewed for coastal, pool-adjacent, humid, or aggressively cleaned environments.

201 and 430 can work for controlled indoor decorative jobs, but they need a clear service boundary. The risk is not that PVD refuses to coat them. The risk is that the substrate gives less corrosion margin, poorer batch uniformity, or less reliable long-term appearance.

## High-Touch Panels Need a Different Conversation

PVD is hard, but it is not magic. Deep scratches still cut through the finish. Fingerprints on black, bronze, and gold panels can also become the daily complaint.

For elevator buttons, appliance fronts, handrail-adjacent trims, and hospitality counters, consider AFP over PVD. AFP does not change the color route; it lowers surface energy so oils smear less and wipe off more easily.

## What the PO Should Say

A clean PVD order should name the whole stack:

> "304 hairline substrate, PVD champagne gold, grain vertical, approved reference sample retained, Delta E <= 2 against reference, protective film for fabrication and installation."

For exterior or coastal work, add grade and cleaning assumptions:

> "316L substrate required for coastal exposure. Chloride-bearing cleaners excluded from maintenance route."

Do all cutting, bending, welding, and edge finishing before PVD. Site repair on coated panels usually means replacing the panel, not touching it up.
