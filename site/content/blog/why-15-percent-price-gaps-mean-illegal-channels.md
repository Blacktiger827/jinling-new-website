# What Does a Big Stainless Price Gap Warn You About?

A quote that is 10-15% below the rest of the market is not automatic proof of fraud. Sometimes a supplier has stock, a simpler route, or a temporary commercial reason to move material.

But it is a warning. On the same grade, finish, thickness, document pack, and shipment route, that kind of gap deserves questions before it deserves a deposit.

## What the Gap Can Hide

| Possible reason | What to check |
|---|---|
| secondary or downgraded stock | surface condition, tolerance, defect allowance |
| grade substitution | MTC, heat number, PMI where justified |
| missing document path | final MTC timing, COO, export licence if applicable |
| different origin or HS route | invoice, packing list, customs code alignment |
| weaker packing or inspection | loading photos and claim evidence |
| real stock clearance | heat, age, finish, quantity, and why it is discounted |

The point is not to accuse first. The point is to make the cheap route explain itself.

## The Honest Counter-Case

Sometimes the low quote is legitimate. A supplier may be clearing stock, combining orders, using a simpler finish route, or quoting a product form that other suppliers priced too cautiously.

That is fine if the supplier explains it cleanly and the documents match. The warning sign is not the discount alone. It is the discount plus vague grade, late MTC, unclear origin, weak packing, or pressure to pay before evidence appears.

If the supplier can explain the gap with one plain reason and a matching document trail, check it. If the explanation keeps moving, slow the order down.

## Questions That Expose the Difference

Ask:

- Can you provide the MTC before booking?
- Does the heat number match the physical material and labels?
- What is the exact HS code and product description?
- What origin document will be issued?
- Is the material prime, secondary, or stock clearance?
- What inspection and packing photos are included?
- If China export licence applies, when is the licence application made?

A serious supplier answers plainly. A risky supplier often moves the answer to "after shipment."

## What Changed After the 2026 Export Licence

China's 2026 steel export licence regime makes document-light exports harder for covered products because the contract, quality evidence, goods description, and customs declaration need to align before export clearance.

That does not eliminate every irregular route. It does make casual paperwork gaps more visible. Buyers should still treat the cheapest quote as the quote that needs the most documentation discipline.

## PO Wording

Use:

> "Supplier to provide MTC before booking, heat/label alignment, final packing list, origin document route, HS code confirmation, and export licence reference where applicable before balance release."
