# Stainless Steel Pickling and Passivation Specs: What Should You Write?

Pickling and passivation are often named together, but they do different jobs.

**Pickling removes scale, heat tint, embedded iron, and chromium-depleted surface layers. Passivation helps a clean stainless surface rebuild a stable chromium-rich film.** Passivation cannot remove heavy oxide. Pickling is too aggressive to use as a casual cleaning step.

If the process note only says "pickle and passivate," the supplier still has to guess the chemistry, sequence, acceptance test, and documentation. That is where surface-treatment disputes usually begin.

## Pickling vs Passivation

| Process | What it removes | Typical use | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pickling | Heat tint, weld scale, oxide, iron contamination | Welded parts, hot-worked surfaces, visible oxide | It is not a cosmetic polish and can dull decorative finishes. |
| Passivation | Free iron and light contamination after cleaning | Machined parts, clean sheet, finished components | It cannot dissolve thick heat tint or mill scale. |
| Mechanical polishing | Scratches, roughness, visual defects | Decorative and hygienic surfaces | It can smear contamination if not followed by cleaning/passivation. |
| Electropolishing | Micro-peaks, light embedded contamination | Pharma, high-purity, premium hygienic work | It does not replace correct welding or root shielding. |

The safe sequence is always: remove the defect first, then passivate the cleaned surface.

## When Each Treatment Is Needed

| Situation | Sensible route |
|---|---|
| Lightly machined 304 or 316L, no weld scale | Clean, degrease, passivate. |
| Welded sheet with straw or brown heat tint | Pickle the weld zone, rinse, then passivate. |
| Blue or black weld oxide | Rework or pickle aggressively, then verify surface recovery. |
| Decorative No.4, hairline, or mirror panel | Avoid blanket pickling unless the visual effect is tested first. |
| Sanitary tube or food-contact weld | Remove tint, passivate, then inspect Ra and visual cleanliness. |
| Duplex 2205 fabrication | Use controlled pickling and strict time/temperature control; over-pickling can attack phase balance at the surface. |

For weld-specific treatment decisions, see [post-weld treatment for stainless steel](/knowledge-base/post-weld-treatment-stainless-steel).

## What to Put on the Route Card

A route card should define the condition to be treated and the acceptance basis. The chemistry belongs to the approved process procedure, but the purchase or inspection document should still be clear enough to release against.

Use language like this:

> Weld heat tint to be removed from product-contact surfaces. Pickle only affected weld/HAZ areas where visible oxide remains. Rinse to conductivity below 50 µS/cm. Passivate per ASTM A967 or agreed equivalent. Final surface: no visible heat tint, no free-iron indication on ferroxyl test, no acid residue, no staining after dry inspection.

That tells the supplier the goal without inviting uncontrolled acid work across the whole product.

## The Inspection Points That Matter

After treatment, inspect the surface in four layers:

- **Visual:** no blue/black tint, no scale, no acid streaks, no etched patches on visible surfaces.
- **Cleanliness:** no grinding dust, carbon-steel contamination, oil, adhesive residue, or trapped slurry.
- **Free iron:** ferroxyl or equivalent test where contamination risk is high.
- **Documentation:** method, batch, time, temperature range, rinse result, inspector sign-off.

If the part is for food, dairy, pharmaceutical, or high-chloride service, visual acceptance alone is not enough. The surface may look clean while the weld zone still carries the wrong chemistry.

## What Not to Over-Specify

Do not demand pickling just because stainless was welded. If the weld was made with good shielding, light tint, and controlled handling, local passivation may be enough. Blanket pickling can create cosmetic mismatch, dull a finish, or cause unnecessary rework on polished panels.

Also do not use passivation as a repair word. Passivation will not fix:

- deep scratches
- rough weld roots
- incomplete fusion
- embedded scale under oxide
- wrong filler metal
- a grade that is under-alloyed for chloride service

Surface treatment protects good fabrication. It does not rescue bad fabrication.

## Pickling and Passivation Shortcut

Use pickling when something unwanted is physically on or in the surface. Use passivation after the surface is clean enough to rebuild its passive film. Specify the sequence, treated area, acceptance test, and document record. That is the difference between a real surface-treatment instruction and a line item that only sounds technical.
