# What's the Difference Between Precision and Extra-Precision Strip?

Precision strip is more than narrow coil. It is a controlled specification package: tolerance class, temper, finish, edge condition, and measurement method.

The difference matters when small thickness or width drift changes the part itself. Springs change force. Connectors change contact pressure. Medical clips and watch parts change fit. For general trim or simple forming, Extra Precision is usually wasted money.

---

## ISO 9445 Classes in Plain Terms

ISO 9445 splits cold-rolled stainless strip into four tolerance classes. The exact values depend on thickness and width, but the buying logic is stable.

| Class | Meaning | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| N: Normal | Commercial strip tolerance | Non-critical strip, trims, simple forming |
| S: Special | Better gauge and width control | Better stamping, appliance parts, moderate die control |
| P: Precision | Tight control for functional parts | Springs, connectors, precision stampings |
| EP: Extra Precision | Tightest thin-strip route | Medical, watch, high-reliability connectors |

At thin gauges, EP can be several times tighter than normal strip. That does not mean every buyer should choose it. It means EP should be reserved for processes that can actually use the tighter band.

---

## When the Class Changes the Product

| Application | Usually enough | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Decorative strip or simple trim | N or S | Visual and forming demands are moderate |
| Stamped appliance parts | S | Die clearance matters but has margin |
| Leaf springs and retaining clips | P | Spring force moves with thickness |
| Electronic connectors | P or EP | Contact force and fit-up are sensitive |
| Watch or medical thin strip | EP | Dimensional drift becomes a reject |

Spring-force sensitivity is the clearest example. A small thickness change can create a much larger force change because spring rate rises with thickness cubed. That is why a strip that is "within ordinary tolerance" can still be unusable for precision springs.

---

## ASTM A666 vs ISO 9445

ASTM A666 is important for austenitic strip temper and mechanical properties. ISO 9445 / EN 10151 is more useful when the dimensional class is the problem.

| Need | Better reference |
|---|---|
| Temper: annealed, 1/4 hard, 1/2 hard, full hard | ASTM A666 |
| Precision strip class: N, S, P, EP | ISO 9445 / EN 10151 |
| Export order needing both temper and tight dimensions | Reference both |

Do not ask one standard to do the other standard's job. Temper controls strength and spring behavior. Tolerance class controls dimensional repeatability.

## What Must Be on the PO

An incomplete PO is the most common cause of precision-strip disputes. "304 strip, 0.50 × 25 mm" is not a precision specification.

Write the requirement as a package:

```text
304 stainless precision strip, coil.
Thickness: 0.50 mm ±0.015 mm, ISO 9445 Precision class.
Width: 25.0 mm ±0.10 mm.
Temper: 1/2 hard per ASTM A666.
Finish: BA.
Edge: slit edge, burr controlled.
Certificate: EN 10204 3.1 with measured thickness values per coil.
```

The numerical tolerance should appear even when the class is named. It gives receiving inspection a direct pass/fail number.

---

## What Affects Achievable Precision?

Not every grade, temper, or width can be supplied at every class.

- Thinner strip can be controlled tightly only on the right rolling line.
- Full-hard temper is harder to hold than annealed or 1/4 hard.
- Wider strip carries more crown and edge-to-center variation.
- Duplex and some high-strength grades are harder to roll to EP class than 304 or 316L.
- Slit-edge burr can ruin stamping even when thickness is correct.

If the part truly needs EP, qualify the supplier's capability before placing the full order. EP is a production route, not a label applied after inspection.

---

## Incoming Inspection

Precision strip should be checked against the PO, not against a catalogue expectation.

At minimum:

- Measure thickness at edge/center/edge and at several points along the coil.
- Measure width and check burr on slit edges.
- Confirm temper and mechanical properties if they are functional.
- Confirm finish and surface condition under appropriate light.
- Match heat number and coil ID to the certificate.

For EP class, hand micrometers may not be enough. Use a bench micrometer or laser gauge where the tolerance band is only a few microns.

---

## Selection Shortcut

Use Normal or Special class for strip that only needs general forming or decoration. Use Precision when the part function moves with thickness or width. Use Extra Precision only when the downstream process, inspection method, and product value justify the cost.

The better question is not "can the mill make EP?" It is "will my next process reject material that is only P class?" If the answer is no, do not overbuy.

## FAQ

**Is EP the same as spring quality?** No. EP is dimensional tolerance. Spring quality also needs temper and mechanical-property control.

**Can 2205 duplex be supplied as EP strip?** Usually not in thin gauges. Duplex is harder to roll to the same tight tolerance as 304/316L.

**Should I always specify ISO 9445?** Only for precision strip. For wide sheet, use ASTM A480 or EN 10088-2.

**Why does EP cost more?** It needs tighter rolling control, better gauging, higher rejection discipline, and often longer lead time.

**What is the biggest receiving mistake?** Measuring only the outer coil wrap. Thickness can drift along the coil; check multiple positions.

Precision strip buying works when tolerance, temper, finish, edge, and inspection method are written together. Leave one vague and the tightest class on paper may still fail in production.
