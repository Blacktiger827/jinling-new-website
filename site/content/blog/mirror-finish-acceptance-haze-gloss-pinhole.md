# How Do You Accept or Reject a Mirror Finish Stainless Steel Sheet?

Mirror finish is judged by the reflection people actually see, not by a quick glance in warehouse light.

That is why mirror disputes can feel oddly emotional. One person sees a clean sheet. Another sees haze under a downlight. Both may be telling the truth. The missing piece is usually the inspection condition.

Before judging the sheet, agree on film state, viewing distance, light direction, panel orientation, and the approved sample.

## Check in This Order

1. **Gloss consistency:** does the sheet reflect evenly across the face?
2. **Haze:** does the reflection look crisp or milky under raking light?
3. **Pinholes:** are there dark dots or inclusion pullout in the visual field?
4. **Residual polish lines:** are directional marks still visible after mirror polish?
5. **Film and handling marks:** are marks on the film or on the steel?

Do not start by debating one tiny edge mark. First decide whether the main visual field carries the mirror quality the project needs.

## What Haze and Pinholes Mean

Haze is diffuse cloudiness. The panel still reflects, but the image loses its sharp edge. It can come from polishing sequence, substrate cleanliness, film handling, or inconsistent pressure on the line.

Pinholes are local dark points or tiny depressions. On brushed finishes they may disappear visually. On mirror they announce themselves. The root cause is often in the base coil, not merely in the final polishing pass.

## A Fair Inspection Setup

| Variable | What to define |
|---|---|
| Film | Inspect film off where possible; if film stays on, record it. |
| Distance | 1 m or installed-view distance. |
| Light | Diffuse light plus raking light for haze. |
| Orientation | Flat, vertical, or installed-position equivalent. |
| Sample | Retained approved sample for gloss and haze tolerance. |

Without these, "no defect" and "obvious defect" may simply mean two different inspection setups.

## What Usually Justifies Rejection

Reject or hold when:

- haze appears in the main visual field
- pinholes repeat across visible panels
- polish lines remain visible after mirror finishing
- adjacent sheets do not match in gloss or tone
- film imprint or handling damage cannot be cleaned off
- the lot does not match the approved sample

Not every edge blemish is a reject. But mirror should be strict where people will actually see the panel.

## What to Put on the PO

Use language like this:

> Mirror finish judged against approved sample. Inspection at 1 m under diffuse light and raking light, film off where practical. Main visual face free of visible haze, pinholes, residual polish lines, and uncleanable film marks. Gloss/Ra target to be recorded on representative samples if specified.

That wording is not fussy. It prevents the shipment from being judged under one light and rejected under another.

## Mirror Shortcut

Mirror inspection needs a shared setup. Agree on the light, sample, distance, and visible face before production. Then reject only what the setup makes clear. If the inspection condition floats, the claim will float with it.
