# When Is a Stainless Sheet Too Wavy to Accept?

"Wavy" is not an acceptance rule. It is a warning that the sheet may not behave on the line. To accept or reject a stainless sheet, the buyer needs the standard, the gauge length, and the maximum allowed deviation.

That distinction matters because a sheet can look poor and still meet the default standard. It can also look acceptable on the floor and fail an automated feeder, laser table, or tight architectural panel route.

## Flatness Is a Contract Number

Most disputes fall into one of three cases:

| What happened | Commercial reading |
|---|---|
| The sheet exceeds the standard or PO limit | Hold the lot and document a non-conformance |
| The sheet meets the standard but fails your process | It is a specification gap, not automatically a mill defect |
| The PO says only "flat" or "no warping" | Hard to enforce because no measurable limit was bought |

ASTM A480, EN 10029, and related standards define flatness by measured deviation over a stated length. The exact table depends on product form, thickness, width, and whether the order bought a normal or special class.

A common ASTM A480 cold-rolled sheet baseline is measured over 914 mm. EN plate language often uses a metre-based flatness class. The exact number must come from the product form and ordered class, but the principle is the same: no gauge length, no enforceable flatness claim.

The buyer does not need to memorize every table. The buyer does need to stop using visual words as if they were inspection clauses.

## Name the Shape Problem First

Different flatness defects come from different causes.

Coil set is a lengthwise curve from being wound. Roller leveling usually fixes it. Edge wave means the edges are longer than the centre, and it can be stubborn. Centre buckle is the reverse. Cross bow curves across the width. Twist lifts opposite corners and may come from transport, stacking, or uneven residual stress.

If the complaint says only "warped," the supplier has to guess. If the inspection note says "edge wave, maximum gap 7 mm over 914 mm straightedge," the conversation becomes usable.

## How to Measure Without Guesswork

Use a flat reference table for smaller sheets. For larger sheets or plate, use a precision straightedge in the rolling direction, across the width, and on both diagonals. Measure the largest gap with a feeler gauge and record the gauge length.

The gauge length matters. A reading over 500 mm cannot be compared fairly with a standard written per 914 mm or per metre. Photos should include the straightedge, feeler gauge, and sheet identification.

For precision strip or coil feeding into automated equipment, ask whether I-unit control is needed. A coil may pass ordinary flatness checks and still be too unstable for laser blanking or high-speed feeding.

## When to Specify Leveling

Roller leveling is enough for normal coil set and many commercial sheet orders. Stretcher or tension leveling is the better route when the line needs very low residual stress and consistent feed. Press leveling belongs to heavy plate or local correction, not broad coil shape control.

If the downstream route is laser cutting, CNC fixtures, decorative wall panels, or automated feeding, do not leave flatness to the default standard. Buy the leveling route and the final measured limit.

## PO Language That Works

For ordinary sheet:

> "Flatness per ASTM A480, applicable table. Maximum deviation to be measured over the standard gauge length."

For tighter work:

> "Flatness maximum [X] mm per [914 mm / 1 m] after leveling. Measurement record required before dispatch."

For precision coil:

> "Stretcher-leveled coil. I-unit target not above [X], measured at leveler exit."

## The Acceptance Habit

Accept the lot when the measured deviation meets the written requirement and the order did not ask for more. Hold it when the measurement exceeds the PO or standard. If the material meets the standard but fails the line, treat it as a specification correction for the next order and negotiate the current lot commercially.

Flatness is not about proving who has better eyesight. It is about turning "this sheet looks wrong" into a number both sides can inspect.
