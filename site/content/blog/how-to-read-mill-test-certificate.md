# How to Read a Mill Test Certificate (MTC): Practical Release Guide

A Mill Test Certificate is useful only if it answers three questions quickly:

- Is this the right heat?
- Does it meet the ordered standard?
- Would this document hold up if the lot became a claim?

If any of those answers is unclear, the MTC is not a release document yet. It is just a PDF that needs investigation.

---

## What a Complete MTC Must Show

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Producing mill | Confirms who made the material, not merely who sold it |
| Heat number | Links the certificate to the physical steel |
| Grade and product standard | Defines the chemistry and mechanical limits |
| Actual chemistry | Proves the heat, rather than only a nominal grade |
| Mechanical test results | Confirms condition and product route |
| Certificate type and signature | Shows document authority |

For international stainless trade, EN 10204 Type 3.1 is the normal document level. Type 2.2 is often too weak for serious B2B release. Type 3.2 is used when an independent witness or project code requires it.

---

## Read the MTC in This Order

1. Match the heat number on the certificate to the material marking.
2. Check certificate type: 3.1, 3.2, or lower.
3. Confirm the grade and product standard match the PO.
4. Review chemistry against the ordered standard.
5. Review mechanical properties and heat-treatment condition.
6. Check any supplementary tests: PMI, hardness, UT, impact, intergranular corrosion, ferrite, or project-specific tests.

Do not start by looking at tensile strength. If the heat number does not match, the rest of the document is not tied to the steel in front of you.

## Chemistry Checks That Catch Most Problems

For common stainless orders, four chemistry fields catch many substitutions and weak certificates.

| Field | What to check |
|---|---|
| Carbon | L-grades should meet the low-carbon limit, usually ≤0.030% |
| Chromium | Confirms the stainless family and minimum passive-film chemistry |
| Nickel | Separates 200/300-series behavior and cost structure |
| Molybdenum | Confirms 316L/duplex chloride-resistance route |

For a 316L order, Mo should not be zero. For an L-grade welded assembly, carbon cannot be treated casually. For duplex, nitrogen and the exact UNS designation matter.

When two standards are named, check against both. A heat can pass ASTM and still fail EN because one element limit is different.

---

## Mechanical Values: What They Tell You

Mechanical values confirm that the product route produced the required condition. They are release evidence, not decorative numbers.

| Value | What it can reveal |
|---|---|
| Tensile strength | General strength level and cold-work indication |
| Yield strength | Design and pressure-equipment relevance |
| Elongation | Formability and annealing condition |
| Hardness | Heat-treatment or cold-work problems |

Values barely above minimum can still be compliant. Values outside the expected range deserve a question. High hardness on a supposedly annealed product, low elongation on a forming grade, or missing mechanical results on a specific inspection certificate should slow the release.

---

## Red Flags That Should Stop Release

Hold the material or document when you see:

- Heat number on material does not match the MTC.
- Certificate type is lower than the PO required.
- MTC lists only nominal ranges, not measured values.
- Issuer is a trader and no original mill certificate is attached.
- Grade chemistry does not match the stated grade.
- Product standard is missing.
- Multiple heats are bundled into one unclear document.
- Signature, inspector identity, or issue authority is missing.
- Values look suspiciously rounded or copied from a datasheet.

These are not small formatting issues. They weaken traceability, compliance, or claim defense.

---

## On-Site Verification

When paperwork is not enough, verify the steel itself.

| Method | Best use |
|---|---|
| PMI / XRF | Fast check of Cr, Ni, Mo, Mn for grade identity |
| OES | More complete chemistry, including carbon |
| Hardness test | Heat treatment, cold work, or wrong condition |
| Magnet check | Quick family clue, not final proof |
| Dimensional check | Confirms ordered size and tolerance |

PMI does not replace the MTC, and the MTC does not replace physical verification on high-risk orders. They answer different parts of the same release question.

## ASTM vs EN Cross-Standard Caution

Do not assume ASTM 316L and EN 1.4404 paperwork are automatically interchangeable. They are close commercial equivalents, but individual element limits and certificate wording can differ.

If the PO names both standards, the MTC should name both standards. If the MTC names only one, someone has to verify the heat against the other before shipment. For export orders, it is cleaner to request dual certification at order stage than to repair the document after the vessel is booked.

---

## MTC Release Shortcut

A usable MTC should let a receiving inspector say:

```text
This heat is the material in front of me.
This heat meets the standard on the PO.
This certificate is specific, signed, and defensible.
```

If the document cannot support those three sentences, do not release the lot yet.

## What We Ship by Default

For export stainless orders, the practical default should be EN 10204 Type 3.1 with heat-specific chemistry and mechanical results. For pressure equipment, PED, NACE, or buyer-witnessed projects, Type 3.2 or third-party inspection must be planned before production release.

The best documentation is built into the order. It is rarely rescued cleanly at the receiving dock.

## FAQ

**Is 3.1 the same as 3.2?** No. 3.1 is issued by the manufacturer's authorized inspector. 3.2 adds independent or buyer-approved witnessing.

**Can a 3.1 MTC show only nominal values?** No. A real 3.1 should show measured values for the specific heat or lot.

**What if the heat number does not match?** Stop release until the correct certificate or traceability proof is provided.

**Should I run PMI on every lot?** Use risk-based PMI: new suppliers, suspicious prices, corrosion-critical service, high-value grades, or chemistry near limits.

**Can a trader issue the MTC?** A trader can pass along documents, but the mill certificate should identify the producing mill and heat-specific data.

For field verification, read the PMI guide. For grade translation across standards, use export grade equivalence.
