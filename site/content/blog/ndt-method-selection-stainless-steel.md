# UT, RT, PT, or MT: Which NDT Method Should I Use on Stainless Steel?

Pick the NDT method by the defect you are afraid of, not by habit.

That is where stainless projects often go wrong. A clause says "MT all welds" because somebody copied it from a carbon-steel job. On 304 or 316L, that report may look clean while the method had little chance of finding the surface crack it was supposed to catch.

Start with the product form and defect location. Surface defect? Internal defect? Weld? Plate? Tube? The method follows from that.

## The Fast Decision Table

| What you need to find | Stainless product | Better method |
|---|---|---|
| Surface-breaking cracks | Austenitic or duplex welds | PT |
| Internal lamination or plate defects | Plate | UT |
| Volumetric weld defects with a permanent record | Pressure welds | RT, often with PT |
| Fast production-line tube defects | Tube or strip | ET |
| Surface cracks in ferritic/martensitic stainless | 430, 410, 420 | MT or PT |
| Visual weld profile and surface condition | Any fabrication | VT first, then PT/UT/RT as needed |

No single method covers every risk. PT will not find a buried lamination. UT will not judge surface heat tint. RT may miss planar cracks depending on beam direction.

## The One Mistake to Avoid: MT on 304 or 316L

Magnetic particle testing needs a material that can carry a magnetic field. Annealed 304, 316, and 316L are austenitic and essentially non-magnetic for this purpose.

If the job needs surface crack detection on austenitic stainless, specify PT. The cost is similar. The inspection actually works.

Duplex contains ferrite, but PT is still usually the safer surface method because MT sensitivity can be inconsistent. Ferritic and martensitic grades are different; MT can work there.

## PT, UT, RT, ET in Plain Language

**PT: liquid penetrant testing.** Good for surface-breaking defects. It needs a clean surface and controlled dwell time. Use it for weld toes, repaired areas, sanitary welds, and crack-sensitive surfaces.

**UT: ultrasonic testing.** Good for internal flaws in plate, pipe, forgings, and thicker welds. Stainless grain structure can make UT noisy, so procedure and operator skill matter.

**RT: radiographic testing.** Good when a permanent weld record is required. Common in pressure work. It is not a universal crack finder and carries safety controls.

**ET: eddy-current testing.** Good for tube and strip production scanning. It is fast and useful for mill-line screening, but it is not the same as final weld acceptance.

**VT: visual testing.** Still the first inspection. A camera, light, gauge, and trained eye catch many defects before expensive methods begin.

## What the PO Clause Must Say

Do not write "NDT required" and leave the rest to the supplier. A usable clause names:

- method: PT, UT, RT, ET, VT
- standard or procedure reference
- scope: 100%, sample percentage, weld length, heat, or lot
- acceptance level
- examiner qualification
- report and record format

Example:

> Austenitic stainless welds: 100% PT per ASTM E165 Type II or approved equivalent. Acceptance per project weld acceptance criteria. Examiner Level II minimum. Report to include weld ID, indication location, disposition, and repair/retest record.

That clause is simple, but it leaves less room for the wrong method to slip in.

## When to Bring in Third-Party Inspection

Use an outside inspector when the NDT record will decide release, payment, or customer acceptance. Pressure equipment, first orders, sanitary lines, duplex welds, and high-liability repairs all justify stronger witnessing.

For ordinary repeat commodity orders, a supplier's internal QC plus receiving inspection may be enough. The boundary is risk, not ceremony.

For inspection scope planning, see [third-party inspection for stainless steel](/knowledge-base/third-party-inspection-stainless-steel).

## NDT Shortcut

NDT does not make a part safe by existing on the paperwork. It only helps when the method can actually see the defect you care about.

For austenitic weld surface cracks, write PT. For plate internals, write UT. For pressure weld records, write RT with the proper acceptance route. For tube production scanning, write ET. If someone proposes MT on 304 or 316L, stop and fix the clause before the inspection produces a clean but empty report.
