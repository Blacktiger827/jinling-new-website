# What If the Chemistry on the MTC Is Out of Spec?

Do not react to one strange number too quickly. Also do not keep cutting while everyone argues about it.

A chemistry issue needs a calm sequence: confirm the standard, check whether the value is really outside the allowed range, verify with the right test, then decide whether the lot can be used, downgraded, replaced, or claimed.

The mistake is jumping straight from "the MTC looks wrong" to either panic or acceptance.

## First, Check the Basis of the Number

An MTC may show ladle analysis, product analysis, or both. Standards can allow small differences between them. A borderline value may still be acceptable if it sits inside the product-analysis tolerance.

That does not mean every deviation is harmless. It means the first question is:

> Which standard did the PO require, and which analysis basis is this number being compared against?

If the PO says ASTM A240 / A480, read the chemistry against that route. If the order was EN or GB, use that route. Do not compare a value against a remembered chemistry table and call it a claim.

For certificate reading, use [how to read a Mill Test Certificate](/knowledge-base/how-to-read-mill-test-certificate).

## Which Chemistry Problems Matter Most?

| Finding | Why it matters | Usual action |
|---|---|---|
| Chromium below minimum | Passive-film margin falls | Hold and verify by certified test. |
| Molybdenum low on 316L or duplex | Pitting resistance drops | Hold; likely claim if confirmed. |
| Nickel low with manganese high on "304" | Possible 201 substitution | Immediate PMI/OES confirmation. |
| Carbon high on 304L or 316L | Welding/sensitization risk | OES or lab test; do not weld until resolved. |
| Sulfur or phosphorus high | Weldability/toughness concern | Check application severity before disposition. |
| Small borderline difference | May be tolerance or measurement uncertainty | Verify before NCR. |

The red-flag pattern is low Ni + high Mn on material sold as 304. That is not a subtle tolerance conversation. It is a grade-identity problem.

## PMI Is a Screen, Not the Final Word

Handheld XRF is excellent for Cr, Ni, Mo, and Mn. It catches most substitution quickly. It does not measure carbon reliably.

Use PMI to decide whether the lot should stay on hold. Use OES, ICP, or another accepted chemistry method when the result will support an NCR, a return, or a commercial settlement.

For the limits of PMI, see [PMI testing on stainless steel](/knowledge-base/pmi-testing-stainless-steel).

## What to Do Before Raising the NCR

Do these steps in order:

1. Quarantine the heat. Do not cut, weld, or mix it into stock.
2. Match the MTC heat number to the physical material.
3. Confirm the exact standard and grade on the PO.
4. Run PMI if substitution is suspected.
5. Send a sample for OES/ICP if the claim depends on chemistry.
6. Keep the original MTC, photos, labels, and test reports together.

Only then write the NCR. The NCR should cite the element, the specified limit, the certificate value, the independent test value, the heat number, and the requested remedy.

## Can It Ever Be Accepted With a Waiver?

Sometimes, but only when the deviation does not affect the service and the customer or engineer accepts it in writing.

A small borderline chemistry issue on dry indoor decorative sheet is not the same risk as the same issue on welded pressure equipment, food-contact equipment, or coastal exterior work. Code-governed jobs usually leave little room for commercial waiver.

A proper waiver names:

- heat number
- PO and invoice
- actual deviation
- end use
- reason it remains acceptable
- who approved it
- whether price credit applies

It should not become an informal relaxation of the next order.

## Chemistry Shortcut

Chemistry claims are usually easier than surface claims because numbers can settle them. The hard part is keeping the process clean: hold the lot, verify the method, compare against the right standard, and decide before fabrication.

If the chemistry is confirmed wrong, move to [how to file a stainless steel quality complaint](/knowledge-base/how-to-file-quality-complaint-stainless-steel). If the question is whether the lot can still be used, use [non-conforming stainless steel decision guide](/knowledge-base/non-conforming-stainless-steel-decision-guide).
