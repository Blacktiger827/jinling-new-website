# What Should You Check Before Signing for a Stainless Steel Delivery?

The delivery note is not a small formality. It is the moment your leverage starts to shrink.

Before the truck leaves, you still have the material, packaging, labels, and carrier all in one place. After signing cleanly, the steel may be unloaded, mixed into stock, cut, or moved. At that point every claim becomes harder.

You do not need a full laboratory at the dock. You need a short, disciplined check before signing.

## Have These Ready

- purchase order
- packing list
- Mill MTC
- camera or phone
- calibrated micrometer
- tape measure
- magnet
- XRF/PMI access if grade risk is high

If the MTC has not arrived, write that on the delivery record. Do not accept silently and hope the document catches up later.

## The 15-Minute Dock Check

| Check | What you are looking for | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Heat number | MTC, tag, and material marking match | Sign with reservation or hold. |
| Grade and standard | PO and MTC say the same grade route | Hold until clarified. |
| Quantity | Bundle count, sheet count, weight match packing list | Note shortage before signing. |
| Packaging | No water, crush, torn film, or edge damage | Photograph before unloading. |
| Dimensions | Thickness, width, length sample inside PO tolerance | Record readings and hold if repeated. |
| Surface | No serious pits, scratches, rust, edge cracks | Photograph and segregate. |
| Grade screen | Magnet/PMI does not contradict label | Hold for PMI or lab check. |

The heat number is the anchor. If the steel and certificate do not share the same heat identity, the certificate does not prove the material in front of you.

## Sign, Reserve, or Refuse?

Use three decisions:

**Sign cleanly** only when identity, count, packaging, dimensions, and visible condition are acceptable.

**Sign with reservation** when something needs fuller inspection but the lot is not clearly rejectable. Write the reservation plainly:

> Signed subject to inspection. Packaging damage and surface marks photographed on bundle 2. Thickness verification pending.

**Refuse or hold** when heat identity is broken, grade looks wrong, thickness is repeatedly below tolerance, or packaging damage may have compromised the material.

Do not let the driver rushing you become your quality decision. If time is tight, photograph the lot and sign with reservation.

## What to Photograph

Take wide and close shots:

- truck/container condition
- bundle labels and heat numbers
- damaged packaging before opening
- surface defects with a ruler
- edge damage and wet areas
- any mismatch between document and physical marking

Good photos make a claim calm. Bad photos turn it into memory against memory.

## What Not to Do

Do not cut first and inspect later. Do not mix unknown bundles into stock. Do not accept a missing certificate as "normal." Do not rely on a magnet test to prove 304 or 316L. Do not use the word "defect" until you have compared the finding with the PO tolerance or acceptance criteria.

If the issue is dimensional, use [out-of-tolerance stainless steel claims](/knowledge-base/out-of-tolerance-stainless-steel-claim). If it is surface-related, use [surface defect acceptance criteria](/knowledge-base/stainless-steel-surface-defect-standards).

## Delivery Shortcut

The receiving check is short because it has to work in real life. Match the papers to the steel, take a few measured readings, look hard at the surface and packaging, and protect your signature. A clean delivery note should mean the lot was checked, not that everyone was in a hurry.
