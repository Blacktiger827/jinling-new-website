# What Can You Really Check Before a Stainless Order?

Samples, MTCs, and certificates of origin do not prove the same thing. Treating them as one document pack is how buyers get a polite "yes" early and a messy argument later.

Ask each document to do its own job.

![Stainless steel sample surface check before order confirmation](/images/blog/samples-mtc-coo/stainless-steel-sample-surface-check-before-order.jpg)

## What Each Item Proves

| Evidence | What it can prove | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|
| physical sample | finish, feel, rough visual expectation | final heat, full-lot consistency, shipping condition |
| sample photos under agreed light | appearance route when shipping a sample is slow | exact lot match |
| preliminary MTC | recent or nominated heat chemistry | final shipped heat unless tied to the order |
| final MTC | actual heat and standard compliance | surface acceptance by itself |
| certificate of origin | customs and origin declaration support | grade or finish quality |
| loading photos | packing condition before container close | what happened after unloading |

The timeline matters. A supplier can show a sample and a recent MTC before order. A final MTC, packing list, bill of lading, and final origin document become real only when the shipment exists.

## Match the Names Across Documents

Document quality is more than receiving every file. The names and numbers have to agree. The grade on the PO should match the MTC. The heat number should match labels. The invoice description should not fight the customs code. The origin document should describe the actual shipped route.

If those details are checked only after the vessel sails, the buyer may have no clean way to fix the file before customs or customer QA asks questions.

![Stainless steel sample photos and document pack discussion before deposit](/images/blog/samples-mtc-coo/stainless-steel-sample-document-pack-discussion.jpg)

## What to Ask Before Deposit

Before the deposit, ask for:

- sample or sample photo set for the surface route
- recent MTC for the same grade and standard
- written confirmation of the expected document pack
- origin route and COO format where customs or tariff treatment matters
- packing method for the product form and finish
- export licence timing if the Chinese HS code is in the licence catalogue

This is not paperwork theatre. It tells you whether the supplier understands the order before money moves.

![Stainless steel loading photos and container packing evidence before shipment](/images/blog/samples-mtc-coo/stainless-steel-loading-photos-container-packing-evidence.jpg)

## Do Not Ask for Impossible Documents

Asking for "all final documents before order" sounds strict, but some final documents cannot exist yet. The better request is staged:

> "Before deposit: sample route, recent MTC preview, expected COO format, packing method. Before balance: final MTC, packing list, loading photos, export licence reference where applicable, and B/L copy."

That wording leaves less room for false certainty.

## When Documents Matter More Than Samples

For distributors, project buyers, and importers in tariff-sensitive markets, the document path may be more important than the sample. If the origin declaration, MTC heat number, invoice description, and customs code do not line up, a beautiful sample will not save the shipment.
