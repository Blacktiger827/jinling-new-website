# Why Can a Pressure-Tight Weld Fail Food-Grade Release?

A stainless pipe weld can hold pressure and still fail sanitary release. Industrial piping asks first whether the joint is sound and traceable. Food and pharmaceutical piping asks whether the inside surface can be cleaned in service.

Those are different questions. A buyer who sends an ordinary industrial weld package into food service may have pressure records and still be missing the bore evidence that hygiene needs.

## The Difference Is Inside the Pipe

| Release question | Industrial pipe | Sanitary or food-grade pipe |
|---|---|---|
| Main concern | Pressure integrity | Cleanable internal bore |
| Common route | Qualified WPS, RT/UT as required, hydrotest | Orbital or controlled TIG, purge record, bore inspection |
| Backing rings | Sometimes allowed | Not acceptable |
| Internal root | Sound fusion | Smooth, flush, no sugaring, no crevice |
| Documentation | WPS, PQR, NDE, pressure test, MTC | Weld log, heat trace, purge, borescope, Ra, passivation |

The alloy may be the same. The acceptance logic is not.

## Why Sanitary Welding Is Stricter

Food and pharma lines are cleaned by CIP, steam, sanitizer, and flow. The inside of the weld must not trap residue. A sugared root, undercut, backing ring, rough bead, or crevice can become a biofilm site even if the weld is pressure-tight.

That is why sanitary pipe work usually uses 304L or [316L](/grades/316l-stainless-steel), TIG or orbital TIG, full back purge, and a documented root condition. The root surface matters because the product touches it.

## Where Industrial Welding Has More Flexibility

Industrial stainless pipe under process piping codes may allow bead profiles, backing aids, and examination plans that would be unacceptable in sanitary service. That does not mean industrial welding is loose. It means the release target is different: containment, code compliance, NDE, and service category.

If the fluid is chloride-bearing or corrosive, industrial piping still needs purge control and cleanup. A code-compliant sugared root can still become the first corrosion site.

## What to Specify Before Fabrication

For sanitary work, do not write only "316L welded pipe." Write the release route:

> 316L sanitary tube, TIG/orbital TIG, no backing rings, back-purge oxygen limit, smooth internal root, borescope where required, Ra and passivation records in release package.

For industrial work:

> WPS/PQR qualified to project code, filler matched to base grade, purge and post-weld treatment defined by service, NDE and hydrotest per piping class.

The earlier this is written, the fewer arguments happen at inspection.

## The Borescope Moment

Sanitary welds are often decided by a simple view inside the tube. If the root is grey, sugared, sharp, undercut, or rough, the pressure test does not rescue it. The weld may be strong. It is not cleanable.

For food and pharma work, plan inspection access before the spool is built. If you cannot inspect the root later, purge and procedure control during welding become even more important.

## The Release Rule

Code-compliant is not the same as hygienic-compliant. Food-grade release needs proof of internal surface quality, not merely proof that the pipe held pressure.
