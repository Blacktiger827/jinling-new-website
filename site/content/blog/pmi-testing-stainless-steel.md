# How Does PMI Testing Work on Stainless Steel, and When Do I Need It?

PMI is good at catching the obvious expensive mistake: the alloy in front of you is not the alloy on the order.

It is not a full laboratory certificate. That distinction matters. A handheld XRF gun can catch 201 sold as 304, or 304 sold as 316L, in seconds. It cannot see carbon, so it cannot prove 304L versus 304 or 316L versus 316.

Use PMI as a fast gate. Use the MTC and, when needed, OES or lab chemistry for formal release.

## What PMI Actually Measures

Most stainless PMI is done with handheld XRF. The instrument reads major alloying elements at the surface and compares them with a grade library.

| Element | Why it matters | XRF result quality |
|---|---|---|
| Chromium | Confirms stainless family and ferritic/austenitic range | Strong |
| Nickel | Separates 200/300/400 series | Strong |
| Molybdenum | Separates 316/316L from 304/304L | Strong enough for screening |
| Manganese | Flags 201-type substitution | Useful |
| Carbon | Separates L-grade from standard grade | Not reliable by handheld XRF |

That carbon gap is the trap. If the question is "is this 316 family or 304 family?", XRF is excellent. If the question is "is this truly 316L?", XRF is not enough.

## XRF, OES, or Lab Chemistry?

| Method | Best use | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Handheld XRF | Fast grade-family screening at receiving or pre-shipment | Cannot verify carbon or nitrogen. |
| Portable OES | On-site carbon check for L-grade boundaries | Needs surface grinding and leaves a spark mark. |
| Lab ICP/OES | Formal chemistry dispute or certificate-grade confirmation | Slower, more expensive, but defensible. |

For normal receiving, XRF plus heat-traceable MTC is usually enough. For pressure, pharma, food-critical, sour service, or a supplier dispute, move to OES or laboratory confirmation.

## The Substitutions PMI Catches Well

| Suspected substitution | What the instrument sees | PMI usefulness |
|---|---|---|
| 201 sold as 304 | Lower Ni, higher Mn | Very strong |
| 304 sold as 316L | No meaningful Mo | Very strong |
| 430 sold as 304/316 | No nickel, ferritic chemistry | Very strong |
| Wrong duplex family | Cr/Mo/Ni not aligned with 2205 range | Useful screen |
| 304 sold as 304L | Carbon boundary only | Not enough |

PMI is therefore a powerful anti-substitution tool, but a weak low-carbon certification tool.

## When You Should Require PMI

Use PMI when the risk is commercial or technical enough that a bad grade would be costly:

- first order from a new supplier
- unusually low price
- 316L, duplex, or high-value grade
- mixed bundles or repacked material
- critical corrosion service
- customer audit or project traceability requirement
- material received through a trading chain rather than mill-direct route

For repeat commodity 304 from a known supplier, sample PMI may be enough. For a first 316L order from an unknown route, checking every bundle is cheap insurance.

## How to Make the Result Defensible

A PMI report should do more than say "pass." It should show what was tested and how.

Minimum record:

- instrument model, serial number, and calibration check
- material ID, heat number, bundle or coil number
- surface condition before testing
- number of readings and test locations
- Cr, Ni, Mo, Mn readings
- grade match conclusion
- inspector name and date

Take at least three readings on clean, uncoated metal. Do not test through paint, film, heavy scale, or oil. If a result sits near the boundary, clean and retest before raising a claim.

## What PMI Cannot Replace

PMI does not replace:

- the Mill Test Certificate
- heat-number traceability
- carbon-capable chemistry testing
- ferrite testing on duplex where phase balance matters
- mechanical testing
- surface or dimensional inspection

The best use of PMI is practical and narrow: it stops the wrong alloy from moving forward.

## PMI Shortcut

Use handheld XRF to answer: "Does this material look like the alloy family we bought?" If the answer is no, hold the lot. If the answer is yes, keep going with the normal release record.

Do not ask PMI to prove things it cannot see. For receiving sequence, pair this with [incoming inspection for stainless steel](/knowledge-base/stainless-steel-incoming-inspection-checklist).
