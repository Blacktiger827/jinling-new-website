# Does a Smoother Stainless Surface Resist Corrosion Better?

Yes, but only after the grade is already in the right neighborhood. A smoother surface holds less chloride, gives deposits fewer places to sit, and removes some pit-initiation sites. It does not make 304 suitable for seawater or make a poor crevice harmless.

Ra is useful because it turns "smooth enough" into something that can be measured. It is also easy to misuse. A single Ra number cannot see every deep scratch, every inclusion, or the chemistry of the first few nanometers of the surface.

## Why Rough Surfaces Corrode Faster

Grooves and scratches slow down rinse water and trap residue. In those small valleys, oxygen drops, chloride concentrates, and the passive film has a harder time repairing itself. That is the beginning of pitting or crevice-like attack.

The risk is higher on directional finishes, heavy brushing, poorly cleaned weld zones, and surfaces that see salt spray, CIP chemicals, hard water, or standing moisture.

## Where Ra Belongs in the Specification

| Service | Useful surface target | What still matters more |
|---|---|---|
| General architecture | Avoid rough grinding marks; choose finish by exposure | Grade, cleaning access, coastal distance |
| Food equipment | Ra around 0.8 µm or finer for cleanability | Weld quality, drainage, cleaning chemistry |
| Dairy, CIP, pharmaceutical | Ra 0.5 µm down to 0.25 µm, often electropolished | 316L, documentation, passivation route |
| Coastal visible panels | Smooth 316L or better, with low-contamination finishing | Alloy margin and maintenance |
| Seawater immersion | Smooth helps little if grade is wrong | 2205, 2507, 254 SMO, crevice control |

The practical order is grade first, geometry second, finish third. Finish refines the margin. It should not be asked to create the margin by itself.

## Why Electropolishing Can Beat Mechanical Polishing

Two surfaces can show the same Ra and behave differently. Mechanical polishing flattens peaks and leaves a directional surface. It may also smear metal, leave abrasive residue, or keep inclusions at the boundary layer.

Electropolishing removes metal electrochemically. It preferentially removes iron-rich areas and surface inclusions, leaving a more chromium-rich passive film. In chloride-sensitive or hygienic service, that surface chemistry can matter more than the last small improvement in Ra.

This is why a mechanically polished 316L at Ra 0.4 µm and an electropolished 316L at Ra 0.4 µm should not always be treated as equivalent.

## What Ra Does Not Tell You

Ra is an average. It may miss a single deep scratch that becomes the first pit. It does not prove the surface is free of iron contamination. It does not confirm passivation. It does not describe weld tint. It does not tell you whether the surface was measured along or across the polishing direction.

For contractual work, specify the measurement method, direction, sampling plan, and whether electropolishing or passivation is required. A bare "Ra <= 0.4 µm" line is better than nothing, but it leaves too much room for dispute.

## A Better Way to Write the Requirement

For corrosion-sensitive visible work:

> 316L surface finish to agreed sample, no visible grinding burn or embedded iron. Ra target stated by surface type. Final clean and passivate after fabrication.

For hygienic or pharmaceutical work:

> 316L, final internal surface Ra limit by measured method, electropolished where required, documented passivation and inspection after final fabrication.

The wording should connect surface quality to the actual service. Otherwise Ra becomes a decorative number detached from corrosion performance.

## Use Finish as Margin, Not a Rescue Plan

A smoother stainless surface usually behaves better, especially where chloride or cleaning chemistry can sit on the metal. Electropolishing can add real corrosion margin because it changes surface chemistry as well as texture.

But finish is still the supporting actor. If the environment needs 316L, 2205, or 2507, a lower Ra on the wrong grade is still the wrong specification, only polished more carefully.
