# Do You Need Mill Edge or Slit Edge?

Edge choice looks small on a purchase order. It is not small when the strip reaches a tube mill, stamping die, roll-forming line, or decorative panel shop.

Mill edge is the as-rolled edge. It is usually rounded, less precise in width, and often cheaper because no trimming pass is needed. Slit edge is cut from a wider coil with rotary knives. It gives tighter width control, but the cut face can carry burr.

The right choice depends on what the next process will punish.

## Choose the Edge by Use, Not by Habit

| Downstream use | Better edge choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tube mill or roll-forming line | Slit edge with burr limit | width and burr affect seam stability |
| Precision stamping | Slit edge, often deburred | edge cracks can start at the burr |
| Decorative exposed edge | Slit, deburred, or rounded | the edge is visible and touchable |
| General fabrication sheet | Mill edge may be acceptable | the edge is often trimmed, welded, or hidden |
| Laser blanks from larger sheets | Either, if final cut removes the edge | buying slit precision may not add value |

"Slit edge" alone is still incomplete for sensitive lines. The order should state the burr limit. A tube mill may need a tighter burr requirement than a general fabrication buyer. "Burr-free" sounds clear, but it is not a number.

Use a clause like:

> "Slit edge. Burr height max [X] mm. Burr direction and inspection method to be agreed before production."

If the edge will be touched, painted, polished, or used as a visible decorative line, add deburring or rounding rather than hoping the slitting line leaves a pleasant edge by default.

## Why Mill Edge Still Has a Place

Mill edge is not a lower-grade product. It is the natural edge from rolling. For many plate and sheet orders, it is perfectly reasonable because the fabricator cuts the final geometry anyway.

It becomes a problem when the buyer expects slit-width control or a clean raw edge without paying for that process. Mill edge can vary more in width, and the edge profile may be wavy or scaled. If the edge enters tooling directly, that variation can turn into feed problems or uneven forming.

## Do Not Separate Edge From Width

Edge type and width tolerance belong together. A PO that says "600 mm wide" but does not say mill edge or slit edge leaves too much room for interpretation.

For coil or strip, write:

> "Width 600 mm +/- [X] mm, slit edge, burr max [X] mm."

For plate that will be trimmed later:

> "Mill edge acceptable. Final machining or trimming by buyer."

That one sentence tells the mill whether width precision is part of the delivered product or only a starting size for later processing.

## The Other Delivery Terms Nearby

Edge is usually discussed with length basis, billing basis, and coil handling limits. Keep those terms short and explicit.

- fixed length means every piece is cut to the ordered dimension
- random length means pieces fall inside an agreed range
- multiple length means the mill supplies lengths that the buyer cuts into repeated parts
- actual weight billing follows the scale ticket
- theoretical weight billing follows nominal size and density
- coil ID, OD, and weight must match the buyer's decoiler, crane, and container plan

Do not let these details drift into side emails. They belong on the PO.

## What Does a Clean Edge Order Look Like?

A good edge clause is not complicated:

> "Cold-rolled stainless strip, 316L, 0.8 mm x 600 mm. Slit edge. Width +/-0.2 mm. Burr max 0.03 mm, measured on first and last coil section. Actual weight billing. Coil ID 508 mm."

That tells production what to make and receiving inspection what to check. If the order only says "edge: normal," the buyer has given away the argument before the coil leaves the service centre.
