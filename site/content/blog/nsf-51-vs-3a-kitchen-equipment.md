# Should Kitchen Equipment Ask for NSF 51 or 3-A?

`NSF 51 stainless` and `3-A stainless` are often used as if they were raw-material grades. They are not. They point to finished-equipment routes.

The difference matters because a restaurant prep table, a kitchen sink, a dairy process line, and a CIP-able tank are not judged the same way.

The standard should describe how the equipment will be cleaned and inspected, not how serious the buyer wants the order to sound.

## Use NSF 51 for Commercial Kitchen Equipment

NSF/ANSI 51 is usually the right language for commercial food equipment such as:

- prep tables
- sinks
- counters
- refrigerator interiors and exteriors
- cabinets and appliance housings
- restaurant work surfaces

For these projects, the stainless supplier can support the file with grade, MTC, finish, and roughness evidence where ordered. The finished-equipment maker owns the final compliance route.

If the buyer needs NSF certification, the certified product or equipment listing is the place to verify it. A mill certificate alone is material evidence, not an NSF approval.

## Use 3-A for Sanitary Process Equipment

3-A belongs to hygienic process routes, especially dairy and food/beverage systems where cleanability, drainage, weld geometry, fittings, and CIP behavior are part of the standard.

Think sanitary tubing, process tanks, dairy skids, valves, pumps, and fittings. In those systems, material chemistry is only one piece. Geometry and fabrication quality are just as important.

## The Quick Split

| Equipment type | Better standard language |
|---|---|
| restaurant prep table | NSF 51 |
| commercial sink | NSF 51 |
| refrigerator cabinet | NSF 51 |
| dairy process tube | 3-A route |
| CIP process tank | 3-A or project sanitary standard |
| food factory transfer line | depends on hygienic-process requirement |

If the equipment behaves like a commercial kitchen product, start with NSF 51. If it behaves like sanitary process equipment, 3-A may be the right route.

This keeps the certificate request honest. A 3-A phrase on a backsplash does not make the backsplash safer. An NSF phrase on a dairy process tube does not answer the hygienic-process questions either.

## What the Mill Can and Cannot Prove

The mill can usually provide:

- MTC and heat number
- grade chemistry
- thickness and dimensional standard
- finish description
- measured Ra if ordered and tested

The mill normally cannot certify the final equipment to NSF 51 or 3-A. It cannot prove weld drainage, corner radius, dead-leg control, installation slope, or final cleanability after fabrication.

That boundary should be written plainly, not hidden in a certificate request.

## PO Wording

For commercial kitchen equipment:

> "304 stainless sheet for NSF 51 equipment route, MTC required, finish and thickness stated. Final equipment compliance by fabricator."

For sanitary process equipment:

> "316L sanitary tube or sheet for 3-A process route, Ra target and heat traceability required. Weld/passivation records by fabricator."
