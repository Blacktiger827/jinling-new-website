# Why We Test Every Sanitary Tube, Length by Length

There is a quiet minute near the end of our sanitary tube line. A finished tube is sealed, lowered into water, pressurized, and watched.

If one bubble appears, that length does not ship.

The pressure number matters, but the more important rule is coverage: every length is tested. Not one sample from the lot. Not one tube from the shift. Every tube.

## Why Per-Length Testing Matters

Sanitary tube is not ordinary pipe with a nicer polish. A pinhole in a milk line, brewery transfer line, or pharmaceutical loop is not a small maintenance nuisance. It can mean cleaning downtime, contamination investigation, product loss, or a customer audit problem.

That is why statistical testing is the wrong shape for this risk. One leaking length is enough to hurt the whole system.

## What the Test Proves

The underwater hydrostatic test catches leaks that visual inspection may miss.

| Failure | What appears in the tank | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Weld pinhole | small steady bubbles | leak path under pressure or cleaning cycle |
| Crack along weld | bubble line following the seam | weak weld that should never reach installation |
| HAZ porosity | diffuse bubbling near weld zone | local defect that may grow with thermal cycling |

A dry visual check can miss all three. Water under pressure makes the defect speak plainly.

## Why Water, Not Air?

Water testing is safer and clearer. Compressed air at high pressure stores more energy. If something fails, air testing can turn a tube end into a projectile. Underwater hydro also makes the leak visible immediately as bubbles.

For sanitary tube, pneumatic shortcuts are not an equivalent release method. They may be faster. They are not the same evidence.

## What Comes Before Hydro

Hydro is the final gate. It should not be asked to rescue a bad line.

Before the tube reaches the tank, the process has already controlled:

- weld shielding
- bright annealing
- interior polish
- weld bead condition
- eddy-current screening
- cutting and end handling

If hydro starts finding repeated failures, the real question is upstream: welding parameter drift, shielding gas, base-metal cleanliness, or handling damage.

## What to Ask for on the PO

Write it simply:

> Per-length underwater hydrostatic test at 24 bar, hold ≥ 1 minute. Pneumatic substitution not accepted. Test record retained by length and supplied on request.

If the supplier cannot confirm per-length testing, ask what exactly is being tested. "Batch tested" and "sanitary tube" should not be treated as the same promise.

## Sanitary Tube Shortcut

The point of the 24 bar test is not to impress anyone with a big number. The point is to keep one leaking length from becoming the customer's problem.
