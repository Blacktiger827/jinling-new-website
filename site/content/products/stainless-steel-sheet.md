# Stainless Steel Sheet & Plate

**Choose sheet and plate when the delivered panel itself has to release cleanly.** Flatness, cut size, finish protection, and arrival condition matter before fabrication starts.

This is the right family for laser blanks, visible decorative panels, kitchen fronts, cladding pieces, and heavy plate where the panel state on arrival is part of the specification. Jinling supplies both light-gauge sheet and heavy plate in one route, from 0.3 mm cold-rolled through 80 mm plate.

Most buying mistakes here happen before processing begins. Buyers need to settle what the panel must look like when it lands, then work backward into grade and finish.

## Choose Sheet When the Panel Is Already the Product

Sheet and plate make sense when the delivered flat panel, blank, or heavy plate needs to be close to its final release condition. If the buyer still needs coil-side conversion after receipt, coil is usually the cleaner route.

**Leveled sheet** is the right start for laser cutting, bending, and panel blanks where flatness, film, cut size, and thickness tolerance travel together.

**Finish-visible sheet** needs finish, grain direction, film, and packing treated as one system because the installed face will be judged later.

**Hot-rolled plate** should name standard, thickness range, cut-to-size route, and MTC wording before the project compares price.

| Heavy plate | Vessel shells, structural blanks, thick parts, duplex plate work | Plate standard, cut-to-size, thickness range, delivery flatness |

## Where Panel Release Gets Cleaner

**Panels are flattened before they are cut.** Tension leveling removes coil memory, which matters immediately on laser nests, press-brake work, and visible panel assemblies.

**Film is chosen for the downstream process.** Buyers can release laser-compatible, standard, or AFP-compatible protection instead of accepting one generic film.

**Decorative sheet and heavy plate stay in one family.** A project can keep the same supplier logic whether it needs 1.2 mm finish sheet or 25 mm cut plate.

## Surface-Finished Panels Need a Different Release Plan

Sheet and plate are where surface treatment should take the lead. For visible panels, lock the finish family, grain direction, protective film, flatness expectation, and packing method before the cut plan is released.

For kitchen, elevator, and architectural panels, Jinling treats finish and geometry as one release package: leveling before cut-to-size, film matched to laser or AFP handling, and packing that keeps the visible face protected through export delivery.

Use the [surface finish library](/surfaces) when choosing between 2B, BA, No.4, Hairline, Mirror, and AFP. Use the [grade library](/grades) when corrosion exposure or service temperature is still open.

## Order Notes Before the Cut Plan

**Panel state.** Say whether the order is laser blank sheet, finish-visible sheet, or heavy plate. That decides the release logic faster than the grade name alone.

**Dimensions and tolerance.** Confirm thickness standard, W x L, flatness expectation, and any cut-size tolerance before quote approval.

**Finish and protection.** If the panel is visible, lock the finish, grain direction, film type, and sample-approval basis before production starts.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between sheet and plate?** The common *ASTM A480* break is 6 mm. Below that is sheet; at or above that is plate, though buyers often quote them together when the project spans both.

**Can I order cut-to-size instead of standard cut lengths?** Yes. Custom W x L panels are available, and pricing reflects yield loss on non-standard sizing.

**Which finish is usually best for laser cutting?** For fabrication stock, 2B is the usual starting point, paired with laser-compatible film when surface protection matters.

**Can Jinling supply MTC documentation with cut panels?** Yes. Mill MTC is standard, with the document type confirmed at order stage.

**Is 2205 available in thin sheet?** 2205 is standard from about 3 mm upward. Thinner-gauge duplex is possible, but it is usually an indent item rather than a shelf item.
