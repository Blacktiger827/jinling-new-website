# Stainless Steel Angle Bar for Frames, Supports, and Unequal-Leg Connections

**Choose angle bar when geometry itself becomes part of the load path.** Equal-leg and unequal-leg sections are different buying choices, even in the same grade.

Angle bar is easy to under-specify. Leg ratio, perpendicularity, and size route show up during fit-up long before anyone cares about a long material explanation.

Most angle-bar mistakes start with geometry left too loose. Fix the section first, then the alloy.

## Set the Geometry Before the Grade

Angle bar is not simply flat stock bent into a corner. The leg ratio, perpendicularity, and straightness decide whether the frame or bracket actually lands cleanly on site.

**Equal-leg angle** is the safer first route when the load and fit-up are balanced on both sides.

**Unequal-leg angle** should be named early when the connection is asymmetric. If the leg ratio is vague, the drawing can look right while the installed support sits wrong.

**Wet, coastal, or higher-load service** changes the alloy after the geometry is clear. That is where 316L or 2205 review belongs.

## Which Geometry Fits the Fabrication?

| Geometry | Best fit | Normal size window | Main release point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equal-leg light section | Trim, light frames, smaller brackets | 20 x 20 to 40 x 40 mm, cold-formed | Corner sharpness and cut length |
| Equal-leg heavy section | Structural frames, supports, mezzanine details | 50 x 50 to 150 x 150 mm, hot-rolled | Thickness, straightness, load path |
| Unequal-leg section | Connection details, shelf supports, asymmetric brackets | 30 x 20 to 150 x 100 mm | Leg ratio and perpendicularity |

## Where Fit-Up Control Matters

**Perpendicularity is treated as a real release point.** Fit-up problems usually come from geometry drift, not from the alloy certificate.

**Equal-leg and unequal-leg work can stay in one project order.** That helps structural buyers who need several connection types in the same shipment.

**The grade range matches real environments.** 304L fits general fabrication, 316L fits wet and coastal exposure, 2205 fits higher load and chloride duty, and 430 stays in non-structural trim work.

## What Tolerances Matter Most?

| Dimension | Tolerance | Standard ref |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Leg length | ±1% of leg | *ASTM A484* |
| Leg perpendicularity | ≤ 1.5° (Class A) | *EN 10056* |
| Leg perpendicularity, tight | ≤ 1.0° (Class B) | *EN 10056* |
| Length, cut-to-length | +5 / 0 mm | *ASTM A484* |
| Straightness | ≤ 0.3% of length | *ASTM A484* |

## Order Notes That Prevent Site Adjustment

**Geometry first.** Confirm equal-leg or unequal-leg, the leg ratio, and the size family before comparing prices.

**Fit-up requirement.** If the job needs tight perpendicularity or cut-to-length control, put that on the order instead of leaving it to site adjustment.

**Grade and documentation.** State the environment, alloy, and any PMI or project-document requirement before release.

## FAQ

**Can I order unequal-leg angle in 2205 duplex?** Yes. Lead time depends on leg ratio, thickness, and quantity, but it is a normal quoted item.

**Does Jinling cut angle bar to drawing length?** Yes. Cut-to-length is available, and the normal tolerance is +5 / 0 mm.

**What is the difference between hot-rolled and cold-formed angle?** Cold-formed suits lighter sections and cleaner corners. Hot-rolled is the normal choice once the section becomes heavier and more structural.

**Can Jinling produce non-standard leg ratios?** Yes, special geometry can be quoted with drawing and quantity, though it is no longer a standard stock item.
