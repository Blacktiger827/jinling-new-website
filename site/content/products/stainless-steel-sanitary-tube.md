# Stainless Steel Sanitary Tube to ASTM A270

**Every tube spends a minute underwater at 24 bar before it is allowed to ship. If it bubbles, it does not.**

That sentence is the entire difference between sanitary tube and any other kind of welded pipe. A pressure pipe proves it can hold — a sanitary tube has to prove it is clean *and* that it holds, and the proof is line-by-line, not batch-by-batch. Industries that carry milk, beer, purified water, reagents, and injectables all settle on the same answer for the same reason: the cost of one leaking joint in service is larger than the cost of testing every tube in production.

Jinling supplies ASTM A270 sanitary tube in 304L and 316L for food, dairy, beverage, and pharmaceutical systems. Here is what we actually do on the sanitary line, and how to write a purchase order that matches the hygienic tier you need.

## What We Do Differently on the Sanitary Line

**The sanitary line is physically separate.** Hygienic tube is not run through the industrial or decorative release path and relabeled later. Different workshop, different hands, different documentation trail.

**The weld is nitrogen-shielded and laser-formed.** In a glass annealing chamber, the tube is heated under nitrogen to stabilise the structure, prevent oxidation, and refine the weld microstructure. A well-tuned anneal is the difference between a weld that polishes to a smooth bore and one that reads dark or rough on the interior.

**The bore is polished with the same seriousness as the outside.** Most tube suppliers polish only the exterior. On A270, we polish the bore up to 600 grit — smoother interior, less surface for residue to cling to, easier CIP clean-in-place. For tighter pharma work we can go further to electropolish.

**Hydrostatic test is line-by-line.** Each tube is sealed and pressurised underwater to 24 bar for at least one minute. Any bubble, even small, and the tube is pulled. We also run eddy current NDT for internal soundness.

**Final cleaning happens before packing.** After all the above, every tube is cleaned and visually inspected one more time. When hygiene is the point, there is no such thing as overdoing it.

## Which Hygienic Tier Are You Releasing?

| Hygienic standard | Typical service | Interior target | Release proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| General food | Transfer lines, tanks, cleanable utility lines | Ra ≤ 0.8 µm | Mill traceability + per-length hydro test |
| Dairy / beverage | Brew, dairy, filling, CIP-return lines | Ra ≤ 0.4 µm | Hydro record + Ra confirmation when specified |
| Pharma / bioprocess | BPE, WFI, injectable, higher-audit loops | Ra ≤ 0.25 µm or EP SF3–SF5 by request | Profilometer record, weld trace, hydro record |

304L covers most food and beverage work. 316L is the normal step-up where chloride-bearing cleaners, pharmaceutical cleaning cycles, or bioprocess exposure make the extra chloride margin worth it. ASME BPE still applies to the finished system — this page is about the material evidence underneath it.

## Why Sanitary Tube Costs More Than Pipe

If the tube were just a cleaner-looking pipe, it would not have its own standard. The cost sits in five places you will not find on an industrial pipe quote:

1. Separate workshop, separate tooling, separate hands
2. Nitrogen-shielded weld + bright anneal instead of a simple weld
3. Interior mechanical polish up to 600 grit
4. Per-length underwater hydro test, not sample-based
5. Eddy current and visual inspection, piece by piece

Paying for sanitary tube on a line that only needs industrial pressure capability is waste. Paying for industrial pipe on a line that carries milk, medicine, or beverage is a recall waiting to happen. The split is not subtle.

## What to Lock Before Ordering

**Ra tier.** State whether the line is food, dairy / beverage, or pharma-grade, and put the interior target on the PO.

**Size and standard.** Confirm OD, wall, ASTM or DIN requirement, and any electropolish request before quote release.

**Document pack.** If the job needs profilometer records, weld trace, or a specific MTC format, call it out before production and packing — this kind of paperwork cannot be retrofitted.

## FAQ

**Is 316L required for every sanitary line?** No. 304L is common in food and beverage. 316L is the usual upgrade for pharma or more aggressive cleaning chemistry.

**Can Jinling supply electropolished sanitary tube?** Yes. Electropolished work is quoted case by case against the requested SF or Ra target.

**Is Ra measured on every order?** For tighter pharma-grade requirements, yes. For general hygienic work, the measurement and record level should be stated at order stage.

**Can sanitary tube also be certified as industrial pipe?** Not as a normal stock item. [Industrial pipe](/products/stainless-steel-industrial-pipe) and sanitary tube release against different requirements.

## What the Sanitary Line Actually Does, End to End

Everything from the environment to the equipment stays clean. Clean workshops, sanitised machines, controlled processes from start to finish. After welding, the tube goes straight into a nitrogen-shielded annealing chamber. An inspector then examines both inner and outer welds. Then the tube goes underwater at 24 bar for at least a minute — if there is a bubble, it does not pass. Even after that, every tube is cleaned and visually inspected one more time before it is packed.
