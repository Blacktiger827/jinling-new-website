# Stainless Steel Bar by Profile, Tolerance Class, and Fabrication Logic

**Use the bar family when the cross-section is already part of the job.** The first decision is usually profile and tolerance class, not grade alone.

Bar is the solid-stock family for turning feed, machined blanks, welded frames, supports, and specialty-alloy shafts. The useful buying question is usually `which stock shape belongs to the work that follows`, then `which grade can carry that work`.

Most bar mistakes happen when buyers choose alloy first and profile later. Choose the stock shape, then narrow the route.

## Choose the Bar by Shape First

Bar orders get cleaner when the profile is treated as part of the design, not as a late stock choice.

**Round bar** belongs to turning, shaft work, pins, and cylindrical fits where diameter tolerance and straightness matter before the alloy debate.

**Flat and angle bar** belong to frames, brackets, supports, and welded structures where edge condition and fit-up decide the release.

**Square and hex bar** belong to keyed stock, blocks, fastener blanks, and valve parts. Here the profile is already doing work for the final part.

## Where Bar Orders Get Cleaner

**The decision starts with stock shape, not generic bar language.** Buyers can move directly into the sub-page that fits the machining or fabrication work.

**Tolerance language stays commercial.** The quote can be written around h8, h9, h11, straightness, chamfer, or corner radius instead of a long handbook explanation.

**Specialty grades still follow the same buying logic.** Standard stock covers 304 / 304L, 316 / 316L, 430, 201, 2205, and 303, while 17-4PH, 904L, Inconel, and other specialty grades are quoted against the same profile logic. Use the [grade library](/grades) when chemistry is still open.

## Order Notes That Keep the Stock Usable

**Profile first.** Round, flat, angle, square, and hex are different buying choices even when the grade is the same.

**Tolerance and stock condition.** Confirm whether the job needs hot-rolled, drawn, peeled, or ground stock, and name the tolerance class that actually belongs to the machine or fabrication plan.

**Grade and release file.** Lock the grade, any specialty-alloy requirement, cut length, chamfer, PMI, and MTC type before production.

## FAQ

**Can I order mixed bar profiles in one shipment?** Yes. Mixed-profile loading is standard when a project needs round, flat, and structural profiles together.

**Is 303 better than 304 for CNC parts?** For high-volume machining, yes. 303 breaks chips more easily, but it gives up weldability and some corrosion resistance versus 304.

**What tolerance class is typical for a precision shaft?** ISO h8 centerless-ground is the usual starting point. ISO h9 bright-drawn works for more general turning stock.

**Can Jinling quote specialty grades like 17-4PH or Inconel?** Yes. Specialty grades are available on request with Mill MTC, and PMI can be added where the project needs it.
