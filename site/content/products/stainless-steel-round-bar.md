# Stainless Steel Round Bar for Shaft Stock, CNC Feed, and Precision Rotating Parts

**Choose round bar when the stock is headed to turning, shafts, or cylindrical fit.** The real buying split is stock condition and tolerance first: hot-rolled, drawn, peeled, or ground.

Round bar behaves like machining stock, not generic long product. A few tenths on diameter or bow show up immediately at the machine, so the stock condition needs to be part of the quote.

Most round-bar problems are solved early by settling the process route before the alloy debate gets too detailed.

## Read the Holding Method Before the Grade

Round bar decisions depend on the next operation. A lathe, a bar feeder, and a rotating fit do not ask for the same release condition.

**Hot-rolled or peeled round** is the practical route for heavier shaft blanks when machining allowance and straightness are known.

**Bright-drawn round** belongs to CNC feed stock where h9 tolerance, chamfer, and bar-puller behavior matter.

**Centerless-ground round** is for tighter rotating fits. Here surface, bow, and h8 tolerance should be named rather than assumed.

## Which Round-Bar Condition Fits the Machine?

| Stock condition | Diameter window | Typical fit | Main release point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot-rolled / black | Ø 25–300 mm | Heavy shaft blanks, structural rounds, large machining allowance | Scale condition and machining allowance |
| Bright-drawn | Ø 6–80 mm | General CNC feed stock | h9 diameter control and stable straightness |
| Turned & peeled | Ø 20–200 mm | Rough-to-finish machining where surface skin must be removed | Cleaned surface and stock allowance |
| Centerless-ground | Ø 6–150 mm | Bar pullers, bearings, tight rotating fits | h8 tolerance, fine surface, lowest bow |

## Where Machining Stock Needs Control

**Straightness is checked before bundling.** The machine shop does not have to discover bowed stock after loading the first bar.

**Double-end chamfer is normal, not special.** That matters on bar-puller and collet-fed lines where burrs create stoppages.

**The alloy range fits real machining work.** 303 covers free-machining parts, 304 / 316L cover general corrosion service, and 2205 plus 17-4PH cover heavier-load shafts and components.

## What Tolerance Window Does Jinling Hold?

| Process | ISO tolerance | Diameter accuracy | Ra (µm) | Straightness |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Bright-drawn | h9 | ±0.05 mm (small dia.) | 0.8–1.6 | ≤ 0.5 mm/m |
| Turned & peeled | h11 | Per *ASTM A484* | 1.6–3.2 | ≤ 1.0 mm/m |
| Centerless-ground | h8 | Tightest | 0.4–0.8 | ≤ 0.3 mm/m |

303 is the normal free-machining route for high-volume turned parts. 304 / 304L and 316 / 316L cover most corrosion-facing components. 2205 and 17-4PH are the step-up route for higher strength or more demanding shaft work. Use the [grade library](/grades) when the alloy choice still needs proof.

## Order Notes That Protect the Machine Route

**Process condition.** Name hot-rolled, bright-drawn, peeled, or centerless-ground before the RFQ is final.

**Fit requirement.** Confirm diameter tolerance, straightness, length, and whether double-end chamfer is required for the feed system.

**Grade and release file.** Put the alloy, heat condition, PMI requirement, and MTC type on the order before production starts.

## FAQ

**What is the tightest tolerance available on round bar?** ISO h8 by centerless grinding, with the finest surface and the lowest bow in the range.

**Is 303 better than 304 for CNC turning?** For machining speed, yes. For weldability and corrosion resistance, no. The finished part matters as much as the machine.

**Can Jinling quote 17-4PH or Inconel round bar?** Yes. Specialty grades are available on request with Mill MTC, and PMI can be added where needed.

**What lengths are standard?** 3 m and 6 m are standard, and cut-to-length can be set for automated feed plans when the order calls for it.
