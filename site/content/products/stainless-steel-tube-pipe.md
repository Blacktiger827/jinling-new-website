# Stainless Steel Tube & Pipe by Standard, Service, and Release Logic

**Choose tube and pipe by standard first.** ASTM A312, ASTM A270, and ASTM A554 can look similar on paper, but they release against different proof, surface, and liability boundaries.

OD 6–426 mm, wall 0.5–30 mm, and grades from 201 through 2205 cover the visible range. What actually separates the route is whether the job needs pressure pipe, hygienic tube, or appearance-driven section.

If the standard is still vague, the order is still vague. Pick the route below and size the job after that.

## Name the Standard Before the Shape

Tube and pipe may look similar in an RFQ, but they are released against different proof, surface, and liability boundaries.

**Pressure or code-facing service** belongs in ASTM A312 / A790 pipe logic, where schedule, wall, hydro test, NDT, and MTC evidence matter.

**Product-contact hygiene** belongs in ASTM A270 sanitary tube logic, where bore Ra, weld condition, traceability, and per-length proof matter.

**Visible tube or handrail work** belongs in ASTM A554 decorative pipe logic, where finish protection and appearance release matter more than pressure language.

## Where Tube and Pipe Orders Get Cleaner

**The decision is standard-first, not shape-first.** Buyers can separate pressure, hygienic, and appearance requirements before the RFQ drifts into the wrong product family.

**The three streams stay separate in production.** Industrial, sanitary, and decorative tube do not share the same release logic or the same inspection burden.

**Mixed loading is easy after the standard is right.** Once the standard is fixed, categories can still ship together with sheet, coil, and bar families where required. Use the [product family](/products) page only when the order mix itself needs sorting.

## Order Notes Before the Standard Is Frozen

**Standard first.** Decide whether the job releases against *A312* / *A790*, *A270*, or *A554*. If that line is missing from the PO, the rest of the order is still weak.

**Size logic.** For industrial pipe, confirm NPS and schedule. For tube, confirm actual OD and wall. For decorative work, add finish and shape family.

**Proof package.** Lock the hydro, NDT, Ra, PMI, or finish-acceptance basis before production so the delivery file matches the receiving file.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between pipe and tube?** Pipe is normally ordered by NPS and schedule for pressure service. Tube is usually ordered by actual OD and wall where fit, bore, or finish matters more.

**What if my drawing calls for ASTM A269 or A213?** Those are valid tube standards, but they sit outside the three main commercial categories on this page. Share the drawing and Jinling can confirm whether the job belongs with general tubing or one of the pages above.

**Can a product qualify as both A312 and A270?** Not as a normal stock item. Pressure-service pipe and sanitary tube release against different proof and surface requirements.

**Can the three categories ship in one container?** Yes. Once the standards and paperwork are separated correctly, mixed-container loading is standard practice.
