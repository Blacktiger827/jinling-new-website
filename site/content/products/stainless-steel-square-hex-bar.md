# Stainless Steel Square and Hex Bar for Fastener Blanks, Frames, and Precision Stock

**Choose square and hex bar when the finished part already needs that profile.** The real buying split is true-flat frame stock versus fastener/profile stock, with tolerance and corner condition fixed early.

Square bar fits welded frames, keyed stock, and machined blocks. Hex bar fits fastener blanks, valve fittings, and parts that need finished wrench flats from the start.

These profiles look simple on paper, but the useful release points are side or AF dimension, corner condition, and stock route.

## Let the Profile Do the First Sorting

Square and hex bar are chosen because the finished part already needs that geometry. The profile is not decoration; it saves machining, sets contact faces, or gives the part its wrench flats.

**Square bar** fits frames, keyed stock, blocks, and components where side size, straightness, face condition, and corner radius matter.

**Hex bar** fits fastener blanks, valve fittings, and parts that need wrench flats from the start. AF dimension and machinability should be confirmed early.

**Specialty grades** such as 303 or 17-4 PH belong only after the profile requirement is clear.

## Which Profile Fits the Part?

| Profile | Typical job | What buyers watch | Grade bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| Square bar | Frames, keyed stock, machined blocks | Side tolerance, straightness, face condition | 304L / 316L / 17-4PH |
| Hex bar | Fastener blanks, valve stems, torque-driven parts | AF dimension, corner radius, machinability | 303 / 304L / 316L |

## Where Geometry Carries the Work

**Profile geometry is controlled for the finished part.** Buyers can quote against side size or AF size directly instead of reworking round stock.

**303 and 17-4PH are normal asks, not exceptions.** That matters for fastener makers, precision parts, and higher-strength profile stock.

**Export QC stays readable.** Dimensional checks, profile identification, and heat traceability are easy to separate at receiving.

Square bar is the better route for welded frames and machined blocks. Hex bar is the better route when the part needs wrench flats or fastener geometry from the start. Hot-rolled square is available for structural use; hex is mainly a bright-drawn product.

## Order Notes for Square and Hex Stock

**Profile and size.** Put square side or hex AF on the order. "Profile bar" is too loose for release.

**Tolerance and corner condition.** If the job needs h9 or a strict corner-radius limit, write that into the RFQ.

**Grade and release file.** Confirm the alloy, condition, MTC type, and any PMI requirement before the order is released.

## FAQ

**Can I order square and hex bar in the same shipment?** Yes. Mixed-profile loads are normal, with packing separated by profile and heat traceability.

**What is the minimum order quantity for hex bar?** Trial quantities are possible on common sizes, but the current bundle minimum depends on AF size and grade.

**Does 17-4PH come in annealed or aged condition?** Condition A is the normal starting point. Aged conditions can be quoted when the project needs them.

**Is hot-rolled square bar available?** Yes. Hot-rolled square is available for structural grades where surface finish is less important than cost and section size.
