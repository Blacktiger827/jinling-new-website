# Stainless Steel Flat Bar for Frames, Brackets, and Rectangular Machining Stock

**Choose flat bar when the job needs rectangular stock with a controlled edge.** The useful split is fabrication stock versus closer-tolerance rectangular stock, rather than flat bar as a generic name.

Flat bar is usually selected because sheet is too panel-like and round bar wastes material on rectangular parts. Edge condition and straightness decide whether it lands ready to weld, mill, or fixture.

Most flat-bar orders become easy once the buyer fixes the stock condition before the grade discussion gets too long.

## Separate Fabrication Stock From Machining Stock

Flat bar should not be treated as sheet strip with a nicer name. The first split is whether the bar will be welded into a frame or machined into a closer-fit part.

**Hot-rolled flat bar** fits brackets, frames, and supports where width, thickness, straightness, and edge prep carry the order.

**Cold-drawn flat bar** fits rectangular machined parts where tolerance and surface condition matter more than lowest stock cost.

**Ground or finished flat stock** belongs to visible hardware and close-fit blanks. Chamfer, finish, and inspection basis should be written before release.

## Which Flat-Bar Condition Fits the Job?

| Stock condition | Best fit | Edge release | Watch point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot-rolled fabrication stock | Welded frames, supports, base plates | Broken or saw-cut edge | Lower-cost choice, wider tolerance |
| Cold-drawn rectangular stock | Brackets, jigs, rectangular machined parts | Cleaner edge and tighter size control | Straightness and finish consistency |
| Ground / finished flat stock | Exposed hardware, close-tolerance blanks | Ground or chamfered edge | Premium choice, confirm prep exactly |

## Where Edge and Fit-Up Control Matter

**Deburred or chamfered edges are easy to specify.** Buyers do not have to leave edge prep as a shop-floor surprise.

**Straightness is controlled bundle by bundle.** That matters on welded frames, bracket runs, and fixture parts where rework adds up quickly.

**Fabrication and machining stock can ship together.** One order can carry simple welded-stock sizes and tighter rectangular blanks without changing supplier logic.

304 / 304L is the normal fabrication grade. 316 / 316L is the step-up for chloride exposure, wash-down, and coastal work. 2205 fits higher-load structural duty, while 430 stays in dry indoor cost-sensitive work. Use the [grade library](/grades) when the stock shape is clear but the corrosion margin is not.

## Order Notes That Keep the Flat Bar Useful

**Cross-section.** Put thickness x width on the order. The words "flat bar" are not enough for release.

**Stock condition and edge.** Confirm hot-rolled, cold-drawn, or ground stock together with the edge condition the shop actually wants.

**Grade and release file.** Lock the alloy, any cut-to-length request, and the MTC / PMI requirement before production.

## FAQ

**Can I get flat bar narrower than 10 mm?** Below 10 mm width, the job usually moves to slit strip from coil rather than standard flat bar.

**Can flat bar be supplied in custom lengths?** Yes. Cut-to-length is available when the order states the required lengths and quantity.

**Does 2205 flat bar carry the same lead time as 304?** For normal sizes, it is often close. Larger cross-sections or low-volume runs are quoted individually.

**Is 430 flat bar suitable for outdoor use?** Not for marine or high-chloride service. Use 316L when outdoor corrosion resistance matters.
