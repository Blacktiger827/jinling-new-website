# 201 Stainless Steel

201 is a cost choice, not a universal substitute for 304. It makes sense when the part is indoors, dry, decorative, and not expected to carry chloride abuse.

## Quick Decision

Keep 201 in the discussion when the part is dry, indoor, decorative, and cost-sensitive. It can fit interior trim, budget decorative tube, PVD base work, and sheet panels that are seen more than they are washed.

Move out of 201 when the service includes frequent wet cleaning, outdoor exposure, coastal air, food contact, bleach, salt, or any real chloride load. In those cases, the safer route is usually 304 / 304L, or 316 / 316L when chloride exposure is already part of the brief.

Jinling check: keep 201 as a controlled indoor cost choice. We usually confirm dry service, finish expectation, and receiving inspection language before treating it as a substitute for 304.

## Where 201 Wins

- Interior panels and trim where the face is seen, not washed hard.
- Decorative tube, display systems, and light formed parts.
- PVD base work in controlled indoor spaces.

## Where It Stops

201 loses margin fast when the surface sees salt, bleach, outdoor moisture, or repeat wet cleaning. It is also the wrong default for welded corrosive service.

If the project already sounds like 304, it is usually 304.

For buyer-side comparison, read [201 vs 304 stainless steel](/knowledge-base/201-vs-304-stainless-steel). If the question is whether a low quote is really 304, use [how to identify real 304](/knowledge-base/how-to-identify-real-304-stainless-steel).

## What to Lock on the PO

1. State `ASTM A240 Type 201 / UNS S20100` if real 201 is required.
2. Name the standard if EN or JIS equivalence matters.
3. Confirm the finish and whether the part is decorative or structural.
4. Confirm that the service stays dry and indoor.

## Support Data

Useful MTC cues for 201:

- Chromium usually sits around 16-18%.
- Nickel is much lower than 304, commonly around 3.5-5.5%.
- Manganese is part of the cost structure, commonly around 5.5-7.5%.
- Nitrogen may be present up to about 0.25%.
- Typical minimum tensile strength is around 655 MPa, with yield around 260 MPa.

## Finishes and Forms

201 is commonly supplied as 2B, BA, No.4, Hairline, and AFP over decorative base finishes. Coil, sheet, slit strip, and decorative tube feedstock are the normal supply forms.

## Common order questions

**Can 201 replace 304 in a kitchen or outdoor job?** No. The service boundary is too tight.

**Why choose 201 at all?** It gives a workable decorative stainless look at lower alloy cost when the environment is controlled.

**What is the fastest selection test?** If the order is dry indoor decorative and cost-sensitive, 201 can stay in the discussion. If not, move up.
