# 304 / 304L Stainless Steel

304 / 304L is the everyday stainless baseline when the service is mild, the supply chain must be broad, and the part still needs to form and weld cleanly.

If chloride, coastal air, or hot saline cleaning is already part of the brief, this is no longer the finish line. It becomes the starting point for an upgrade.

## Quick Decision

Keep 304 / 304L as the baseline for mild indoor fabrication, broad supply programs, food and appliance work in ordinary service, and formed parts that need predictable welding and finishing.

Write 304L when the order is welded and corrosion still matters. Move to 316 / 316L when saline cleaners, coastal exterior exposure, or stronger chloride language enters the brief. Warm seawater and severe chloride exposure are no longer 304 decisions; they need duplex or higher review.

Jinling check: treat 304 / 304L as the baseline only after the environment is named. We lock product form, finish, film, and MTC wording together so the order does not become a chemistry-only decision.

## Where It Wins

- General fabrication with broad global availability.
- Food, appliance, and interior work in mild environments.
- Formed parts that need balanced welding and finish behavior.

## Where It Stops

304 / 304L is not a chloride grade. It is strong enough for everyday work, but not the right answer when the environment starts doing the damage.

When the order is welded and corrosion matters, use 304L unless the drawing explicitly wants 304.

For the most common upgrade decision, read [304 vs 316 stainless steel](/knowledge-base/304-vs-316-stainless-steel). For coastal projects, use [is 304 enough for coastal use](/knowledge-base/is-304-enough-for-coastal-use) before releasing the PO.

## What to Lock on the PO

1. Name the grade and the standard together.
2. State whether the order is 304 or 304L, or dual-certified stock.
3. Confirm finish, edge condition, and film if the part will be cut or handled visibly.
4. Confirm whether chloride is absent or only occasional.

## Support Data

Useful MTC cues for 304 / 304L:

- Carbon is the first split: 304 allows up to 0.08%, while 304L is capped at 0.030%.
- Chromium is usually 18.0-20.0% for both.
- Nickel usually sits around 8.0-11.0% for 304 and 8.0-12.0% for 304L.
- Typical minimum tensile strength is around 515 MPa for 304 and 485 MPa for 304L.
- Typical minimum yield is around 205 MPa for 304 and 170 MPa for 304L.

## Finishes and Forms

Common finishes are 2B, BA, No.4, Hairline, mirror, and AFP. Coil, sheet, strip, bar, and tube routes are standard supply forms.

## Common order questions

**Is 304 enough for coastal work?** Usually no.

**Why does 304L matter?** Lower carbon keeps welded zones safer from sensitization.

**What is the first upgrade step?** Move to 316 / 316L when chloride becomes real.
