# 2205 Duplex Stainless Steel

2205 is the step-up grade when 316L no longer feels safe enough for chloride, stress, or weight-driven design.

The decision includes corrosion resistance, but it also asks whether higher yield can reduce wall thickness enough to offset the alloy cost.

## Quick Decision

Keep 2205 in the discussion when chloride margin, stress, pressure, or weight reduction are part of the same problem. It fits coastal splash-zone structures, desalination, brine, water treatment, and process work where 316L no longer feels safe enough.

Treat 2205 as conditional for warm seawater immersion, severe brine, or service where qualification language gets specific. Hot service above about 300 C usually belongs in another alloy conversation.

Jinling check: use 2205 when chloride margin, strength, or pressure weight reduction justifies duplex. We confirm heat chemistry, heat treatment, weld controls, and document language before release.

## Where It Wins

- Process lines and structures where 316L has too little margin.
- Weight-sensitive pressure parts where higher yield matters.
- Chloride-bearing service where stress-corrosion cracking is part of the risk picture.

## Where It Stops

2205 is not a universal seawater or high-temperature alloy. It has a narrower fabrication window than austenitic grades, and the weld procedure has to be controlled.

If the job is hot service rather than chloride service, duplex is usually the wrong path.

For the family-level decision, read [duplex vs austenitic stainless steel](/knowledge-base/duplex-vs-austenitic-stainless-steel). For the chemistry behind the grade, use [2205 duplex chemical composition](/knowledge-base/2205-duplex-chemical-composition).

## What to Lock on the PO

1. Name `2205` and the preferred UNS designation, usually `S32205`.
2. Confirm whether the job is chloride, pressure, or sour service.
3. Confirm ferrite range, hardness, and inspection needs.
4. Confirm whether third-party inspection is required.

## Support Data

Useful MTC cues for 2205:

- Chromium is typically around 22.0-23.0%.
- Nickel usually sits around 4.5-6.5%.
- Molybdenum is commonly around 3.0-3.5%.
- Nitrogen usually sits around 0.14-0.20%.
- Typical minimum tensile strength is around 620 MPa, with yield around 450 MPa.
- PREN is usually about 35, but the heat chemistry should confirm the exact value.

## Finishes and Forms

Common finishes are 2B, No.4, Hairline, and pickled/passivated. Coil, sheet, plate, bar, welded pipe, and seamless pipe are standard forms.

## Common order questions

**Why choose 2205 over 316L?** Chloride margin and higher yield.

**Is 2205 magnetic?** Yes, because of the ferrite phase.

**When should you move beyond 2205?** When the environment becomes hot seawater, severe brine, or higher-temperature chloride service.
