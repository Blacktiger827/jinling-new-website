# 316 / 316L Stainless Steel

316 / 316L is the pair you move to when 304 is too light on chloride margin but duplex would still be too much.

The decision is usually direct: use 316L when the part is welded and the service still needs chloride resistance. Use 316 only when the drawing or stock condition specifically wants it.

## Quick Decision

Keep 316 / 316L in the discussion when the job has coastal exterior exposure, salt-laden air, welded hygienic equipment, mild chemical service, or cleaning chemistry that makes 304 feel too light.

Write 316L when the part is welded and chloride resistance still matters. Treat warm seawater, severe crevices, high chloride plus stress, and hot concentrated acid service as review points, not automatic 316L approvals. Those cases may need 2205 duplex or a higher alloy route.

Jinling check: use 316 / 316L as the chloride upgrade, then check whether welding, cleaning chemistry, or coastal exposure makes 316L the safer wording on the PO.

## Where It Wins

- Welded tanks, housings, pipework, and sanitary equipment.
- Coastal architectural work and salt-bearing atmosphere.
- Food and chemical service where 304 is not enough.

## Where It Stops

316L is not a universal seawater answer. When chloride, temperature, and stress all climb together, duplex becomes the honest next discussion.

If the order is welded and the environment is chloride-bearing, 316L should usually be the default.

For the 304-to-316 decision, read [304 vs 316 stainless steel](/knowledge-base/304-vs-316-stainless-steel). If the project is coastal and visible, use [316L mirror for coastal lobby elevators](/knowledge-base/316l-mirror-for-coastal-lobby-elevator) for the finish-side risk.

## What to Lock on the PO

1. Name `316` or `316L` explicitly.
2. Confirm the standard and certificate type.
3. Confirm whether the job is welded, sanitary, or coastal.
4. Confirm whether the project needs PREN verification on the heat.

## Support Data

Useful MTC cues for 316 / 316L:

- Carbon is the main suffix split: 316 allows up to 0.08%, while 316L is capped at 0.030%.
- Chromium usually sits around 16.0-18.0%.
- Nickel usually sits around 10.0-14.0%.
- Molybdenum should be around 2.00-3.00%; this is the chloride-margin reason 316 exists.
- Typical minimum tensile strength is around 515 MPa for 316 and 485 MPa for 316L.
- Typical minimum yield is around 205 MPa for 316 and 170 MPa for 316L.
- PREN is usually about 25 for 316 and about 24-25 for 316L, depending on heat chemistry.

## Finishes and Forms

Common finishes are 2B, BA, No.4, Hairline, mirror, and AFP. Coil, sheet, bar, welded tube, and seamless pipe are normal supply forms.

## Common order questions

**Why choose 316L over 304L?** Chloride margin.

**Why choose 316L over 316?** Welded service.

**What is the next step up when 316L is not enough?** 2205 duplex.
