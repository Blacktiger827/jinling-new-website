# 430 Stainless Steel

430 is the right answer when the job wants magnetic response, lower nickel exposure, and a finish-led indoor appearance.

It is not a cheaper 304. It is a different family with a tighter service boundary.

## Quick Decision

Keep 430 in the discussion for dry indoor appliances, trim, magnetic interior parts, and selected indoor mirror or decorative work where nickel-free cost control helps.

Treat frequent wet cleaning or humid service as a warning. Move out of 430 for outdoor air, coastal exposure, chloride service, acid service, or anything that expects 304-level corrosion reserve. The safer route is usually 304 / 304L, with 316 / 316L for chloride-bearing service.

Jinling check: keep 430 in dry indoor and appliance-facing work where nickel-free cost control matters. If moisture, chlorides, or exterior exposure enter the brief, step back to 304 or 316L.

## Where It Wins

- Appliance fronts, elevator interiors, and indoor decorative trim.
- Magnetic features where ferritic response is useful.
- Cost control on controlled indoor work.

## Where It Stops

430 loses margin quickly once the project is wet, chloride-bearing, or expected to hold its appearance outdoors.

If the order is indoor and dry, 430 can be very good value. If the order needs corrosion reserve, it is the wrong place to save money.

For a practical mirror-panel split, read [304 vs 430 mirror panels](/knowledge-base/304-vs-430-mirror-panels). For kitchen equipment, use [304 vs 430 kitchen equipment](/knowledge-base/304-vs-430-kitchen-equipment) before choosing by price alone.

## What to Lock on the PO

1. Confirm `ASTM A240 430` or the required equivalent.
2. Confirm the surface is for indoor use.
3. Confirm whether magnetism is part of the brief.
4. Confirm whether the finish is BA, No.4, hairline, mirror, or AFP.

## Support Data

Useful MTC cues for 430:

- Chromium usually sits around 16-18%.
- Nickel should be low, typically no more than 0.75%.
- Typical minimum tensile strength is around 450 MPa.
- Typical minimum yield is around 205 MPa.
- Elongation minimum is commonly around 22%.
- PREN is about 17, so the corrosion boundary should stay modest and indoor.

## Finishes and Forms

430 is commonly supplied as 2B, BA, No.4, Hairline, mirror, and AFP on indoor programs. Coil, sheet, strip, and foil are the usual forms.

## Common order questions

**Is 430 magnetic?** Yes, in all normal conditions.

**Can 430 work outdoors?** Not as a default choice.

**Why choose 430 at all?** Because controlled indoor work often needs the look and formability more than the corrosion reserve.
